# Source: "Preparing for the Great Race, A Guide for Rookies and Returning Teams" (Bill Croker, rev. Sept 2015)

Authoritative. Supplied by Josh 2026-10-03 as rookiehandbook.pdf (49 PDF pages; printed page = PDF page - 2).
This file covers the main body (printed 1-15) and Appendix D (printed 25-30). Appendices B, C, E, F are in
08b-rookie-handbook-appendices.md. The 2026 Event Regulations are in 09-event-regulations-2026.md.
Where this file and the regulations disagree, the regulations win (the handbook says so itself, p.1).

## 1. What the event is (p.1)
- "A timed, navigation, controlled speed, endurance race" on public roads, all speeds at or below the limits.
- Objective: match the precise time allowed for each section. Course Instructions issued each morning.
- "It is not difficult. However, excellent scores require skill on the part of both the driver and navigator."

## 2. Rally equipment (p.3-6)
- Speedometer: the official unit is the Timewise 825 electronic speedometer (~$1,000). Stock speedometer is
  allowed; any other type needs Great Race approval. Daily opportunity to calibrate on a specific course.
- Why it matters (p.3): "if your stock speedometer is only off by 1 per cent, your error will be 36 seconds
  per hour. Most teams, including rookies, will have scores of less than 10 seconds per hour. If you can't tell
  the difference between 49 and 50 mph on your stock speedometer, your error will be 2 per cent which amounts to
  72 seconds per hour."
- Analog clock (p.5): mount on the dash or lapboard where both can read it. Many use a Sawtooth Rally Clock:
  continuous motion, minimal backlash, every second numbered.
- Stopwatch (p.5): "A good digital stopwatch is a necessity for accurate rallying. Be sure to get one that has a
  lap-split function and a time-of-day function." Worn on a lanyard or fixed to the lapboard. Wristwatches allowed.
- Lapboard (p.5-6): flat or roller style; holds instructions (three rings, bring a hole punch), pens, calibration
  charts under clear vinyl, daily starting order. Backup batteries, extra pens, transparent coloured markers.

charts may be used as-is by rookies who have no time ("better than nothing").

## 4. How to make up lost time (p.10)
- Ten per cent rule: drive 10 % above the instructed speed for 10 x the seconds lost (38.5 mph for 40 s to make
  up 4 s at 35; 44 mph for 44 s to make up 4.4 s at 40). Same for losing time: 10 % below for 10 x the seconds.
- Lost-time formula for driving slower than assigned: (Assigned - Actual) / Assigned x seconds at the reduced
  speed = seconds lost. 40 assigned, 30 actual, 20 s = 5 s lost.

## 5. Course Instructions (p.11-12)
- Issued "usually 30 minutes before your assigned start time each morning"; notes may be made in that time.
- Speedometer calibration: after the calibration run compare your stopwatch with the printed correct cumulative
  time (example: actual 28m47.3s vs correct 28m43.2s = 4.1 s late) and adjust the speedometer (Appendix C).
- Transit zones (hourglass symbol, full = begin, empty = end): untimed; the printed time is a guide so you arrive
  at the next time-of-day restart on time; lunch, pit, fuel and rest stops happen inside transits.
- Transit between checkpoints after a time-of-day restart "is critical ... must be executed exactly": record the
  exact time of day you pass the IN sign, add the printed transit time (example 30 min), write it as your OUT time
  at the end-of-transit instruction, arrive a few minutes early, pull up to the sign close to the OUT time and
  depart exactly then at the assigned speed. Cars are no longer exactly one minute apart after such a transit.
- A transit at the end of the timed section is a guide back to the Observation Checkpoint.
- Stop signs: pull up, pause for the chart time, depart at the assigned speed; nothing to make up unless traffic.
- Turns: slow to the chart's apex speed, turn, accelerate to the indicated speed, make up the chart loss with the
  10 % rule as soon as practical.
- Speed changes: "split the speed change at the sign": cross the sign at the midpoint speed (35 to 30: cross at
  32.5 and keep slowing). The gain and loss cancel. (This is the handbook's form of "lead by half the ramp".)

## 6. The Four S's (p.13-14)
1. Safety first. Signal intentions; do not pass blind; reckless driving, running stop signs or signals, or a ticket
   = penalty or disqualification for the day. If you lose a lot of time, submit a time allowance instead of
   chasing it. "Time allowances are acceptable for unsafe situations, or situations that are beyond your control,
   but are not allowed for items such as mechanical breakdowns, flat tires, navigation errors, wrong turns, etc."
   "Do not continue to make up time after passing a checkpoint. You are automatically on time the instant you
   reach a checkpoint. At a checkpoint the previous leg is complete with whatever errors were incurred and the
   next leg begins with no penalty."
2. Start on time. Nobody tells you when to start. After a time-of-day restart it is assumed you left on time.
   Two parts: clock calibrated to the officials' WWV radio clock at instruction pick-up, and actually leaving at
   the indicated time. Leaving on the wrong minute "will take a lot of make up to get back on time, assuming you
   even recognize that you left on the wrong time."
3. Stay on course. "Much more important than trying to maintain perfect times." A wrong or missed turn usually
   costs several minutes. Always work the current instruction and the next one, especially with "comes quick" in
   column D. The navigator always tells the driver the next sign or intersection to look for, because the
   navigator's head will be down. Complete all of one instruction before moving to the next (App. D #14).
4. Stay on time. Execute each manoeuvre consistently per the charts and make up losses before the checkpoint.
   "The errors associated with stops and turns are usually in seconds and not minutes. So for rookies,
   concentrate on the first 3 S's where the errors are usually in minutes."

## 7. Driver and navigator tips (p.15)
1. Consistent vocabulary: the same words every time for a road sign ("soft right curve", "soft offset right curve").
2. Team errors only; after an error both must make the correction; work together on hard signs/street names.
3. In timed sections, only communication needed to follow the instructions; scenery and post-mortems wait.
4. The driver repeats the instruction he hears (after several lefts it is easy to hear "left" for "right").
5. Cross off each instruction when completed (identical consecutive instructions; large transparent marker).
6. At restart points do not pull up to the start point until it is your minute.
7. Make up losses as soon as it can be done safely; you do not know where the next checkpoint is.
8. Pre-read notations: transfer the speed from the bottom of one page to the top of the next; mark all speeds not
   shown on the instructions (App. E #40); highlight every "comes quick" and other column D items, and if one is at
   the top of a page, note it on the previous page.

## 8. Appendix D: the real instruction format (printed 25-30, "Example Rally", (c) 2014 Great Race)
Four columns per numbered row, each row a box ~1 inch high, 6-7 rows per page, footer "Hemmings Motor News Great
Race, Page n of 6".
- Column A: the CAMEO: a dot at the bottom (you), a bold arrow for the route, thin lines for other roads, sign
  faces drawn as boxes with the sign text ("Speed Limit 65", "Leaving Chattanooga City Limit"), landmarks as small
  pictures (courthouse, church, playhouse) with their names, intersection control drawn at the junction (STOP
  octagon, traffic light, blinker star, yield triangle), railroad as a hatched bar, bridge as parallel lines.
- Column B: symbols: hourglass full = begin transit, hourglass empty = end transit, with a 4-digit odometer box in
  tenths of a mile (0 0 4 5 = 4.5 mi, 0 2 4 0 = 24.0 mi); speedometer face = begin speedometer calibration run;
  tyre (COKER) = tire warm-up; crossed-out camera = begin free zone, camera = end free zone; crossed-out clock =
  end timed portion; cup = hosted pit stop; knife and fork = meal stop; fuel pump = refuelling; restroom figures =
  rest stop; checkered flag = finish line.
- Column C: speeds and times, stacked one per line: "40 MPH"; pause = "0 MPH / 0m15s / 45 MPH"; timed segments =
  "30 MPH / 0m36s / 45 MPH / 1m12s / 50 MPH"; calibration point = boxed "interval / cumulative" ("5m32.0s" over
  "7m21.3s"); transit guide times in parentheses "(0m30s)", "(3h25m00s)"; time-of-day restart = clock face
  "EDT 8:55:00" over "30 MPH"; calibration run start = "26m00s / 50 MPH / * 0m00.0s" (start the stopwatch).
- Column D: the sentence: "Turn right onto Buchanan Blvd at a crossroad at a Traffic Light." "Pass a sign on your
  right reading in whole or in part 'Leaving Chattanooga City Limit'." "Go straight to cross Roosevelt Rd at a
  crossroad at a Stop Sign." "Grade level Railroad Crossing. Continue previous average speed (in this case 45 miles
  per hour) since no speed is given." "Change average speed to 45 miles per hour at the apex of the intersection
  (since there is no referenced sign)." Plus notes: "comes quick", toll amounts, "turn into middle lane".
- Where speed changes apply: at the referenced sign; at the apex of the intersection when there is no sign.
- Stage skeleton in the example: #1 start "Leave here at 8:00:00 plus your assigned start position in minutes",
  tire warm-up (8 mi, 20 min, transit) -> #5 speedometer calibration run (21 mi at 50 mph, 26 min, cumulative
  times printed at #6, #7, #9, #10; "official time (for scoring purposes) is 26 minutes") -> transit 4.5 mi / 9 min
  -> #12 time-of-day restart "8:55:00 plus your assigned start position", 30 mph -> timed instructions (#13-#16:
  speed change, timed segment, blinker pause, RR crossing) -> #17 end timed portion, transit 75 mi / 3h25m with pit
  (#18 leave 2h55m prior to end of transit), refuel (#19, 2h10m prior), lunch (#21, 45 min prior), rest stop
  (#22, 3 min prior) -> #23 time-of-day restart 2:55:00 + ASP, 30 mph -> timed (#24 timed segment, #25 free zone
  begins at a turn under a bridge, #28 end free zone at a sign, #30 transit 12 mi "exactly 20 minutes", #32 end
  transit "leave 20 minutes after instruction #30", #33 STOP turn with pause) -> #34 end timed portion, transit 20
  mi / 30 min -> #35 finish line, "Stop at Observation Checkpoint; submit any Time Allowance Requests."
- Time-of-day restart rule: printed base time plus the car's Assigned Starting Position (ASP) in minutes; the ASP
  changes daily and is not the car number (Appendix E intro: Trophy Run began at noon, ASP 42, start 12:42,
  instructions at 12:12).
- Transit mileages are approximate ("odometers are covered in the race cars, the 10 miles is indicated as a guide").

## 9. What this changes for the simulator (summary; detailed deltas in docs/spec/SPECS.md v2 section)
- Book layout and wording: adopt the four-column format and column C/D phrasing above (UI-005, GEN text).
- Stage skeleton: tire warm-up transit, calibration run with printed cumulative times at calibration points and an
  "official" transit allowance, transit with hourglass + odometer box, time-of-day restart = base + ASP minutes,
  free zones, end-timed-portion, pit/fuel/lunch/rest inside transits with "leave X prior to end-of-transit", a
  timed in-leg transit (IN time + exact minutes = OUT time), finish at an Observation Checkpoint.
- Time allowances are submitted at the end-of-stage Observation Checkpoint, not at the next checkpoint (confirm
  against the regulations); not allowed for navigation errors or breakdowns.
- Performance charts: three handbook-layout matrices (accel/decel net loss, stop & go pause times, turn loss) with
  the Packard example as a selectable known car; D06 builds these three charts.
- Teaching: speed change = split at the sign; 10 % rule; lost-time formula; Four S's priority (course before time);
  the eight team tips as the "team protocol" lesson; pre-read notations (speed carried to page top, speeds not
  shown, comes quick) as D15 items.
- Score reality: most teams including rookies under 10 s per hour; 1 % speedo = 36 s/hour.
- Instruments: handbook recommends a digital stopwatch with lap/split and time of day; analog dash clock.
