# 11a. Frame review: the 2024 and 2026 Great Race training sessions (123 frames, 41 cues)

Scope: every frame in `docs/research/frames/` starting `2024-` (19 cues) or `2026-` (22 cues), three frames per cue
(`-3`, `+0`, `+4` seconds). Each `+0` frame was read first; `-3`/`+4` were read where `+0` did not show the expected
thing. Hard-to-read areas were cropped and enlarged (the frames are only 1280 x 720, so small digits are marked `(?)`).
Precedence (Josh's rule): organizers' material (2026 Regulations, Rookie Handbook, the organizers' slides and sample
sheets shown here) overrides everything, including the earlier transcript-based synthesis in 10 and 10a.

## 0. Headline findings

1. **Both schools show the same organizer sample sheets.** The "real page" in both videos is the 2014 Hagerty Trophy Run
   sheet set ("Page n of 13", "(c) 2014, Great Race"), the same pages already transcribed in 08b section 3. There is no 2026
   five-column sample with "page 1 of 26" in any frame; the spoken "of 26" is not on screen (the 2026 frame shows "Page 1 of 13").
   Other real pages that appear: one 2019 sheet (Stage 7, Page 6 of 22, shown clean and hand-marked) and one photographed
   2025 sheet on a lapboard (2025 Cutler Tire Great Race Spring Rally, Stage 1, Page 19 of 2?).
2. **The "five columns" are a number gutter plus A, B, C, D.** The gutter has no header letter; the column-letter row
   reads only A B C D. This reconciles REG VII.B.3.c(5) "fifth column (labeled D)" with the four lettered columns.
3. **The Navigating 101 chart differs between the schools.** 2024: rows 50..10, columns Speed | Dec | Acc | S/G | T@15 | T@20
   (six columns, no 55 row). 2026: rows 55..10, columns Speed | Dec | Acc | S/G | T@15 (five columns, new 55 row, different
   numbers). Exact copies in section 2.1 and 2.2. All S/G values equal Dec + Acc (checked for every row of both charts).
4. **Real sheet details the simulator does not draw** (section 3/4): the restart/start "clock" is a digital wristwatch
   drawing (not an analog dial) with the time zone above and the time inside its display; the "end timed portion" symbol is a
   circle-slash around that watch; free-zone icons are a camcorder on a tripod; calibration boxes are thin rectangles with the
   interval left-aligned over the cumulative right-aligned; the calibration start row prints the speed before the interval;
   column proportions are A:B:C:D about 30:20:23.5:26.5; rows vary in height and pages carry 5 to 10 rows; the footer is three
   blocks; the TA row is a rounded black-outlined box spanning A to D; end-of-day "Information Box" rows exist.
5. **Cue/timestamp mismatch.** The slide on screen at a cue time is often not the one the transcript analysis expected
   (a presenter lingers on a slide while talking about a physical object, e.g. the Timewise unit held to the camera, which is
   never on screen). Details per cue in section 1.

### 2.4 Text slides (verbatim)

#### 2.4.1 "Speedometer Calibration" (2024 slide 15; 2026 `25m27s`)
- **Follow installation instructions from TimeWise**: Attach two small magnets to the left front wheel with JB Weld (*also recommend putting magnets on your spare*), fabricate a bracket to hold the electronic pickup, mount the speedometer for viewing by the driver (many teams also insist the navigator can see it), and connect to a power source.
- **Initial calibration**: With tires fully inflated, measure 5 rotations of the wheel on the ground. Total inches to nearest tenth (disregard decimal) is your factor to enter on the back of your Timewise using a small screwdriver to adjust the dials. ***Mark top and bottom of the back side!***
- *Note:* Appendix C of Rookie Handbook provides more details on how to do the initial calibration and the following two methods for making adjustments.

#### 2.4.2 "Creating a Performance Chart", course (2024 slide 17 / 2026 `32m09s+4`, `35m30s`)
2024: Find a straight level section of road about a half mile long with little or no traffic. You will also need a way to turn around safely near each end of the course. / You will be traversing this course at different speeds ranging from **15 to 50 mph** and making frequent stops. / A course that takes about **40 seconds to traverse at 50 mph** is about right. Mark each end of the course with a visible marker. An orange traffic cone or a stick with a flag will work. You may want to use a shorter course for the lower speed runs to save time. / Run the course entering at speeds of **15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45 and 50 mph**. Make at least 4 runs at each speed and record the times. Run the course in both directions. You need at least 4 runs to get a good average time. If you have a lot of variation, make more runs. / Once you have a good average time on this course at each speed, **this becomes your base**. Now, you need to determine your time lost when you accelerate from a stop to each speed, come to a stop from each speed, and make a turn from each speed.
2026 differs in three places: "ranging from **10 to 55 mph**", "about **40 seconds to traverse at 55 mph**", "entering at speeds of **10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50 and 55 mph**".

#### 2.4.3 "Creating a Performance Chart", the four losses (2024 slide 18)
- **Acceleration:** Start from a stop at the beginning of your course and accelerate to speeds of 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45 and 50 mph. Make at least 4 runs at each speed and record the times when you reach the end of the course. Determine the average of all runs at each speed and subtract it from your base time at each speed.
- **Stop-and-Go:** Enter the course at each speed, then when your navigator tells you to stop, do an immediate stop-and-go to resume your speed. If your course is long enough, do two or three stop-and-gos. Make at least 4 runs at each speed and record the times. Divide your time loss by the number of stop-and-gos. Subtract it from your base time to determine your stop-and-go loss at each speed.
- **Deceleration:** Now that you have your stop-and-go and acceleration numbers, simply subtract your time loss for acceleration from your time loss for a stop-and-go and you'll have your deceleration loss at each speed.
- **Turns:** This is just to give you a ballpark number to work with. We recommend that you slow down to 15 mph at *every* turn for consistency. Enter the course at each speed, then when your navigator tells you, brake quickly to 15 and then resume your speed. If your course is long enough, do it two or three times. Make at least 4 runs at each speed and record the times. Divide your time loss by the number of times you slowed to 15. Subtract it from your base time to determine your turn loss at each speed.

#### 2.4.4 "Speedometer Adjustments" (2026 frame `32m09s-3`; the only frame with the formula)
- **Correction and adjustment using a calculator** *(not allowed during race)*
  - Run the measured mile as many times as necessary to lock it in
  - If you are **early** you need to **increase** your factor
  - If you are **late** you need to **decrease** your factor
  - Convert your actual and the correct times into seconds
  - **Correct time / Actual time x Factor = New Factor**
- **Adjustment during the race** *(after or during speedo calibration section of course instructions)*
  - Divide your initial speedometer factor by 3600. This will give the number of "clicks" the speedometer must be changed for each second per hour that you are early or late. **Write this down**, either on your performance chart or on the back of your speedometer.
  - Convert your early or late time into seconds per hour, multiply by your single click factor
  - Increase or decrease your factor by this number of clicks

## 3. GRIID page anatomy (from the 2014, 2019 and 2025 sheets)

**Page frame.** Portrait letter page, black on white, photocopy-grade, no colour on the printed sheet (the school's red
arrows and ellipses, pink cursor rings and yellow highlighter are overlays or hand additions). No page header. One table
filling the page width with a thin header row above it ("A", "B", "C", "D" centred, regular weight, small), then the rows. The
table's outer border and every cell border are the same thin black line (about 1 px at slide scale), no shading, no zebra
rows, no heavier rule under the header.

**Column widths** (measured on three sheets; fractions of the A+B+C+D width): A 0.30, B 0.20, C 0.235, D 0.265 (2014 p4: 297 :
195 : 233 : 263 px; 2019 p6: 260 : 164 : 198 : 240 px). Ratio A : B : C : D = 1 : 0.66 : 0.78 : 0.89. Number gutter: about
0.04 of the full table width (0.13 of A), no header letter (a tiny dot or nothing in its header cell).

**Row height.** Content-driven, not fixed: the minimum (one speed line, a small sign) is about 0.11 of the table width (about
0.8 in on a letter sheet), a 3-line Column C stack about 0.155, a 6-line stack or a large CAMEO about 0.19 to 0.27, the
Information Box row about 0.25. The Handbook's "about 1 inch" is the average. A page holds 5 to 10 rows (2014: 6, 10, 6, 8, 8,
7, 7, 7, 7, 5 rows on the pages seen; 2019 p6: 6; the 2025 sheet: 7), so the printer fits as many rows as the page height
allows. Row numbers are continuous across pages (page 2 starts at 7, page 3 at 17, ...).

**Row number.** Small regular sans (about a third of the height of a C line), in the gutter, anchored at the bottom-left of the
row. When a row is complete the competitor strikes the number with a diagonal slash (2019 and 2025 hand-marked sheets).

**Column A (CAMEO).** Bold arrow route: a heavy shaft with a large solid arrowhead; the dot (solid circle about twice the shaft
width) is at the bottom of the cell, 10 to 15 percent above the bottom border, usually in the left third; the shaft rises
straight and, for a turn, bends at a right angle with the arrowhead at the end (straight for "go straight", a curve for bears,
forks and exits; some turns are drawn at a slant). Thin 1 px lines are the other roads, running the full width or height of
the cell through the junction (a cross road passes across the stem; a T stops at the stem). A dashed segment appears on
"traffic-signal / stop" rows: a short dashed line across the route above the dot (p11 #78), a short dashed stub on the cross
road (p3 #21, p5 #45) (meaning not stated in the frames; 08b reads it as the far side / stop line). Road names and sign
legends are **bold** Arial/Helvetica, mixed case, placed beside the road they name. Sign faces are drawn square-cornered
white rectangles with a black border about 1.5-2 px: "Speed Limit 65" (three lines), "Speed 45" (two lines, an assigned-speed
sign, which is not a speed-limit sign; in 2019 the sign says 45 and the Column C speed is 40), place-name signs ("Kennebunk /
Kennebunkport"). A sign stands on the side of the road it is on (left of the shaft = left side, right = right side); an overhead
sign has the arrow passing through the box; Column D repeats it in words ("sign on left", "(sign on right)", "sign overhead").
Warning signs are white diamonds with a thick black outline and a black pictogram (crossroad plus, T, right curve, reverse
curve, winding road, a Stop Ahead octagon with an up-arrow, or the words "Stop Ahead"), with a small rectangular advisory plaque
below (large number over small "MPH"). Business signs are black rectangles with small white mixed-case text ("Cumberland
Farms", "Tim Hortons", "Shell"). Landmarks are small line drawings with a bold caption (Ogunquit Playhouse, Toll Booth).
Controls at the junction, small and line-drawn: **STOP** = a tiny outlined octagon with "STOP" inside (about one eighth of the
row height, white fill); **traffic light** = a vertical rounded rectangle containing three circles (top circle dark), drawn at
the corner; **blinker** = an eight-ray sun-burst with a centre dot; **Y-fork with two STOP signs** = two octagons, one on each
leg. Bracketed text in Column A, "(US 1 North)", "(Kennebunk Pond)", "(Maine Mall Rd)", "(Ricker)", is bold in parentheses (an
unposted or informational name, REG VII.B.3.e). Handwritten lettering inside this column: only "STOP LIGHT" at a blinker.

**Column B.** Pictograms centred in the cell, with the odometer box centred under the tire, speedometer or begin-hourglass:
- **Odometer box**: four separate small squares (thin black border, one digit each, regular weight, white) with a small gap
  between squares; the fourth (tenths) square is solid black with a white digit. 0 0 9 0 = 9.0 mi, 0 2 4 0 = 24.0, 0 0 5 0 =
  5.0, 0 1 0 0 = 10.0. It appears only under begin-type symbols, never under the end hourglass.
- **Tire** (warm-up): a black tyre disc, white wire-wheel spokes in the middle and the white arc lettering "COKER" along the
  top (a sponsor mark), a little larger than a C text line.
- **Speedometer** (calibration): a landscape rectangle (about 2:1) showing a gauge scale arc with tick numerals 0 to 80, the word
  "MPH" and a needle.
- **Hourglass** (transit): an upright glass drawn with elliptical caps and two vertical posts, grey sand. Begin = sand in the
  upper bulb with a trickle in the neck; end = sand all collected in the lower bulb, upper bulb empty. Its height is about 0.35
  of the row.
- **Camcorder** (free zone): a video camera on a tripod, solid black. A free zone **begins** with the camcorder inside a circle
  with a diagonal slash (no camera); it **ends** with the plain camcorder (p11 #76 and #80).
- **Knife and fork**: crossed (an X), outlined; the word "no-host" in bold above it for an unhosted lunch.
- **Gas pump**: a grey line-drawn pump with display panel and hose.
- **Restroom**: a man silhouette, a small outhouse (pointed roof, door with a crescent), a woman silhouette, in a row.
- **Checkered flag**, **cup**, **Observation sign**: not shown in these frames.
Handwriting in B is the organizer's explanatory note (all capitals, thin upright pen, e.g. "BEGIN TIMED TRANSIT OF APPROX. 10 MILES
TAKE EXACTLY 30 MIN.") with long thin vertical arrows linking the begin and end symbols (tire/hourglass to the calibration row; camera to camera).

**Column C.** Bold Arial/Helvetica, centred horizontally and vertically in the cell, one item per line with generous leading
(pitch about 1.8 times the font size, so a 3-line stack of "0 MPH / 0m15s / 35 MPH" takes about 60 percent of a 160-unit row).
No frame around the cell. A "NN MPH" line occupies about 37 percent of the column width. Items:
- speeds "40 MPH"; pause stack "0 MPH / 0m15s / 45 MPH"; timed "30 MPH / 3m00s / 45 MPH", "25 MPH / 1m12s / 30 MPH",
  "40 MPH / 1m30s / 45 MPH / 1m20s / 50 MPH"; delayed "1m39s / 30 MPH" (here the carried speed 20 is hand-written above).
- advisory times in parentheses "(1m30s)", "(10m00s)", "(8m00s)", "(3m00s)", "(0m30s)", also bold and centred.
- a plain interval "16m00s", "29m00s", "30m00s", "55m00s" (no parentheses) for official intervals.
- a **calibration box**: a thin rectangle (about 78 percent of the column width), regular weight, interval left-aligned on the
  first line and cumulative right-aligned on the second, each with a bit of padding; the transit time "55m00s" is printed in bold
  below the box on the last calibration row.
- the **calibration start**: "50 MPH" then "29m00s" then the empty box, a dot (the "*" mark) at its top-left and "0m00.0s"
  right-aligned.
- the **restart/start**: the zone label in bold ("EDT", "PDT") centred above; the **wristwatch pictogram** below it: a top view of a digital watch,
  a rounded-rectangle case with a black band stub above and below (two thick black bars), a small crown on the right edge, four small circles (screws) at the corners,
  and an inner rounded-rectangle display holding the time in bold ("12:00:00", "1:40:00", "9:15:00"); under it the next item ("16m00s" or "20 MPH" or the first speed).
- the **end of timed portion**: a circle drawn around the same watch silhouette with a bold diagonal slash through it, the interval "30m00s" printed under it; the hourglass sits in Column B on the same row.
Handwriting in C: the struck-through "0m15s" (a short diagonal slash) with the pause "P10.2" written to the right in a cursive
upright hand about the size of the printed text; the lost time as a **circled negative number** under the OUT speed ("-2.9",
"-.5", "-4", "-1", "-2.2", "-1.2"); the carried speed written in an empty C cell in a large plain hand ("20"); small up and down
arrows or check marks beside speeds (2019 sheet); organizer notes in capitals in the margin.

**Column D.** Regular weight (not bold) Arial/Helvetica, left-aligned and top-aligned, 3-4 px padding, line height about 1.2.
Real Column D is mostly empty or holds short remarks: "comes quick", "comes very quick", "comes very quick-- use rightmost toll
booth", "look sharp", "look sharp-- small black-on-white sign", "look sharp-- 1st named road", "1st exit", "turn into middle lane",
"limited parking", "possible construction zone", "sign on left", "(sign on right)", "sign overhead", "Subway on right (look sharp)",
a price in a large font "$1.50", ordinal-counted controls with an inline icon ("2nd [traffic light]", "5th [traffic light]",
"2nd [blinker]", "after [crossroad diamond]"), and, in transits, lists of food and fuel (with smaller type for the "(off-course)"
tags and "WingStreet"). **No full sentence appears anywhere on these real sheets** (so the Handbook's Example Rally sentences are not on a
real race sheet). Handwriting in D (organizer or competitor): times of day and cumulative stops ("9:34:00", "9:59:18", "2:22:00"),
instructions ("HOLD SPEED AT THE SIGN. START STOPWATCH ..."), explanations ("PLANNED PAUSE TIMES ARE FROM CHART ..."), loss notes
("(LOST TIME ON A 40-35 TURN)"), all in upright capitals; highlighter over "comes quick" items.

**Page top and bottom.** Pre-read hand-writing sits above the header row at top centre: the speed carried from the previous page
("30", "35", "20", "20", "30"), about twice the height of a C text line. Footer below the table, three blocks, regular Arial about
the size of a D line, no rule: left "(c) 2014, Great Race"; centre two lines "Hemmings Motor News Great Race" / "Page 1 of 13";
right two lines "Hagerty Trophy Run" / "Friday, June 20, 2014" (2019: "Stage 7" / "Friday, June 28, 2019"; 2025 centre: "2025 Cutler Tire
Great Race Spring Rally" / "Page 19 of 2?", right "Stage 1" / "Friday, May 2, 2025"). When the next page starts with "comes quick",
the bottom margin carries a circled next-row number, a long arrow, and "COMES QUICK" with an arrowhead.

**TA box.** One rounded-corner rectangle (corner radius about 0.03 of the table width) with a black border about twice the cell-border weight, spanning from Column A to Column D, covering the grid lines; the row number sits in the gutter (122); text left-aligned
regular, larger than a normal D line, URL bold. (REG Example #18 and #36: "TA box (yellow)".)

**Information Box.** A rounded rectangle spanning the B and C columns (and growing the row to about 2.3 times a normal one), body text in regular Arial, left-aligned and wrapped;
Column D keeps its own list beside it.

**Dots, arrows and bold lines in one sentence.** The dot is where you are (the approach), the bold arrow is where the route goes,
thin lines are roads not taken, a bracketed bold name is an unposted road, dashed short lines are drawn on stop/signal rows.

**Clock face (instrument, not the book).** The 2026 lapboard shows a bezel rally clock: a rotating black ring with minute
numerals 5 to 60, inner green and red coloured scales, a white face with 12-hour numerals, black hour and minute hands, a red second hand.

## 5. Spec deltas (what the frames establish that SPECS.md V2/V3 does not say, or says differently)

- **GRIID-001 / UI-029 ("five-column row (number, A, B, C, D)")**: confirmed, add that the number column is a headerless gutter (about 4 percent of the width) and only A-D are lettered; REG VII.B.3.c(5) calls D "fifth" by counting the gutter.
- **GRIID-002 (calibration start "26m00s" / "50 MPH" / "* 0m00.0s")**: the real 2014 sheet prints **"50 MPH" / "29m00s" / box** (speed first, then the official time, then an empty box with a dot at top-left and "0m00.0s" right-aligned). REG Example #5 and 08 list it in the other order. Real wins. Also, GRIID-002's `box "5m32.0s" over "7m21.3s"` should say: interval left-aligned on top, cumulative right-aligned below, regular weight; the plain transit time ("55m00s", bold) follows below the box on the last calibration row (matches STAGE-009).
- **GRIID-002 (restart as "a clock face 'CDT 8:55:00' over '30 MPH'")**: the clock face is a **digital wristwatch drawing** with the time inside its display, the zone label ("EDT", "PDT") above it, and the speed or interval under it. The warm-up start also carries a watch: "EDT / 12:00:00 / 16m00s" (a time-of-day start with the interval below). A restart row can carry a **timed chain** under the watch (2019 #40: "40 MPH / 1m30s / 45 MPH / 1m20s / 50 MPH").
- **GRIID-003 ("end timed portion crossed-out watch")**: it is a circle-slash drawn around the watch silhouette, with the interval ("30m00s") printed under it, in Column C; the hourglass with an odometer box (9.0 mi here) is in Column B of the same row; Column D carries "END OF TIMED SECTION". Consistent with GRIID-010; the picture is not a clock with hands.
- **GRIID-003 ("crossed camera / camera", "meal (knife and fork)")**: the camera is a **camcorder on a tripod**; begin = inside a slashed circle, end = plain. The meal icon is **crossed** knife and fork with the label **"no-host"** above it (hosted vs no-host meals are distinguished in the book). The rest stop icon is man + outhouse + woman. TA is not a Column B symbol: it is a full-width rounded box (GRIID-013), so drop the Column B `ta`.
- **GRIID-003 (hourglass full / empty)**: specify as drawn: begin = sand in the upper bulb with a trickle; end = sand all in the lower bulb. The odometer box (four separate squares, last one black) appears only under begin symbols (tire, speedometer, begin hourglass).
- **GRIID-004 (Column D sentences) and GRIID-009**: no real sheet in any frame prints a sentence in D. Real D holds only remarks, ordinals with an icon ("2nd [signal]", "5th [signal]", "after [crossroad diamond]"), side-of-road words ("sign on left", "(sign on right)", "sign overhead"), prices in a large font ("$1.50"), and service lists with "(off-course)" tags. Supports 'race' style as the real thing; add the ordinal-with-icon and the side-of-road remarks to the note vocabulary.
- **GRIID-001 (sign boxes on the side of the road)**: confirmed (left of the arrow = left side, centred = overhead); the Column D words repeat it. Real sign faces: "Speed Limit NN" (3 lines) **and "Speed NN"** (2 lines); in 2019 the "Speed 45" sign sits on a row whose Column C says 40, and "Speed Limit 50" on a row that says 45: the number on the sign is not the assigned speed. Warning signs are diamonds with an optional advisory plaque ("30 MPH", "35 MPH") below. STAGE-007 / VII.D already say limits are references only when they apply; the frames show the other wording.
- **GRIID-011 ("the row before the end of an advisory transit prints (0m30s)")**: the real sheet prints a **countdown over several rows**: (10m00s) at the meal row, (8m00s) next, then (3m00s), (0m30s) before the restart (2014 rows 21, 22, 24, 25). Each is the time left to the end of the transit.
- **GRIID-013 (TA row = full-width yellow banner)**: in the 2026 frame the TA box is a **rounded-corner, black-outlined box** across A-D (white/grey in the photo), text left-aligned, "Today is Stage N", and a **handwritten 4-digit "Code 8537"** beside it; row number in the gutter (122). REG Example #18/#36 say yellow; keep yellow fill but add rounded corners and the border. The meaning of the Code is not in the frames; it may be the day's Password.
- **GRIID-014 ("real sheets carry 6-9")**: the 2014 sheets carry **5 to 10** rows (page 2 has 10, page 12 has 5), the 2019 page 6 carries 6, the 2025 sheet 7; heights vary with content, so the number per page is a result of the layout, not a setting. The same sheets total 13 pages (2014), 22 pages (2019), about 21 (2025).
- **GRIID-012 / PREREAD-001 (pre-read conventions)**: the frames give the exact marks: strike "0m15s" with a short diagonal and write "P" + number to its right ("P10.2"); write the lost time **circled and negative** under the OUT speed ("-2.9"); write the carried speed in large digits at **page top centre** and in an **empty Column C box**; write "COMES QUICK" at the **bottom of the previous page** with the circled number of the next page's first row and a long arrow; write a time of day in Column D at each restart ("1:40 + 42 = 2:22"); strike/slash the row number when done; use small up/down arrows beside speeds for the change direction; the note to carry the speed is explicit on the page ("WHEN INSTRUCTIONS ARE RECEIVED, WRITE SPEED FROM PRECEDING PAGE HERE"). The V3 note "(D15) pre-written chart losses beside stops and turns ('10.2', '-2.3')" should read "P10.2" and a circled negative loss.
- **GRIID (new, no spec)**: **Information Boxes** (end of day): rounded rectangle across Column B and C with a text paragraph (reception time, address, parking, parc ferme/"do not go to your hotel until after you have been to the Observation Checkpoint"); a Column D list of fuel/food alongside; stand-alone rows with a gas pump and no timing in C ("APPROX. 9 MILES AND 30 MIN. TO GET TO THE OBSERVATION CHECKPOINT"). STAGE-001's end-of-day sequence lacks the information box, and no spec id covers an "off the clock: drive any safe speed, follow the instructions to the finish gate" segment.
- **STAGE-006 (calibration run / official time)**: consistent: the real 2014 run prints "29m00s" at the start (24 miles) and its last cumulative is 28m43.2s, which rounds up to the printed 29 minutes; the transit allowance printed on the last row is 55m00s for 5 miles. Calibration boxes run from row 7 (4m03.4s) to row 18 (28m43.2s): **12 boxes**, against 3-6 in the Handbook Example Rally. Rows 7-16 each carry a named sign (place-name signs, the sign on the left/right/overhead), so STAGE-006's "3-6 calibration points" is a minimum; the real 2014 run carries about a dozen.
- **STAGE-007 / SPEED-001 (speeds)**: the school charts' speed set is {10, 12, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 48, 50, 55}: **12 mph is a row**, 48 is a row, and the Brandon tool's matrices use only 15..55 in steps of 5 (plus 0 in the accel/decel one). SPEED-001 lists 10-55 and 48; add 12.
- **CHART-001 (c) turns**: the school charts show **T@15 and T@20** (2024) or T@15 only (2026); the 25-year chart splits turns into "Turn at 15", "RIGHT turn at 20" and "LEFT turn at 20" (left and right differ by about 0.1-0.3 s); "+x.x" in a turn column means a gain (loss below zero), "N/A" means the approach speed is at or below the apex speed. The Handbook's own method (slide, 2024 18) is still a single 15 mph turn; CHART-001(c) can stay single but may record these.
- **CHART-001 (b) pause chart**: the "Pause time at 15-second stop" matrix = 15 - Dec(in) - Acc(out) exactly (all legible cells, 11 x 11), which gives a ready formula; and the Brandon tool defines (b) as "Start / Stop Lost Time" with "(START/STOP TIME)-(accel IN + accel OUT)" under it and says "instead of pausing for alloted 15 seconds, pause for this amount of time".
- **CHART-006 (D06 tool)**: the real tool has **three measurement sheets** with Run 1-4 + Avg. (constant speed, from stop, start from roll stopping at the cone), explicit order "Do First / Do Second / Do Third", and the output tabs "Time lost due to Accel", "Time lost due to Decel", with a printed "Charts" tab (Accel/Decel, Start/Stop Lost Time, Turns). It warns "If you see ANY negative numbers ... something went wrong", matching the outlier rule, and reminds "To correct for lost time, start that amount of time EARLIER from a STOP / If 4.5 seconds are lost, start 4.5 seconds earlier". It uses 15..55 only.
- **CHART-005 / CAL-006 (calibration arithmetic)**: slide wording is "Correct time / Actual time x Factor = New Factor" (early = increase the factor, late = decrease), "divide your initial speedometer factor by 3600 = clicks per second per hour", "a calculator is not allowed during the race" for the first method, "write this down on your performance chart or on the back of your speedometer"; the 25-year chart's box says "Sec/hr error x factor / 3600; 1 sec = 1.36 clicks; 3 sec = 4 clicks; late: set backward; early: set forward". The Initial calibration uses **5 rotations of the wheel** and "Mark top and bottom of the back side!" (2024 and 2026), "two small magnets on the left front wheel with JB Weld, put magnets on the spare as well". CHART-005's worked example (28m43.2s correct vs 28m47.3s actual) is exactly the handwritten note in 2014 row 18 ("ASSUME YOUR ACTUAL TIME IS 28M47.3S. YOU ARE 4.1SEC. LATE"), which is the organizer's own calibration exercise.
- **TAF-001 (web form fields)**: real fields are: Login screen: Car Number, Password, Phone Number, green "Login"; entry screen: Stage (shown), Leg Number (select "Leg 3"), Between Instructions (two numeric boxes), Allowance in two boxes "m" and "s" (3 m 40 s), Reason (one-line text "Delayed by train"), green "Submit", yellow "CLICK to see Time Allowances Sumbitted" (typo as printed), red "CLICK this after submitting ALL Time Allowances for the ENTIRE stage", grscores.com. **No witness field**, no separate phone field on the entry screen, the cause is a single free-text line; phone number is typed once at login. Update TAF-001/UI-037's "cause, witnesses" to cause only and add the "see submitted" button. The in-book TA box states the 15m00s window and the URL; a handwritten code appears in it.
- **TAF-002 (the 'Delayed by train' example)**: the frame's own example is leg 3, instructions 44 & 45, 3m40s, "Delayed by train" (the transcript's "3:47 made up 7" is not on screen).
- **INST-001 / equipment**: the 2026 lapboard shows a **bezel rally clock** (rotating outer minute ring, coloured scales) with the stopwatch below it and the page on the left, with pens/highlighter clipped at the clock's side. UI/LESSON for the equipment screen could draw it; the REG II.H.1.d "no digital readout" rule is about the clock instrument, not the book's watch drawings.
- **Nothing found for**: the red Observation sign, the checkered flag row, a Timewise rear face, a "page n of 26" sheet, or a 2026-issue sheet at all: the 2026 school uses 2014 and 2019 and 2025 samples only. V3's "Visual moments worth a screenshot" entries for those cannot be satisfied from these frames.
