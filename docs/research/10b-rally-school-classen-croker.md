## A. PART 1 - John Classen, Director of Competition

### A1. Speaker and agenda
- [00:04] John Classen, "director of competition for Great Race". First hour of rally school, ~10 min late. Official start of today's run 11:30. Plan: ~1 h,
  10-15 min break, then Bill Croker does the second half (Part 2). He then leaves to lead the route and set up all the checkpoints.
- Agenda actually covered: nature of the event (00:34) -> checkpoint types and terminology: stage, leg, checkpoint (02:07-05:09) ->
  how scoring works with a worked example (09:13-18:34) -> reading the Course Instructions columns A and D, instruction by instruction
  (19:05-35:45) -> columns B and C, instruction by instruction (36:18-53:22) -> finish-line check-in and a few questions (53:22-55:01).

### A2. Nature of the event
- [00:34-01:05] Not "fastest car wins"; "we can't do a fastest car wins type event on public roads". It is a "controlled speed endurance road rally".
  Organizers give "precise driving instructions telling you what roads to take, what speeds to try to attain, when to leave various places".
- [01:35] Leave on time at the right point, follow the route, drive exactly the speeds = perfect score (he jokes "$50,000 and a trip to Japan"; not a rule).
- [01:35-02:37] How do they know? "we will check on your progress several times each day ... at various checkpoints". Two types: Observation (red) and Timing (green).

### A3. Checkpoints and scoring
Checkpoint types
- [02:37] Observation checkpoint: red sign that says STOP, held on the ground; "red means stop; you need to stop and talk to the worker".
- [03:07] Purposes of a red sign: (1) equipment inspection, "make sure you don't have any illegal equipment in the car"; (2) "collect time allowances";
  (3) "most likely place ... is at the finish line each day". At the finish they check you in, "may have your checkpoint times or your scores for the day",
  and endorse the Course Instructions "to verify that you have been here".
- [03:37] Red signs may also deliver emergency instructions ("the road is flooded today, we will flag you down and give you emergency instructions").
- [04:08] Green sign = Timing checkpoint: "we will time you and we will give you a score ... on the portion of the event that just ended". You won't be told the score.
- [05:39] "Green means go; the minimum that you need to do at a green sign is nothing". Organizers do the timing and the scoring centrally.
- [06:11-07:12] What he would like: write down, in the instruction you are working on, the checkpoint number and your arrival time to the second
  ("nine o'clock, 22 minutes, 14 seconds"). Reason: score disputes. If a team says "mark me at 9:12:17 and I think I got there at 9:13", that is investigable;
  "I don't know" is much harder. "Not necessary if you have other things going on ... but if you could write down the time that would be helpful."
- [07:42] How many timing checkpoints per day: "anywhere from one to lots and lots". "We do have four or five checkpoint crews so there will probably be at least four,
  maybe five, maybe more timing checkpoints each day."
- [08:12] Another red sign: "at lunch most days"; a crew member (name garbled "K"; green shirt, grey hat) collects time allowances there.

Terminology
- [04:08-05:09] Stage = a day of competition. Trophy Run = stage 0 ("today's stage is the trophy run"), tomorrow = stage one. Front cover of the instructions and the
  footer of each page state the stage number. Leg = a portion of a stage that ends at a checkpoint. "Legs and checkpoints are sort of referred to interchangeably;
  they're not quite the same: a leg is a portion of the route that ends at a checkpoint; the checkpoint is where we time you at the end of a leg."
- [05:09] Start the run on leg one; at the first green sign (checkpoint 1) "immediately you will begin leg two" and so on.

Start time arithmetic
- [09:13-09:43] Know your start time: it is in the Order of Start (handed out the day before), in instruction #1, and in the event schedule. Today 11:30.
  "Does that mean 117 cars pull out side by side at 11:30? Obviously not."
- [10:14-11:17] Worked example, car 129, start position (ASP) 112 listed on the second page of the order of start near the bottom: 11:30 + 112 min =
  1 h 52 min later = 1:22 pm (the captions say "122"). "If your start position is 3 it's 11:30 plus 3 (11:33); if 55, 12:25." So car number (129) and
  start position (112) are different things. Start time = stage start time + start position in minutes.
- [11:49] Perfect time to checkpoint 1 in the example: 42 min 10 s, so due 2:04:10 pm (1:22:00 + 42:10, derived).

Scoring example (stated numbers are "examples, not the correct numbers")
- [12:21] Arrives 2:04:06 against 2:04:10 = 4 s early; leg 1 score 4.
- [12:52] Leg 2 true time 3 min 9 s; due 2:07:15; arrives 2:07:20 = 5 s late. Leg 2 score 5.
- [13:25] "Early and late arrival do not cancel, do not compensate; they're cumulative": after two legs the score is 9 s, not 1 s.
- [13:55] End of day: add the leg scores, multiply by the age factor of the vehicle ("a 63 ... 0.927 (?) or something like that"). Lowest score wins the day; rookies
  who have the best score are the rookie winner.
- [14:26] Trophy Run: optional, no money, trophies at the party. "It is a tiebreaker down the road" - if two teams are tied in California the team with the higher Trophy Run
  finish wins; it "doesn't count directly toward the championship".
- [14:56-15:28] Cars run at one-minute intervals. The 30-second interval mentioned earlier "has nothing to do with today's trophy run"; it applies only between the hotel and
  the train station tomorrow; "once you leave the train station it's back to one-minute intervals".
- [15:59] Subtlety: leg 2's true time is "based on not when they should have arrived at checkpoint one but when they actually did arrive". (Leg reset.)

The flat-tire example (leg reset and caps) [15:59-18:34]
- A 70-minute leg, flat tire, 8 minutes lost, next eight cars pass. Team drives faster (legally) and gets back to 6 minutes late at the checkpoint.
- "We have a maximum of late penalty at any checkpoint if you're less than a half an hour late: only two minutes." So 6 min late = 2 min penalty.
- Wrong move: they then keep making up the other six minutes on the next leg. "Each leg starts from scratch when you get to the green sign." They would then be 6 min
  early on leg 2: "we're not quite so forgiving on early arrival, you get up to a five minute penalty." Total 2 + 5 = 7 min. 
- Right move: "once you get to that checkpoint you are now dead on time for the next leg ... go back to speeds, follow the instructions even though you're six minutes behind where you
  think you should be you can still get a perfect score; we call that an ace." Aces get ace stickers ("lots of stickers on some cars").

### A4. Course Instructions: delivery and structure
- [19:36] Most mornings, stages 2-9 handed out by two crew members 30 minutes before your start time. Tomorrow's instructions are given at the finish line "under the front canopy"
  when you finish today; there is no separate Order of Start tonight ("it's the same piece of paper you already have").
- [19:36-20:39] "Each course instruction has five columns; the first column does not have a letter" - it holds the instruction numbers 1, 2, 3 ... in order. Do everything
  instruction 1 requires, then 2, etc. Column A = CAMEO diagrams of intersection and sign configurations. Column B = "the portion of the event that is beginning at that place".
  Column C = "timing and speed information". Column D = "our hint column ... in conjunction with column A it helps you do the instruction correctly".
- [21:40] CAMEO grammar: a bold dot at the bottom = your position entering the intersection; arrow = your position leaving it; bold line dot-to-arrow = your route; thin line(s) =
  "the road or roads not taken".
- [20:39-21:09] Road names in parentheses in the CAMEO "may not be identified as you make the turn" (known from days there or identified at the next intersection).
- [24:50] Information in brackets in Column D "is interesting stuff that you can completely ignore" (historic Route 66 comments).
- [22:13] Hints: "it's the second traffic light you'll come to". Without the hint "you'd have to get to the first traffic light, is this it?". 
- [22:43, 23:16] "comes quick" in Column D = this instruction follows the previous one closely.
- [22:43] Overhead sign: drawn with the sign sitting on top of the arrow. [23:47] Sign on the right: arrow on the left, sign box on the right. Hint: "most freeway signs are green; that'll be a
  brown sign" so you match the right sign. Signs can be on the left too (instruction 55: "all the signs so far have been over here or on the right; this one's on the left").
- [23:16] Sign wording: the quoted names "will be among the wording on the sign and a prominent portion" (not necessarily all of it) - the "in whole or in part" idea.
- [24:18] "I-" is the abbreviation for interstate; instruction: merge left to stay on I-255; "don't take an exit". 
- [23:16, 25:21] Double intersection / double exit: two thin roads not taken; stay on the middle road, or exit right then right again.
- [26:24] "The rules say that you will not take private roads, dead-end roads, unpaved roads, questionable roads unless there's an instruction. Such roads may be identified by a dashed
  line" in the CAMEO ("might be an alley, a dead-end road").
- [25:21] Duplicate landmarks are disambiguated: "the near right corner Jack-in-the-Box is not the first Jack-in-the-Box you'll come to; there is a different one at instruction 21".
- [26:54-27:26] Speed-limit signs are instructions: "speed limit 40", next "speed limit 40"; a curve-warning sign with "35 mph" plate on top of it; "speed limit 45 AHEAD" is a yellow
  diamond sign, "not a speed limit 45 sign".
- [27:58] "Make sure you have the emergency instructions": at instruction 30 the printed instruction says straight, but the road was closed (rain/flood) so the emergency sheet
  directs a detour between #29 and #31. A question "which direction at #30?" got "left" (from emergency sheet) - the printed answer "straight" was wrong that day.
- [28:29] "Stop ahead" sign (yellow) is its own instruction, distinct from the stop sign itself. A stop sign on a "slanty intersection". "Comes quickly after the previous instruction".
- [29:32-31:38] What to do at intersections that are not shown: "obvious principal road". "The event regulations specify that ... follow the obvious principal road. In most cases the obvious
  principal is the road directly ahead." For a curve warning sign where the road curves with the center stripe and the side road has a stop sign: "if your grandmother said come visit me, get on
  this road and keep on going you would go around that corner without giving it another thought; that's the obvious principal road". Instruction 45 "follow this curve warning sign" is a hint that
  resolves it. "I have tried very hard" to avoid ambiguity.
- [32:10] Two-part instruction (#49): "a sign and an intersection: you turn right on the slanty crossroad but only after you pass the sign" (yellow diamond crossroad sign with a plus).
  [49:45] "an instruction like this - you do your speed change not at the intersection but at the crossroad sign".
- [32:10-32:41] Street-naming sign (small city-style street sign) is a sign type; instruction 57 double hint: the first paved road you come to, and the sign is on the left.
- [35:45] A highway sign "interstate 270 [gap] 6" means 6 miles ahead; "there may be other things on the sign".

Refuel, finish, and finish boxes
- [33:11] #62 refuel because "the next refueling stop isn't for 190 miles; we think you all have 200 mile range". [36:18-36:48] The 190 miles include the rest of today, the five miles to
  tomorrow's start, and then tomorrow; "if you have 250 mile range we encourage you to refuel when we tell you to". Gas stations are also available at lunch although not listed.
- [34:13] Finish: #76 left into the hotel; checkered flag; "comes very quick after the previous instruction"; "stop at the observation checkpoint, the red sign under the canopy" - about
  a minute per team, expect a queue ("just sit there in the rain until you can pull up").
- [34:13-35:15] "Finish boxes": two rounded-corner ovals at the end of the instructions: tonight's social/trophies and race-car parking; next day's route (city to city), when/where to get
  next day's instructions, breakfast start times at each hotel, how to reach the start, "this is the only time you'll be at 30-second intervals", next day's start time, and the distance to the
  first refuel.

### A7. Traps/rookie mistakes stressed by Classen
- Treating early and late as cancelling; trying to make up lost time after a checkpoint (carry-over error).
- Missing the emergency instruction sheet; not writing down the time at the start of a transit/emergency interval.
- Pulling up to the restart sign before your minute; leaving the hotel late.
- Not checking in at the finish red sign (DNF).
- Misreading "speed limit 45 ahead" as "speed limit 45"; misreading a "comes quick".
- Choosing the first of two same-name landmarks (Jack-in-the-Box).
- Missing that Column C blank = continue the previous speed (which may be on the previous page).

## B. PART 2 - Bill Croker (veteran, author of the Rookie Handbook)

### B1. Speaker and agenda

  takes expertise, time, effort, practice; you have to do more than one Great Race before you get very good."

- Then: picking up instructions/preparing (02:05-12:36), the start line (12:36-14:40), communication/vocabulary (14:40-21:00), stop signs and pause times (19:57-24:34), corners (24:34-25:35), speed changes (25:35-28:09), making up time (28:09-30:14),

### B2. Rules, numbers, procedures, tips by topic

Start procedure, clocks, instructions pick-up
- [02:05-03:07] A digital WWV time-calibration clock is at the canopy where instructions are picked up (and "today" as you leave). "You should calibrate or check your clock and/or your stopwatch every morning so you make
  sure you are on the same time as all the course workers" - can be done any time, "you don't have to wait till it's your turn".
- [03:07] You can also use time.gov (internet) or a phone call to WWV. Q about cell-phone clock: "I trust the one out front; that's what John and his crews are using". A teammate "trusts the phone call to WWV but verifies against John's number" - "trust but verify".
- [07:50] Instructions normally 30 minutes before your scheduled start. [08:20] "You do not have to sit there at the hotel for those 30 minutes; you can leave at any time after you get your course instructions" (stage-start example: pick up at 11:00 + start position for an 11:30 start).
- [07:20] "The key time point on this rally today is instruction 26 ... the second digital clock of the day, that's the one where you have to be precisely on time", no matter when you left or finished the speedo check.
- [06:49] "If you do not start on time at that restart point in the field, if you're a minute late and don't know it, all the good corners and stop signs won't help ... people get started a minute off and don't know it, and if you don't know it you just proceed in ignorance."
- [12:36] At the restart: you arrive early, "probably there'll be 10 to 20 cars sitting there; find a safe place to wait away from the action", watch others go; after the car in front of you has left (it is your minute) pull up to the start position and wait for your exact time.
  "Don't go a minute early." [14:10] "There's nobody at that start line to tell you when to go; it is your total responsibility to know when to leave." If the car ahead has missed his minute and shows up late, "he has a problem; he needs to allow you to go when you're supposed to go". If he sits and doesn't go, "pull up around him and leave on your minute" - an early-leaver "found himself a mile down the road stopping and waiting to get back on time".
- [13:08] Dash-clock tip: the minute hand sits between numbers and (caption garbled, ?) appears to lead/lag depending on which side of the dial it is on, so "we use the time of day on our stopwatch to make sure we're on the absolute right minute; then switch the stopwatch back to its normal function".

Pre-read of the instructions (do before leaving the hotel; ~10 minutes)
- [08:53] "Count the pages" ("really bad news at two o'clock in the afternoon you find out page 26 is missing"); printing/assembly errors happen.
- [09:24] Punch three holes at the top for the clipboard. Write the current speed at the top of every page from the bottom of the previous page ("when you look at the bottom of page 5 you're doing 20 miles an hour; 20 at the top of page 6"). [09:57] If an instruction (e.g. #35) has no speed written,
  copy the speed from the previous instruction into that blank.
- [10:30-11:30] Highlight "comes quick" in Column D; "if it's at the top of a page, make yourself a big note on the page before" so you turn the page quickly. The example (#34 after a stop sign at #33 at the bottom of the previous page) was caught by a show of veterans' hands. Hint: "it was another stop sign but it might not be" - could be a turn.
- [12:03] Joke: "83 separate mistakes you can make ... I've counted them"; a rookie reading "20 mph then going to zero at the stop sign" crossed off one.

Speed changes (the "split the sign" method)
- [25:35-27:39] Example: 40 mph at #34 to 25 mph at #35. "Brake smoothly and consistently across that sign so you cross the sign at half the speed between the two speeds - 32.5 mph." "Don't wait until the sign and then start braking, or brake ahead of time and get all the way to the speed before the sign." "Splitting the sign." "If you need to err a little bit,
  err on braking a little too early, especially if it's a very large speed change; you'll be more accurate." "You do the same thing going up."
- [27:07-27:39] Practice: figure out the half speed in your head before you reach the sign, glance at the sign as it goes by, check the speedometer, "see how close you hit it ... you'll get better and better". "If you do those right you won't have any lost time."

Stops and pauses
- [21:30-22:02] The printed 15-second stop-sign pause assumes instantaneous stopping and starting; no car (even a Corvette or 426 Hemi Plymouth) can, "so you need to stop for less time".
- [22:02-22:32] Use the stop chart in the Rookie Handbook: read the IN speed, go across to the OUT speed; it says e.g. 8.6 s instead of 15. "You're probably not going to be able to handle tenths of a second; round off to the nearest whole second." If you did not make your own chart, drive "like a '36 Packard" and use the handbook's. A 426 Hemi team used the handbook chart at "Joplin (?) a few weeks ago and thought it worked fine".
  "You can make your own chart ... for next year."
- [23:02] "If you do the stop sign correctly, when you come out of the stop sign you will be back on time."
- [19:57-21:00] Stop-sign technique in his car: driver stops; navigator starts the stopwatch; she says "we're going to turn left on 8 seconds" (the chart pause), by then she is at about 4 s, and counts "4, 5, 6, 7, GO" - "the last word is always GO because the driver forgot when he was supposed to go even though he heard it four seconds earlier; he's watching traffic".
  "Always count UP rather than down; use GO as the last word." As the driver accelerates the navigator reminds him how fast to leave: "you're going to 40".
- [23:32] If the driver is held up by oncoming traffic: let the watch run, stop it when he goes, subtract the planned pause (chart 8 s, held 15 s = 7 s behind) and make that up. If he leaves early (e.g. at 6 instead of 8 because he saw a gap between two 18-wheelers) you are 2 s too fast and must lose it.
- [24:02] "Make those adjustments fairly quickly, the first opportunity you have; do not carry them forward from one page to the next ... sure enough a checkpoint will show up and you'll be in trouble."

Turns (corners)
- [24:34] Use the same chart idea: in speed (row), out speed (column), the intersection = net seconds lost on the corner. "You always lose speed on a corner."
- [25:05] The handbook corner chart assumes slowing to 15 mph at the apex, shifting gears, accelerating out. "You can make your own chart; there are more advanced techniques for corners ... ask the veterans when you're ready for it."
- [27:39-28:41] Example: coming round a corner the navigator says "you're down 3.6 seconds"; make it up with the ten percent rule.

Making up time
- [28:09] Ten percent rule: multiply the error by 10; go 10% faster for that many seconds (3.6 s -> 36 s; 30 mph -> 33 mph for 36 s). Works "up or down".
- [29:14-29:44] Lost-time formula (in the Handbook): (speed difference / correct speed) x seconds. Example: school bus, 30 instead of 40 for 30 s: 10/40 x 30 = 7.5 s lost to make up.
- [04:08-05:43] Safety: "all the speeds are below the speed limit, even if you have to make up time you should be able to do it within the speed limit." "No score is worth an accident." After a wrong turn you are five cars out of position: "your temptation is to pass the next five cars and get back in position; we've all done it
  ... if you can't drive fast enough to do it, if you're breaking speed limits be very careful." Cars behind that are trying to regain position: "be courteous to them but it is their problem not yours ... they need to find a safe place to pass you."

- [32:26-32:58] "Some transits are just intended to get you someplace, not intended for you to take a break": a 45-minute transit can be consumed by a bathroom stop; "make sure you know how long it's going to take you to get to that restart point".

### B3. Driver/navigator teamwork and vocabulary (details)
- [14:40] The team needs "a way of communicating that is clear, concise ... if you start talking about other things you will miss a sign".

  The navigator should "every few minutes say you're doing 40, you're doing 40" (humour: drivers have bruises on the right arm), or the driver reports the speed.
- [18:56] "No matter if your navigator makes all the errors, it's team errors and it takes a team to fix them." ### B5. Traps and common rookie mistakes (Croker)
- Not starting on the exact minute; not knowing you are a minute off.
- Missing pages; not carrying the speed to the top of the next page; missing a "comes quick" at the top of a page.
- Transit mistakes (not recording the entry time; using the transit for a bathroom stop and being late back).
- Carrying time errors forward across pages instead of correcting at the first opportunity.
- Driving to an assumed last checkpoint; over-speeding to regain position after a wrong turn.
- Leaving a stop sign too early or sitting too long and not noticing/correcting.
- Saying "turn left" and not the exact sign type (hard vs soft, snake left vs right).
- Doing the second identical instruction zero times or three times.
