## 0. What a TSD rally is (one-paragraph framing)

- SCCA's framing: RoadRally is run on open public roads at speeds below the legal limit; two-person teams compete at "reading route instructions carefully, following the route, and maintaining an assigned average speed." For TSD events, penalty points are assigned for arriving at a checkpoint early or late, lowest score wins. (https://racer.com/2016/04/06/how-to-start-racing-scca-roadrally/ ; https://www.scca.com/announcements/291-roadrally-beginners-guide-to-tsd-zoom-get-together)
- Northwest Classic Rally: a TSD rally "is not a race; it is a precision driving competition"; the basis is "the accurate application of complex rules under time pressure to follow a designated route in a set period of time." Written instructions are "somewhat 'coded' by use of abbreviations or graphics." Teams start at one-minute intervals and are timed at locations not given in the instructions; a rally has multiple separately-scored "legs" of roughly 5–20 miles. Penalties accrue for early/late arrival and for going off course. There are three roles in the car (driving, navigating, timekeeping) but only two people, so "the navigator typically also keeps time and the driver must contribute to keeping on course." (https://www.nwclassicrally.org/How-TSDs-Work.html)
- Cascade Sports Car Club (CSCC) rulebook: "the object of a time-speed-distance road rally is to complete the rally route on course and as close to on time as possible." Being on course = following the rallymaster's intended route; being on time = arriving at checkpoints when the rallymaster intended. (https://github.com/jhelwig/cscc-tsd-rally-rules , file road-rally-rules.tex)
- "Official time -- intended or allowed time -- is the amount of time it takes to drive the intended route at the average speeds specified in the route instructions." CSCC measures the route in thousandths of a mile between speed changes and computes allowed time to the nearest hundredth of a minute. (CSCC road-rally-rules.tex, same repo)
- SCCA Beginners Guide (2021) distinguishes two TSD flavors: **Tour rallies** are "designed so you will not get lost," no tricks, the challenge is to stay on time; **Course rallies** require logic and observation to follow the route, with possible off-course scenarios and traps, so the challenge is twofold (follow the course AND stay on time). (https://cdn.connectsites.net/user_files/scca/downloads/000/057/386/Beginners%20Guide%20to%20TSD%20Presentation%20Handout.pdf)
- Minimum kit per SCCA Beginners Guide: a driver and a navigator; clipboard; a timing device displaying seconds or 1/100th of a minute; odometer; phone apps; pens/pencils/highlighters/sticky notes. (same handout)
- Minimum kit per Grassroots Motorsports forum veterans: "a stopwatch, a car with a working odometer, a pen, a highlighter, and a good navigator"; a map light is near-mandatory; use a RED light at night to preserve the driver's night vision. (https://grassrootsmotorsports.com/forum/grm/learn-me-tsd-rallying/4480/page1/)

## 1. Core concepts and vocabulary

Terminology below is drawn from (a) the PCA Parade Rally Glossary (https://mediaassets.pca.org/pages/microsites/parade2024/files/uploads/44736_pcars_2025_tsd_rally_appendix_vii.pdf), (b) the SCCA RoadRally Rules (https://cdn.connectsites.net/user_files/scca/downloads/000/073/902/2025%20RoadRally%20Rulebook.pdf), (c) the Cascade Sports Car Club rulebook glossary (https://github.com/jhelwig/cscc-tsd-rally-rules/blob/master/glossary.tex — the one primary rulebook whose full text was directly readable in this environment), (d) Detroit Region "Road Rally 103 – Glossary of Terms" (http://drscca.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/road_rally_103.pdf), and regional General Instructions. Wording differs slightly between clubs; the simulator should treat the *event's* General Instructions as authoritative.

### 1.1 Speed and time vocabulary

| Term | Definition (source) |
|---|---|
| **CAST** | "Change Average Speed To" — the preceding average speed is discontinued and replaced by the value after CAST (PCA glossary; CSCC glossary: "Change average speed to."). SCCA Beginners Guide: average speed "will likely change many times throughout the rally, using CAST." Example from PCA San Diego: "at mile 12, you'll CAST 25 MPH." (https://www.pcasdr.org/rally) |
| **CSU / CSD** | CSCC: "Change average speed up by the amount indicated" / "Change average speed down by the amount indicated" (relative CASTs). (CSCC glossary.tex) |
| **Official mileage (OM)** | Distances in the route instructions as measured by the rallymaster's odometer. CSCC: an instruction with OM "must be executed at that mileage provided that: The reference is correct and An appropriate action point exists"; competitors need only determine mileage to 0.1 mile. (CSCC route-instructions.tex) |
| **Odometer correction factor** | Your odometer / official odometer over the odometer-check leg. CVR PCA: "Factor = (your car's mileage) divided by (the Official Mileage)"; then "multiply all distances written in your route instructions by the Factor." Also "adjust your speeds by the same factor; if the given speed is 40 mph you would drive at 38 (.96 x 40)." (https://cvrpca.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/2013_TSD_Rally_Basics.pdf ; same example at https://www.pcasdr.org/rally). CSCC formula: factor = "Your odometer Miles / Official miles"; corrected speed = CAST x factor; "when odometer readings fall short of official distances, drivers must reduce speed...when odometer readings exceed official distances, increased speed is necessary." (CSCC TSD_formulas.tex). Rally West novice school: "Your Distance / Rally Distance = Odo Correction Factor"; "Odo Distance = Rally Distance x Factor." (https://www.rallywest.com/f/vnb/212/TSDschool_(1).pdf) |
| **Odometer check / odo leg / calibration zone** | First leg of nearly every rally, with generous time, used to compute the factor. CSCC: "a clearly defined odometer calibration zone...at least ten miles with intermediate mileage reference points and contains no checkpoints or route controls." (CSCC procedures.tex). Harvey Cain: "The first part of every rally is an Odometer check that allows each team to correlate their odometer with the odometer used by the rallymaster." (https://www.zone8.org/assets/docs/Rallying_for_beginners.pdf) |
| **Perfect time** | The rallymaster's calculated arrival time at a control. "You will receive a penalty point for each second you arrive at the checkpoint which deviates from the rallymaster's calculated perfect time." (San Diego Region GIs, https://sdrscca.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/San-Diego-Region-SCCA-Road-Rally-GENERAL-INSTRUCTIONS-1.pdf). SCCA Beginners Guide: "The rallymaster knows mathematically the time to travel the distance to the checkpoint, and your score is determined by how close you are to 'perfect.'" |
| **Time of day vs elapsed time** | SCCA TSD timing is time-of-day based: you receive an out-time (time of day) at each control and are scored on the time of day you arrive at the next. Harvey Cain: "You must set your watch to the official time of day, and if your watch is off by 30 seconds, you may still end up with a 50-point penalty." (zone8 PDF). CSCC: competitors "synchronize timepieces at the official time station"; start times are "in one-minute increments based on car number." (CSCC procedures.tex). Some apps/clubs display "24-hour actual and ideal time rather than elapsed rally clocks" (https://github.com/fantasais/TSD-Rally README). |
| **Hundredths vs seconds** | Clubs differ. CSCC: "each hundredth of a minute that you arrive early or late on each leg is worth one penalty point." (CSCC road-rally-rules.tex). San Diego Region: 1 point per second. (SDR GIs). "Some rallies are timed in hundredths of a minute (0.6 sec), while others are timed in seconds." (search summary of ABC Region / SDR generals). CSCC includes a seconds-to-hundredths conversion table (:01=0.01 ... :30=0.50 ... :60=1.00). (CSCC TSD_formulas.tex). Richta records arrival "to a resolution of .1 second" and scores "0.1 point per 0.1 second." (Alcan 5000 Richta setup, https://www.alcan5000.com/pdf/AlcanRichtaSetup.pdf) |
| **Pause** | PCA: "To delay a specified time at a named point or during passage of a specified distance. The pause time is added to the time required at a given average speed...The specified distance in which a pause is operative is a free zone." CSCC: "To pause for a specified time. The PAUSE time is added in the leg time calculations." |
| **Gain** | PCA: "To make up a specified time during passage of a specified distance. The gain time is subtracted from the time required..." CSCC: same, "The distance over which a GAIN is operative is free of checkpoints and route controls." |
| **Free zone** | PCA: "A part of the timed rally route in which there are no timing controls." (Speed still applies; you are simply guaranteed no checkpoint.) |
| **Transit zone / TRANSIT** | PCA: "A part of a rally route in which there are no timing controls and in which no specific speed need be maintained." CSCC: "Route instructions apply, but you may leave the route for rest or refueling stops. The TRANSIT time is added in the leg time calculations." |
| **Time allowance / time declaration (TA)** | A competitor-declared delay added to their perfect time, used instead of speeding to catch up. PCA Sacramento GIs: "If you are delayed...road blockage, slow traffic, getting lost, nature call, or any other reason; do not speed to try to make up the lost time" — take a time allowance. (https://dl.motorsportreg.com/77342833-0e55-40f3-8393-e03f2f94cef3/). CSCC: "A time declaration must be requested on the half-minute and cannot be claimed in even minutes," one per leg, max 19.50 minutes per event. (CSCC procedures.tex). Richta: pressing TA gives 10 s, then 20 s, then 30 s, then +1 min per press up to 19:30 (Alcan setup PDF) — another doc says up to 9.5 minutes (search summary; exact caps are event-configurable, UNVERIFIED which is default). |

### 1.2 Checkpoints / controls

| Term | Definition (source) |
|---|---|
| **Open control** | Manned checkpoint, visible, on the right side of the road. Harvey Cain: "At an open control you stop past the control vehicle so as not to block the official's line of sight to the timing line. You'll carefully walk back to the timing vehicle and receive the time you entered the control, the time you should leave the control (your 'out time') and information on the leg you just completed." (zone8 PDF). CSCC: "Pull up to the control station beyond the checkpoint sign"; scorecard comes back with "End leg time (in time) and Start leg time (out time)"; then proceed to the out marker and "time themselves out." Blocking a control entrance = max leg score (300). (CSCC procedures.tex) |
| **Closed / hidden control** | Not confirmed in the fetched sources; the NW Classic text says timing locations "are not given in the instructions" (https://www.nwclassicrally.org/How-TSDs-Work.html). SCCA rulebook distinguishes open vs closed controls — UNVERIFIED wording here because the rulebook PDF could not be fetched. |
| **DIYC (Do-It-Yourself Checkpoint)** | PCA Parade rules: "Do-It-Yourself legs (DIYCs) are legs that do not end in a manned checkpoint...except for an instruction that says 'End DIYC at...' You must give yourselves a departure time at the end of the DIYC...usually your arrival time plus one-minute and should be recorded as your out-time for the next leg." (https://mediaassets.pca.org/pages/microsites/parade2024/files/uploads/91052_pcrs_2024_tsd_rally.pdf). CSCC: record arrival "to the hundredth of a minute as your End Leg time...Record your next Start Leg time as exactly two (2.00) minutes after your End leg time." (CSCC glossary.tex). PCA: at least 5 controls, no more than half DIYC, never two DIYCs in a row. |
| **Passage control / route control** | CSCC "Route Control": stop, present scorecard, receive "a PAUSE for the time spent stopping at the control" plus any supplemental instructions — i.e., proves you passed a point without re-timing the leg. (CSCC procedures.tex). SCCA "passage control" wording UNVERIFIED. |
| **Out time / in time** | See open control row; the out time is the new zero for the next leg's timing. "You cannot make up time from previous checkpoints. When you leave a checkpoint you are basically starting from scratch." (PCA Sacramento GIs). "The score for each leg is independent of the other legs. You cannot make up for a late arrival at a checkpoint by arriving early by the same amount at the next." (ABC Region generals, https://cdn.connectsites.net/user_files/scca/downloads/000/067/543/RR%20TSD_2Page_Generals%2020230408.pdf) |
| **Early vs late penalties** | Symmetric: "You receive penalty points for both early and late arrivals – driving fast to arrive at a control early will only reward you with penalty points." (SDR GIs) |
| **Max** | Per-checkpoint cap. CSCC: 300 points (3+ minutes early or late). ABC Region: 200 points max per checkpoint. San Diego: 300. PCA (NTR/NCR): penalty "shall not exceed two (2) minutes." (sources as cited above) |
| **Richta GPS Checkpoints** | Smartphone app replacing manned controls: "monitors your car's location using GPS and records your arrival time at each control to a resolution of .1 second, computes your score, and displays it to you. At each timing control, the Richta Competitor App will ding and report your time and score." Tap the bar to see "a critique for that leg, including the exact mileage for that control" to adjust your mileage. No cell service needed, only GPS. (Alcan 5000 Richta setup PDF; Milwaukee Region event pages on motorsportreg.com) |
| **Critique** | Post-leg (or post-rally) sheet showing official mileages, perfect times and the correct course, so you can find out where you lost time. (Richta description above; Gary Starr's driver tips mention keeping "critiques" on the centerboard — http://www.indyscca.org/RallyFiles/TIPS4DRIVERS.pdf) |

### 1.3 Classes

- SCCA / general (search summary from https://oregonrally.com/roadrally/, https://www.wdcr-scca.org/road-rally, OVR Magazine): **Equipped** = "computers that integrate distance and time"; **Limited** = "cars equipped with auxiliary odometers capable of precise mileage measurement"; **Stock** = "'pencil and paper' timekeeping utilizing a basic calculator, tables and a stock odometer"; **SOP (seat-of-the-pants)** = no measuring equipment beyond what came on the car, no use of the car's computer for average speed, no calculating devices except paper and pencil; **Novice** = SOP equipment rules and neither contestant has competed in five or more TSD rallies.
- CSCC: Unequipped division (Novice "for beginners only", SOP "intermediate", Masters "experienced") — "pen, paper, timepiece(s) and stock odometer" only, no calculation devices; Equipped division: Limited (calculators/computers/GPS allowed but "does not receive a direct input from any distance-measuring device interfaced with the vehicle" — mileage entered by hand) and Unlimited. (CSCC appendix.tex)
- Scoring summary from Oregon Rally: "Each second is a point, therefore if you pass a control 10 seconds early or 10 seconds late, you are given 10 points. The lowest cumulative score wins." (https://oregonrally.com/roadrally/)

### 1.4 Route-following vocabulary (course rallies)

From CSCC glossary.tex unless noted (its full text was readable; SCCA definitions are similar but not identical — see http://drscca.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/road_rally_103.pdf and the SCCA rulebook):

- **General Instructions (GIs)** vs **Route Instructions (RIs)**: PCA: GIs are the "set of instructions specific to a given rally that defines the format and rally-specific rules to include CASTs, pauses, transits, all control locations, etc." RIs are the numbered turn-by-turn list. JCNA rule: "Don't Assume Anything - Be sure to read the General Instructions and don't hesitate to ask questions." (https://www.jcna.com/library/rally/chapter9.html)
- **Numbered / Note / Supplemental instructions**: Numbered RIs completed in ascending order; Notes are unnumbered and "active from its introduction until cancelled," superseding numbered instructions; Supplementals are handed out at checkpoints and must be done before resuming the numbered sequence. (CSCC route-instructions.tex). Route priority: emergency directions from officials > supplemental > note > numbered > main road. (CSCC rally-route.tex)
- **Main road rule / Main Road Determinants (MRDs)**: PCA: "The main road is the road that is most directly ahead." CSCC applies a strict hierarchy, each used only if it yields exactly one road: (1) ONTO/FOLLOW, (2) TOWARD, (3) PROTECTION ("the single road which leaves the intersection without a back-facing stop or yield sign"), (4) SURFACE (same paved/unpaved type), (5) STRAIGHT AS POSSIBLE (not applicable if equally straight or all exits >45 degrees), (6) LEFTMOST. (CSCC main-road-determinants.tex). SCCA also lists CURVE ARROWS ("the road leaving the intersection as shown by an official curve warning ahead...sign") and CENTERLINE STRIPE as main-road determinants (SCCA rulebook via search summary).
- **ONTO**: SCCA: "When placed onto a road by name or number, the rallyist is to continue on that road, however it may turn, until a subsequent course-directing Route Instruction is executed." (SCCA rulebook summary). CSCC: ONTO/FOLLOW makes that named road the main road "until a non-MBCU deviation occurs."
- **AT**: PCA: "'In the vicinity of', for actions that direct a course to travel; 'even with' for other actions, including CASTs, pauses, mileage, etc."
- **TOWARD**: follow signs in the direction of the landmark at each intersection.
- **T**: "An intersection having the shape of the letter T as approached from the base. It is not possible to execute the instruction S or the instruction STRAIGHT at a T." SCCA: "exactly three roads."
- **Y**: same shape logic, "approached from the base"; SCCA: "always regarded as approached from the bottom of the symbol."
- **SIGNAL**: CSCC: "multi-light traffic signal(s) which may be operating as blinker(s) or may be off." SCCA: "need not be operational...Count only one SIGNAL per intersection."
- **BLINKER**, **STOP** (official stop sign where you must legally stop), **YIELD**, **CROSSROAD** ("A crossing of two legal rally roads...may cross at any angle"), **SIDEROAD** (can TURN in only one direction), **OPP** (opportunity — a possible deviation that is paved and/or named as indicated).
- **L / R / S / TURN**: L = "Leftmost deviation of any angle off the main road"; S = "straightest deviation...within 45 degrees of straight ahead"; TURN = "deviation from the main road in the only direction possible...cannot be executed if an instruction to go straight would take the contestant on the same route."
- **ONEOF**: not in the CSCC glossary; its SCCA/Detroit meaning (one of several named references in a list, execute at whichever appears) is UNVERIFIED here because the SCCA/DRSCCA PDFs could not be fetched. CSCC's analogous construct is **OR**: execute one half of the instruction, "the reference point of the two possibilities which is located first determines which half."
- **ITIS** ("if there is such"): execute only if its action point appears before the next NRI's action point; otherwise skip it and consider it completed.
- **MBCU** ("may be considered unnecessary"): a labeled deviation that may be executed to follow the main road.
- **AFTER / BEFORE**: first intersection beyond / last intersection prior to and in sight of the referenced sign.
- **API** ("after previous NRI"): at the indicated point beyond completion of the previous instruction.
- **OBSERVE**: "To visually note and pass a sign or landmark."
- **SOL**: "Sign must be on left. May not always be given."
- **Action point / Reference point / Landmark**: Action points are where instructions execute (an intersection where you turn, a main-road intersection with official mileage, a stated distance/duration). Reference points are signs, landmarks or official mileage that mark the start of an instruction. Landmarks appear "in upper case (all capital letters) not in quotation marks." (CSCC route-instructions.tex)
- **Alpines**: intersection diagrams; "The dot...represents the road on which the contestant enters" and "an arrowhead indicates the road on which the contestant leaves"; if multiple legal routes, take the shortest. (CSCC)
- **Tulip diagrams**: the European/Indian equivalent of alpines (ball = you enter, arrow = you exit). CVR PCA: a good navigator "can read tulip maps and make calculations without getting flustered." (CVR basics PDF). Team-BHP's TSD explainer also uses tulips. (https://www.team-bhp.com/forum/indian-motorsport/168825-explained-tsd-time-speed-distance-rallying.html)
- **Sign conventions**: CSCC: glossary words have their special meaning "only...when they appear in upper case (all capital letters) and not in quotation marks"; lower-case words mean the dictionary definition. Signs are shown "in upper case within quotation marks" matching "spelling, punctuation, detail, and shape of symbols"; any prominent part of a sign may be quoted but "no intervening words...will be skipped, split, combined, or reordered"; house numbers, mailboxes, graffiti, temporary posters and signs on vehicles are never used; "A speed control sign will be used only when the sign controls the legal speed limit of the road you are currently travelling upon." (CSCC signs.tex)
- **Not visible from the course / "visible from the course"**: standard GI language that only signs readable from the rally route count; exact SCCA wording UNVERIFIED (rulebook not fetchable).
- **Speed-change execution point**: CSCC: speed changes at intersections "execute upon entering intersections"; at references, "as you pass by and are even with the reference"; "Temporary speed changes revert after specified time or distance." (CSCC route-instructions.tex)
- **Course markers**: CSCC uses club-initial markers — green = on course, orange = off course; "DNE" marks a non-rally road; numbered correction markers mean "execute that instruction before continuing." (CSCC rally-route.tex)
- **Rally road**: "A rally road is a public through road"; private, closed, dead-end roads and roads requiring illegal turns are excluded; "No uninstructed U-turn(s) will be necessary to follow the rally route." (CSCC rally-route.tex)
- **Leg**: the timed segment between two controls, scored independently (NW Classic; ABC Region).
- **Critique / "trap"**: course rallies include deliberate traps where a careless reading sends you off course; the SCCA Beginners Guide says course rallies have "possible off-course scenarios and traps." Tour rallies have none.

## 2. The math

### 2.1 Core formulas as taught

- Fundamental identity: "DISTANCE = TIME X SPEED," hence Time = Distance/Speed and Speed = Distance/Time. (JCNA Chapter 4, https://www.jcna.com/library/rally/chapter4.html)
- Minutes form used by every club: **Time (minutes) = 60 x Distance (miles) / Speed (mph)**. CSCC: "Time = (60 X Distance) / Speed"; example: CAST 30 over 2 miles = 4 minutes. (https://github.com/jhelwig/cscc-tsd-rally-rules TSD_formulas.tex). Rally West: "Time = Distance x 60 / Average Speed" (and the km version). (https://www.rallywest.com/f/vnb/212/TSDschool_(1).pdf). Team-BHP: "60 x (Miles to travel at average speed) divided by official average speed = number of minutes," e.g. 3.1 miles at 30 mph = 60 x 3.1 / 30 = 6.2 min = 6 min 12 s. (https://www.team-bhp.com/forum/indian-motorsport/168825-explained-tsd-time-speed-distance-rallying.html)
- Warning taught alongside it: decimal minutes must be converted to min:sec (0.2 min = 12 s, not 20 s). "You cannot simply divide distance by speed in decimal format—you must convert results into hours, minutes, and seconds format." (Team-BHP explainer, search summary)
- **Rate (minutes per mile) = 60 / Speed.** "TSD navigation strategies are based on knowing the rate (minutes-per-mile) at your assigned speed." (https://roadrallyhandbook.com/rrtable.htm). JCNA: at 30 mph "in 60 minutes you would cover exactly 30 miles, one-half mile per minute, or two minutes per mile"; build a table: 0.1 mi = 0:12, 0.5 mi = 1:00, 1.0 mi = 2:00. (JCNA Ch. 4)
- **Seconds per mile = 3600 / Speed**; seconds per tenth = 360 / Speed. MGA Guru's description of the "sharp navigator": 42 mph is "60/42 minutes per mile, or 42/60 miles per minute, or 6/42x60 seconds for each 1/10 mile." (https://mail.mgaguru.com/mgtech/competition/comp130.htm)
- **Gain formula** (CSCC): "Speed = 60 / ((Time at stated CAST - GAIN time) / Distance)"; example: CAST 30 with GAIN 1 minute over 2 miles -> 60 / ((4 - 1)/2) = 40 mph. (CSCC TSD_formulas.tex)
- **Pause**: simply add the pause time to the computed leg time (PCA glossary; CSCC glossary). Transit time is added likewise (CSCC).
- **Odometer factor applied to speed**: corrected speed = CAST x factor (CSCC); e.g. factor .96, CAST 40 -> drive an indicated 38.4 (CVR/PCA SDR say "38"). Applied to distance: corrected odo reading = official mileage x factor; e.g. factor .96, instruction at 2.00 -> execute at 1.92. (https://www.pcasdr.org/rally)
- **Cone's Factors / Stimson's Rallye Factors**: published tables that give minutes-per-mile at an indicated average speed *and* fold in odometer error ("Norman Cone's Corrected Minutes Per Mile Factors or Stimson's Rallye Factors"). (JCNA Ch. 4). The **speed factor method**: multiply each official mileage by the factor (min/mile) for the current CAST to get the minutes due at that mileage; sum with pauses. Stock-class navigators pre-print such factor tables (roadrallyhandbook.com/rrtable.htm; https://www.goss.com/rrbkn.htm "TSD Navigation Tables").
- Historical tool: the **Curta** mechanical calculator was the classic rally computer for this multiplication (https://rallyracingnews.com/manuals/curtaman.html — not fetchable; cited from search listing).

### 2.3 Mental-math tricks recommended by experienced navigators (collected)

1. **Know your rate, not the formula.** Convert every CAST into minutes-per-mile (or seconds-per-tenth) once, then multiply by distance. (roadrallyhandbook.com/rrtable.htm; JCNA Ch. 4)
2. **Build a per-speed table of tenths and whole miles** before the rally ("make up a chart or table showing time and distance for tenths of a mile and for integral miles, making calculations so easy you could do them in your head"). (JCNA Ch. 4)
3. **Seconds-per-tenth shortcut**: sec per 0.1 mi = 360 / mph. 36 mph -> 10 s per tenth; 40 -> 9; 45 -> 8; 30 -> 12; 24 -> 15; 20 -> 18; 60 -> 6. (arithmetic; the 36-mph "100 seconds per mile" case is the classic one cited in the task brief — UNVERIFIED as a quote, but the arithmetic is exact)
4. **Hundredths-of-a-minute shortcut**: hundredths per mile = 100 x 60 / mph = 6000/mph. 30 mph -> 2.00 min/mile = 200 hundredths; 40 -> 1.50 min = 150; 48 -> 1.25 = 125; 50 -> 1.20 = 120; 60 -> 1.00 = 100. Hundredths-based clubs (CSCC, Detroit) make this trivially additive because there is no base-60 carry. (CSCC scoring in hundredths; the seconds<->hundredths table in CSCC TSD_formulas.tex)
5. **Reciprocal trick**: miles per minute = mph/60 (30 mph = 0.5 mi/min; 45 = 0.75; 60 = 1.0; 36 = 0.6; 24 = 0.4; 20 = 0.333). Multiply elapsed minutes by this to know where you *should* be. (MGA Guru description of the "sharp navigator")
6. **Carry the error, not the total** ("carrying error" method): the navigator "writes the error in the right-hand margin of the instructions, and adjusts the next hold time accordingly," refining continuously rather than making dramatic speed changes. (JCNA Ch. 9, via search summary)
7. **Factor-correct the route book once, not every mile**: beginners "find it more logical to apply 'corrections' to the official mileages wherever they appear in the route instructions by writing in what their odometers will read at those given points." (https://www.pcasdr.org/rally). Rally West: apply the factor "to all the distances in the route book to come up with corrected odo readings for the car."
8. **Drive a hair over CAST, bleed off before the control**: GRM forum veterans' rule of thumb — "Drive around 3 mph faster than the designated speed to make up for stop signs, etc." (https://grassrootsmotorsports.com/forum/grm/learn-me-tsd-rallying/4480/page1/). The rallymaster's perfect time assumes continuous average speed, so every stop sign costs ~5–10 s that must be recovered.
9. **"The 1/3 rule"**: the task brief asks about a "1/3 rule"; no source reachable in this session states it. Possible meanings (UNVERIFIED): (a) the rule of thumb that a full stop-and-go costs roughly the time of the stop plus about one-third of the decel+accel time; (b) "take back a third of your error per mile" to avoid over-correction. Treat as UNVERIFIED.
10. **Checkpoint-as-reset**: the only time you must be exactly right is at the control, so do your fine correction in the last mile. Gary Starr: "the only place you must be on time is at the control." (http://www.indyscca.org/RallyFiles/TIPS4NAVIGATORS.pdf)
11. **Half-minute declarations**: because CSCC time declarations are only on the half-minute, round deliberately (CSCC procedures.tex). Richta TAs are 10/20/30 s then whole minutes (Alcan Richta setup).
12. **Stop-start compensation callouts**: at a pause/stop the navigator "can count off the hold seconds out loud" and the driver calls "Mark!" as the speedo passes the halfway speed during decel/accel, so the lost time can be estimated as roughly the time spent below half-speed. (JCNA Ch. 1, via search summary of https://www.jcna.com/library/rally/chapter1.html)

### 2.4 Worked examples (computed; consistent with the cited formulas)

- *Where should we be at instruction 7?* Out-time 10:32:00. Instruction 7 is at official mile 4.60 with CAST 36 throughout. Time = 4.60 x 100 s = 460 s = 7:40 -> due at 10:39:40. With a factor of 0.97, your odometer should read 4.46 at that point.
- *Pause then CAST change:* CAST 30 for 2.0 mi (4:00), PAUSE 0:30, then CAST 40 for 3.0 mi (4:30). Leg time = 4:00 + 0:30 + 4:30 = 9:00. Over the 5.0 miles that is 108 s/mile average, i.e. you will be "on time" overall even though you never drove 33.3 mph.
- *Late by 20 s with 2 miles to the control at CAST 40 (90 s/mile = 180 s needed):* to be on time you need 160 s for 2 miles = 80 s/mile = 45 mph; i.e. CAST + 5 mph for 2 miles. General rule: new speed = 60 x D / (60 x D / CAST - late_minutes) (same algebra as the CSCC GAIN formula).
- *Convert seconds to hundredths:* 18 s = 0.30 min; 45 s = 0.75 min; 7 s = 0.12 min (CSCC table: :01=0.01 ... :30=0.50 ... :60=1.00).

### 2.5 Stopwatch vs clock

- Time-of-day method (standard SCCA/PCA/CSCC): sync your watch to the official clock at the start; at each control you get an out-time; compute due times as *clock* times and compare against the clock. Harvey Cain's warning about a 30-second watch error costing 50 points shows why sync matters. (zone8 PDF; CSCC procedures.tex)
- Stopwatch / elapsed method (common for novices and in GRM advice): start a stopwatch at the out-time (or at each CAST change) and compare elapsed time to computed elapsed time. GRM "learn me": the basic equipment is "a stop watch." Downside: one missed restart loses the leg; upside: no base-time arithmetic.
- Many apps show both "actual" and "ideal" time-of-day (fantasais/TSD-Rally README) or "Ideal Time vs Actual Time deviations" (chaitanyajalan README). Richta shows arrival time and score at each control (Alcan PDF).

## 3. Common newbie mistakes (every one found, with source)

Course / reading mistakes
1. **Getting lost / going off course at all.** Universal #1. JCNA rule 1: "Don't Get Lost - Concentrate on the route first, on time second, as time lost while off course may never be made up and can bring the greatest penalty." (https://www.jcna.com/library/rally/chapter9.html). GRM: "On time and off course is way worse than on course but off time." Gary Starr: "it is meaningless to keep time on the wrong road." (TIPS4NAVIGATORS.pdf)
2. **Not reading (or skimming) the General Instructions.** JCNA: "Don't Assume Anything - Be sure to read the General Instructions and don't hesitate to ask questions, as many penalties can be avoided." (JCNA Ch. 9)
3. **Following the car ahead.** JCNA: "Don't assume that the car(s) in front of you know where they're going, and remember that rallymasters may have the route double back upon itself." (JCNA Ch. 9)
4. **Checking off instructions in advance / losing your place in the list.** JCNA: "Checking off items in advance is the surest way to miss that instruction altogether." Gary Starr: the *driver*, not the navigator, should check off route instructions to keep attention and avoid mistakes. (JCNA Ch. 9; TIPS4DRIVERS.pdf)
5. **Not re-reading new instruction sheets handed out at controls/breaks.** JCNA: "Rallymasters may issue new instructions after breaks or special checkpoints." (Ch. 9)
6. **Driving past an intersection while unsure, then backing up.** Gary Starr: "Never driving past an intersection without being sure; slowing down or stopping before the intersection rather than backing up, as the latter wastes significantly more time." (TIPS4DRIVERS.pdf)
7. **Misapplying glossary words vs plain words** (e.g., treating a lower-case "left" like a glossary L, or a quoted sign as a landmark). CSCC rule: upper-case unquoted words carry the special definition; quoted upper-case = sign text. (CSCC glossary.tex, signs.tex)
8. **Not knowing the main-road rule** and therefore turning at a "T" or stop when the instruction says nothing. (CSCC MRDs; PCA "main road is the road most directly ahead")
9. **Using the wrong class of sign** (mailbox, house number, sign on a vehicle). (CSCC signs.tex)

Timing / math mistakes
10. **Skipping the odometer check / not calibrating.** CVR PCA: "You shouldn't worry about doing the math on your first few rallies...but DO THE ODOMETER CHECK." (CVR basics PDF)
11. **Applying the factor to distance but not speed (or vice versa).** CVR/PCA SDR: "You should also adjust your speeds by the same factor." (pcasdr.org/rally)
12. **Dividing miles by mph and treating the decimal as minutes:seconds.** Team-BHP: convert decimal minutes to min:sec. (Team-BHP explainer)
13. **Forgetting a PAUSE or a GAIN.** The PCA glossary defines them precisely because they are where legs are lost; Gary Starr recommends highlighting pauses in their own colour (TIPS4DRIVERS: highlight "speed changes, pauses, restarts, controls, transit zones, and quoted signs" in different colours).
14. **Executing a CAST at the wrong point** (at the sign vs at the intersection). CSCC: at intersections, speed changes execute "upon entering"; at references "as you pass by and are even with." (CSCC route-instructions.tex)
15. **Not synchronizing the watch to official time.** Harvey Cain: 30 s watch error -> ~50-point penalty. (zone8 PDF)
16. **Starting the leg clock at the wrong moment / not restarting at the out-time.** CSCC: "Drivers then proceed to the out marker and time themselves out." DIYC out-time must be arrival + exactly 1.00 (PCA) or 2.00 (CSCC) minutes — getting the convention wrong costs a full minute. (CSCC glossary.tex; PCA Parade rules)
17. **Trying to carry error across a checkpoint.** "You cannot make up time from previous checkpoints. When you leave a checkpoint you are basically starting from scratch." (PCA Sacramento GIs); ABC Region: legs are independent.
18. **Speeding to catch up (and then arriving early).** SDR GIs: "driving fast to arrive at a control early will only reward you with penalty points." PCA Sacramento: "do not speed to try to make up the lost time" — declare a time allowance instead. CSCC: a moving-violation citation = automatic DQ. (CSCC procedures.tex)
19. **Over-correcting** — lurching between too fast and too slow. The JCNA "carry the error" method exists to prevent this (JCNA Ch. 9 summary). Rebelle Rally's head scoring judge's rookie list also warns against chasing the number (https://www.rebellerally.com/top-ten-advice-for-rookies-from-head-scoring-judge-chrissie-beavis/ — content UNVERIFIED, page not fetchable).
20. **Driving exactly CAST and not allowing for stop signs / traffic.** GRM: drive ~3 mph over to absorb stops. (GRM learn-me thread)
21. **Blocking the control entrance** (stopping at the sign instead of past the timing car) — CSCC gives max leg score 300 for it; Harvey Cain: stop past the control vehicle so as not to block the official's line of sight. (CSCC procedures.tex; zone8 PDF)
22. **Assuming the route book has all time/distance info.** Brian Baker (first TSD, Sports Car Market): discovered "much of the time/distance information would not be provided in the route book" and realized "This might be harder than we thought." (https://www.sportscarmarket.com/blogs/alex-scm-staff/the-monte-shelton-nw-classic-my-first-tsd)
23. **Misreading the odometer / trusting a stock odometer's tenths.** GRM "TSD Rally - no odometer" thread: an odometer or GPS "becomes important when deciding between unmarked roads that are close together." (https://grassrootsmotorsports.com/forum/off-topic-discussion/tsd-rally-no-odometer/72873/page1/). Stock odometers only resolve to 0.1 mi, which is why Limited/Equipped classes exist.
24. **Wrong unit assumptions (km vs miles)** — PopovMP's calculator exists because "my car is in miles, but the Road Book is in km." (https://github.com/PopovMP/regularity-test-calculator)

Team / human-factor mistakes
25. **Arguing / getting flustered.** GRM: "Get a Navigator you won't kill when you get angry"; "Communication is key—speak clearly to each other, and don't get flustered"; "You need patience and a sense of humor. Laugh at yourselves." (GRM learn-me thread)
26. **Navigator head-down in the math while the car goes off course.** Gary Starr: navigators should help with course following — "four eyes are always better than two." (TIPS4NAVIGATORS.pdf)
27. **Driver not announcing decisions**, so the navigator can't catch a wrong turn. Gary Starr: drivers should "call out loud the main road and where they are going at every intersection." (TIPS4DRIVERS.pdf summary)
28. **Hurrying through intersections to stay on time.** Gary Starr: take "as much time as necessary to figure out intersections, even if it means going down 2 minutes." (TIPS4NAVIGATORS.pdf)
29. **Trying to do the math on the first rally instead of staying on course.** Rally master Simon Levear told first-timer Brian Baker not to "worry about times and should just focus on not getting lost." (SCM blog). GRM: "Don't sweat the math for your first rally." CVR: "This is 'seat of the pants'."
30. **Poor cockpit setup** — no clipboard/centerboard, no map light, instructions sliding around. Gary Starr recommends a 1/4" Plexiglas centerboard between the seats holding routes, critiques and main-road determinants. (TIPS4DRIVERS.pdf)
31. **Not using the critique** to find where time was lost (Richta leg critique shows exact control mileage so you can "make minor adjustments to your mileage"). (Alcan Richta setup PDF)

## 4. How teams communicate and divide labour

### 4.1 Division of labour
- NW Classic: three jobs (driving, navigating, timekeeping), two people; "the navigator typically also keeps time and the driver must contribute to keeping on course." (https://www.nwclassicrally.org/How-TSDs-Work.html)
- Gary Starr (navigator tips): "Stay on course first!" — it is "meaningless to keep time on the wrong road"; help the driver follow the course ("four eyes are always better than two"); keep a log; make periodic time checks easy on yourself; take your time at intersections; "the only place you must be on time is at the control." (http://www.indyscca.org/RallyFiles/TIPS4NAVIGATORS.pdf)
- Gary Starr (driver tips): driver checks off each instruction as executed (keeps the driver engaged, prevents skips); driver calls out the main road and the intended action at every intersection ("I'm going to do a left at T to follow the main road"); driver reminds the navigator when a speed change is coming up; at a control the *driver* fills out the scorecard while the *navigator* sets up the next leg; colour-highlight speed changes, pauses, restarts, controls, transit zones and quoted signs. (http://www.indyscca.org/RallyFiles/TIPS4DRIVERS.pdf)
- JCNA: "The navigator's job is to keep tabs on where the team is in order to keep the driver on course and on time, and it boils down to good communications between the driver and navigator." (https://www.jcna.com/library/rally/chapter1.html)
- CVR PCA: want "a logical-minded navigator partner, one who can read tulip maps and make calculations without getting flustered." (CVR basics PDF)
- SCCA Beginners Guide agenda explicitly covers "team communication, tips for drivers & navigators." (SCCA 2021 handout)

### 4.2 What the navigator says, and when (synthesised from the above; phrasing is illustrative)
- Reading ahead: the next instruction, its reference, and the official/corrected mileage: "Next is 14: right at STOP, official 6.30, our odo 6.11."
- Speed changes with lead time: "CAST 40 coming up at the signal... at the intersection... now 40." CSCC rules define *where* the change takes effect (entering the intersection / even with the reference), so the call must be timed to that point. (CSCC route-instructions.tex)
- Time checks at known mileages: "At 5.0 we should read 10:41:30 ... we're 8 seconds late."
- Pause handling: count the hold aloud ("twelve thousand, 'leven thousand, ten thousand...") while the driver calls "Mark!" at half-speed on decel/accel. (JCNA Ch. 1)
- Confirmation of the driver's intersection call: driver says "Left at T, main road," navigator confirms "Agreed, no instruction active" or "No — 15 says straight."
- Control approach: "Control possible any time now, we're 5 early — slow to 33 for the next half mile."
- After the control: read out-time back ("Out at 10:52:00, next leg starts CAST 36 from the out marker"), re-zero/record the odometer, resume.

### 4.3 What the driver does
- Drives the indicated (factor-corrected) speed smoothly; treats stop signs/traffic as time debt to be repaid gradually (GRM "3 mph over" habit).
- Calls every intersection decision aloud; never passes an uncertain intersection (stop short rather than back up). (TIPS4DRIVERS)
- Watches for signs/landmarks the navigator is looking for; reads sign text aloud in quoted form.
- Handles the scorecard at controls; obeys all traffic laws (citation = DQ in CSCC). (CSCC procedures.tex)

## 5. Recovery techniques

1. **Off course**: stop early rather than late (TIPS4DRIVERS). Return to the last point you were certain of and re-execute from the last confirmed instruction; course rallies often place markers — CSCC: green = on course, orange = off course, numbered correction markers mean "execute that instruction before continuing." (CSCC rally-route.tex). Do not try to recover the lost minutes by speed; take a time allowance/declaration instead (PCA Sacramento GIs; CSCC half-minute declarations up to 19.50 min/event; Richta TA button 10/20/30 s then minutes).
2. **Lost the clock / missed a restart**: fall back to time-of-day math from the last out-time (which is written on the scorecard), or from the last time check you logged — Gary Starr's advice to "keep a log" exists precisely for this. (TIPS4NAVIGATORS)
3. **Late at a checkpoint**: take the penalty, read the out-time, and start the next leg clean — "When you leave a checkpoint you are basically starting from scratch" (PCA Sacramento GIs). Legs are scored independently (ABC Region generals).
4. **Early approaching a checkpoint**: slow down or, where legal and not blocking anyone, stop short of the control and roll in at the perfect time — being early is penalised exactly like being late (SDR GIs). Never stop *at* the control sign or block the entrance (CSCC: max score).
5. **Unsure whether a control has passed (hidden controls)**: continue on time; the leg ends only where you are actually timed. Free zones are the only places you are guaranteed no control (PCA glossary).
6. **After any control**: read the critique if provided (Richta shows exact control mileage) and nudge your factor or mileage accordingly; set up the next leg (navigator) while the driver fills the card (TIPS4DRIVERS); time out from the out marker at the out-time (CSCC procedures.tex).
7. **DIYC**: record arrival to the hundredth, add the fixed 1.00 (PCA) / 2.00 (CSCC) minutes for your new out-time, time yourself out. (PCA Parade rules; CSCC glossary)
8. **Blown pause/gain**: recompute the remaining leg: required speed = 60 x D / (minutes remaining), capped by legal limits; if that exceeds the legal/safe speed, declare time instead.

## 7. Implications for a training simulator (prioritised)

**P0 — must teach and simulate (the things every source says lose legs)**
1. **Stay-on-course first**: a route-instruction engine with glossary semantics (upper-case glossary words vs quoted signs vs lower-case prose), main-road rule hierarchy (ONTO/TOWARD/PROTECTION/SURFACE/STRAIGHT/LEFTMOST, plus SCCA curve-arrow and centerline variants), T/Y/SIGNAL/STOP/CROSSROAD/SIDEROAD intersection types, ITIS/OR/MBCU/AFTER/BEFORE/API constructs, and notes/supplementals that override numbered RIs. Score going off course as its own failure mode and show the "trap" afterwards (critique).
2. **Odometer check leg and factor**: simulate a car odometer that disagrees with official mileage (e.g. 0.95–1.04), force the user to compute the factor, then apply it to *both* distances and speeds; penalise the classic "forgot to factor the speed" error by showing the resulting drift.
3. **CAST arithmetic drills**: 60xD/S, min/mile and sec/tenth tables, decimal-minute to min:sec conversion, hundredths vs seconds, cumulative due-time at each instruction; adaptive drills on the common speeds (20–60) and the 36-mph/100-s anchor.
4. **Pauses, gains, transits and free zones**: insert them into legs; make the user add/subtract them; show the leg time before and after; model transit as "no speed, time added."
5. **Checkpoint procedure and leg independence**: open controls with in-time/out-time, restart of timing at the out marker, no carry-over of error between legs, symmetric early/late penalties, per-leg max (configurable 200/300 or 2 min), DIYC with fixed +1.00/+2.00 restarts, Richta-style GPS controls that "ding" and show the score.
6. **Time-of-day discipline**: a synced master clock, a watch that can be deliberately offset to show what a 30-second error costs, both stopwatch (elapsed) and clock (ToD) workflows.

**P1 — should simulate (realism that drives the mistakes above)**
7. **Stop signs, traffic lights, slow traffic** that steal 5–15 s each, so the user learns to run slightly over CAST and to bleed time before the control rather than speeding; allow a time allowance/declaration mechanic (half-minute or 10/20/30-s/minute increments) and show how it is folded into perfect time.
8. **Over-correction dynamics**: a speed/accel model where chasing the error produces oscillation; teach "carry the error" and the gain-formula speed (60 / ((T_cast - gain)/D)) with legal-speed caps.
9. **Two-role cockpit**: optional driver/navigator split (two players or AI partner) with scripted callouts — instruction read-ahead, speed-change countdown, intersection confirmations, pause hold counts, "Mark!" at half-speed — and a mode where the AI driver takes wrong turns unless the navigator confirms.
10. **Hidden vs open controls**: random control placement outside free zones so the user cannot "save it for the end."
11. **Classes**: SOP/Novice mode (stock 0.1-mi odometer, pencil only), Stock (tables/calculator), Limited (manual-input computer), Equipped (live computer) — same route, different tooling, to show why the classes exist.

**P2 — nice to have**
12. **Critique screen** after each leg: perfect time, your time, where the error accumulated (per instruction), off-course excursions, and the factor you should have used.
13. **Sign realism**: signs quoted exactly, decoys (mailboxes, vehicle signs, speed signs on the wrong road) that must be ignored; "not visible from the course" cases.
14. **Course-rally traps** (double-backs, ONEOF/OR ambiguity, curve-arrow main roads) vs **tour-rally mode** with no traps — matching the SCCA tour/course distinction so rookies start in tour mode.
15. **Units toggle** (miles/km, seconds/hundredths) to mirror club differences.
16. **Cockpit ergonomics tutorial** (clipboard, highlighter colours per Gary Starr, red map light) as onboarding content rather than simulation.

Open items flagged UNVERIFIED in this document and worth confirming against the SCCA RoadRally Rules PDF when it is reachable: exact SCCA definitions of ONEOF, open vs closed controls, passage controls, "visible from the course" wording, and the "1/3 rule."
