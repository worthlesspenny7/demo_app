## 0. What a TSD rally is (one-paragraph framing)

- SCCA's framing: RoadRally is run on open public roads at speeds below the legal limit; two-person teams compete at "reading route instructions carefully, following the route, and maintaining an assigned average speed." For TSD events, penalty points are assigned for arriving at a checkpoint early or late, lowest score wins. (https://racer.com/2016/04/06/how-to-start-racing-scca-roadrally/ ; https://www.scca.com/announcements/291-roadrally-beginners-guide-to-tsd-zoom-get-together)
- Northwest Classic Rally: a TSD rally "is not a race; it is a precision driving competition"; the basis is "the accurate application of complex rules under time pressure to follow a designated route in a set period of time." Written instructions are "somewhat 'coded' by use of abbreviations or graphics." Teams start at one-minute intervals and are timed at locations not given in the instructions; a rally has multiple separately-scored "legs" of roughly 5–20 miles. Penalties accrue for early/late arrival and for going off course. There are three roles in the car (driving, navigating, timekeeping) but only two people, so "the navigator typically also keeps time and the driver must contribute to keeping on course." (https://www.nwclassicrally.org/How-TSDs-Work.html)
- Cascade Sports Car Club (CSCC) rulebook: "the object of a time-speed-distance road rally is to complete the rally route on course and as close to on time as possible." Being on course = following the rallymaster's intended route; being on time = arriving at checkpoints when the rallymaster intended. (https://github.com/jhelwig/cscc-tsd-rally-rules , file road-rally-rules.tex)
- "Official time -- intended or allowed time -- is the amount of time it takes to drive the intended route at the average speeds specified in the route instructions." CSCC measures the route in thousandths of a mile between speed changes and computes allowed time to the nearest hundredth of a minute. (CSCC road-rally-rules.tex, same repo)
- SCCA Beginners Guide (2021) distinguishes two TSD flavors: **Tour rallies** are "designed so you will not get lost," no tricks, the challenge is to stay on time; **Course rallies** require logic and observation to follow the route, with possible off-course scenarios and traps, so the challenge is twofold (follow the course AND stay on time). (https://cdn.connectsites.net/user_files/scca/downloads/000/057/386/Beginners%20Guide%20to%20TSD%20Presentation%20Handout.pdf)
- Minimum kit per SCCA Beginners Guide: a driver and a navigator; clipboard; a timing device displaying seconds or 1/100th of a minute; odometer; phone apps; pens/pencils/highlighters/sticky notes. (same handout)
- Minimum kit per Grassroots Motorsports forum veterans: "a stopwatch, a car with a working odometer, a pen, a highlighter, and a good navigator"; a map light is near-mandatory; use a RED light at night to preserve the driver's night vision. (https://grassrootsmotorsports.com/forum/grm/learn-me-tsd-rallying/4480/page1/)

## 1. Core concepts and vocabulary

Terminology below is drawn from (a) the PCA Parade Rally Glossary (https://mediaassets.pca.org/pages/microsites/parade2024/files/uploads/44736_pcars_2025_tsd_rally_appendix_vii.pdf), (b) the SCCA RoadRally Rules (https://cdn.connectsites.net/user_files/scca/downloads/000/073/902/2025%20RoadRally%20Rulebook.pdf), (c) the Cascade Sports Car Club rulebook glossary (https://github.com/jhelwig/cscc-tsd-rally-rules/blob/master/glossary.tex — the one primary rulebook whose full text was directly readable in this environment), (d) Detroit Region "Road Rally 103 – Glossary of Terms" (http://drscca.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/road_rally_103.pdf), and regional General Instructions. Wording differs slightly between clubs; the simulator should treat the *event's* General Instructions as authoritative.

### 1.1 Speed and time vocabulary

| Term | Definition (source) |
|---|---|
| **CAST** | "Change Average Speed To" — the preceding average speed is discontinued and replaced by the value after CAST (PCA glossary; CSCC glossary: "Change average speed to."). SCCA Beginners Guide: average speed "will likely change many times throughout the rally, using CAST." Example from PCA San Diego: "at mile 12, you'll CAST 25 MPH." (https://www.pcasdr.org/rally) |
| **CSU / CSD** | CSCC: "Change average speed up by the amount indicated" / "Change average speed down by the amount indicated" (relative CASTs). (CSCC glossary.tex) |
| **Official mileage (OM)** | Distances in the route instructions as measured by the rallymaster's odometer. CSCC: an instruction with OM "must be executed at that mileage provided that: The reference is correct and An appropriate action point exists"; competitors need only determine mileage to 0.1 mile. (CSCC route-instructions.tex) |
| **Odometer correction factor** | Your odometer / official odometer over the odometer-check leg. CVR PCA: "Factor = (your car's mileage) divided by (the Official Mileage)"; then "multiply all distances written in your route instructions by the Factor." Also "adjust your speeds by the same factor; if the given speed is 40 mph you would drive at 38 (.96 x 40)." (https://cvrpca.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/2013_TSD_Rally_Basics.pdf ; same example at https://www.pcasdr.org/rally). CSCC formula: factor = "Your odometer Miles / Official miles"; corrected speed = CAST x factor; "when odometer readings fall short of official distances, drivers must reduce speed...when odometer readings exceed official distances, increased speed is necessary." (CSCC TSD_formulas.tex). Rally West novice school: "Your Distance / Rally Distance = Odo Correction Factor"; "Odo Distance = Rally Distance x Factor." (https://www.rallywest.com/f/vnb/212/TSDschool_(1).pdf) |
| **Odometer check / odo leg / calibration zone** | First leg of nearly every rally, with generous time, used to compute the factor. CSCC: "a clearly defined odometer calibration zone...at least ten miles with intermediate mileage reference points and contains no checkpoints or route controls." (CSCC procedures.tex). Harvey Cain: "The first part of every rally is an Odometer check that allows each team to correlate their odometer with the odometer used by the rallymaster." (https://www.zone8.org/assets/docs/Rallying_for_beginners.pdf) |
| **Perfect time** | The rallymaster's calculated arrival time at a control. "You will receive a penalty point for each second you arrive at the checkpoint which deviates from the rallymaster's calculated perfect time." (San Diego Region GIs, https://sdrscca.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/San-Diego-Region-SCCA-Road-Rally-GENERAL-INSTRUCTIONS-1.pdf). SCCA Beginners Guide: "The rallymaster knows mathematically the time to travel the distance to the checkpoint, and your score is determined by how close you are to 'perfect.'" |
| **Time of day vs elapsed time** | SCCA TSD timing is time-of-day based: you receive an out-time (time of day) at each control and are scored on the time of day you arrive at the next. Harvey Cain: "You must set your watch to the official time of day, and if your watch is off by 30 seconds, you may still end up with a 50-point penalty." (zone8 PDF). CSCC: competitors "synchronize timepieces at the official time station"; start times are "in one-minute increments based on car number." (CSCC procedures.tex). Some apps/clubs display "24-hour actual and ideal time rather than elapsed rally clocks" (https://github.com/fantasais/TSD-Rally README). |
| **Hundredths vs seconds** | Clubs differ. CSCC: "each hundredth of a minute that you arrive early or late on each leg is worth one penalty point." (CSCC road-rally-rules.tex). San Diego Region: 1 point per second. (SDR GIs). "Some rallies are timed in hundredths of a minute (0.6 sec), while others are timed in seconds." (search summary of ABC Region / SDR generals). CSCC includes a seconds-to-hundredths conversion table (:01=0.01 ... :30=0.50 ... :60=1.00). (CSCC TSD_formulas.tex). Richta records arrival "to a resolution of .1 second" and scores "0.1 point per 0.1 second." (Alcan 5000 Richta setup, https://www.alcan5000.com/pdf/AlcanRichtaSetup.pdf) |
| **Pause** | PCA: "To delay a specified time at a named point or during passage of a specified distance. The pause time is added to the time required at a given average speed...The specified distance in which a pause is operative is a free zone." CSCC: "To pause for a specified time. The PAUSE time is added in the leg time calculations." |
| **Gain** | PCA: "To make up a specified time during passage of a specified distance. The gain time is subtracted from the time required..." CSCC: same, "The distance over which a GAIN is operative is free of checkpoints and route controls." |
| **Free zone** | PCA: "A part of the timed rally route in which there are no timing controls." (Speed still applies; you are simply guaranteed no checkpoint.) |
| **Transit zone / TRANSIT** | PCA: "A part of a rally route in which there are no timing controls and in which no specific speed need be maintained." CSCC: "Route instructions apply, but you may leave the route for rest or refueling stops. The TRANSIT time is added in the leg time calculations." |
| **Time allowance / time declaration (TA)** | A competitor-declared delay added to their perfect time, used instead of speeding to catch up. PCA Sacramento GIs: "If you are delayed...road blockage, slow traffic, getting lost, nature call, or any other reason; do not speed to try to make up the lost time" — take a time allowance. (https://dl.motorsportreg.com/77342833-0e55-40f3-8393-e03f2f94cef3/). CSCC: "A time declaration must be requested on the half-minute and cannot be claimed in even minutes," one per leg, max 19.50 minutes per event. (CSCC procedures.tex). Richta: pressing TA gives 10 s, then 20 s, then 30 s, then +1 min per press up to 19:30 (Alcan setup PDF) — another doc says up to 9.5 minutes (search summary; exact caps are event-configurable, UNVERIFIED which is default). |

### 1.2 Checkpoints / controls

| Term | Definition (source) |
|---|---|
| **Open control** | Manned checkpoint, visible, on the right side of the road. Harvey Cain: "At an open control you stop past the control vehicle so as not to block the official's line of sight to the timing line. You'll carefully walk back to the timing vehicle and receive the time you entered the control, the time you should leave the control (your 'out time') and information on the leg you just completed." (zone8 PDF). CSCC: "Pull up to the control station beyond the checkpoint sign"; scorecard comes back with "End leg time (in time) and Start leg time (out time)"; then proceed to the out marker and "time themselves out." Blocking a control entrance = max leg score (300). (CSCC procedures.tex) |
| **Closed / hidden control** | Not confirmed in the fetched sources; the NW Classic text says timing locations "are not given in the instructions" (https://www.nwclassicrally.org/How-TSDs-Work.html). SCCA rulebook distinguishes open vs closed controls — UNVERIFIED wording here because the rulebook PDF could not be fetched. |
| **DIYC (Do-It-Yourself Checkpoint)** | PCA Parade rules: "Do-It-Yourself legs (DIYCs) are legs that do not end in a manned checkpoint...except for an instruction that says 'End DIYC at...' You must give yourselves a departure time at the end of the DIYC...usually your arrival time plus one-minute and should be recorded as your out-time for the next leg." (https://mediaassets.pca.org/pages/microsites/parade2024/files/uploads/91052_pcrs_2024_tsd_rally.pdf). CSCC: record arrival "to the hundredth of a minute as your End Leg time...Record your next Start Leg time as exactly two (2.00) minutes after your End leg time." (CSCC glossary.tex). PCA: at least 5 controls, no more than half DIYC, never two DIYCs in a row. |
| **Passage control / route control** | CSCC "Route Control": stop, present scorecard, receive "a PAUSE for the time spent stopping at the control" plus any supplemental instructions — i.e., proves you passed a point without re-timing the leg. (CSCC procedures.tex). SCCA "passage control" wording UNVERIFIED. |
| **Out time / in time** | See open control row; the out time is the new zero for the next leg's timing. "You cannot make up time from previous checkpoints. When you leave a checkpoint you are basically starting from scratch." (PCA Sacramento GIs). "The score for each leg is independent of the other legs. You cannot make up for a late arrival at a checkpoint by arriving early by the same amount at the next." (ABC Region generals, https://cdn.connectsites.net/user_files/scca/downloads/000/067/543/RR%20TSD_2Page_Generals%2020230408.pdf) |
| **Early vs late penalties** | Symmetric: "You receive penalty points for both early and late arrivals – driving fast to arrive at a control early will only reward you with penalty points." (SDR GIs) |
| **Max** | Per-checkpoint cap. CSCC: 300 points (3+ minutes early or late). ABC Region: 200 points max per checkpoint. San Diego: 300. PCA (NTR/NCR): penalty "shall not exceed two (2) minutes." (sources as cited above) |
| **Richta GPS Checkpoints** | Smartphone app replacing manned controls: "monitors your car's location using GPS and records your arrival time at each control to a resolution of .1 second, computes your score, and displays it to you. At each timing control, the Richta Competitor App will ding and report your time and score." Tap the bar to see "a critique for that leg, including the exact mileage for that control" to adjust your mileage. No cell service needed, only GPS. (Alcan 5000 Richta setup PDF; Milwaukee Region event pages on motorsportreg.com) |
| **Critique** | Post-leg (or post-rally) sheet showing official mileages, perfect times and the correct course, so you can find out where you lost time. (Richta description above; Gary Starr's driver tips mention keeping "critiques" on the centerboard — http://www.indyscca.org/RallyFiles/TIPS4DRIVERS.pdf) |

### 1.3 Classes

- SCCA / general (search summary from https://oregonrally.com/roadrally/, https://www.wdcr-scca.org/road-rally, OVR Magazine): **Equipped** = "computers that integrate distance and time"; **Limited** = "cars equipped with auxiliary odometers capable of precise mileage measurement"; **Stock** = "'pencil and paper' timekeeping utilizing a basic calculator, tables and a stock odometer"; **SOP (seat-of-the-pants)** = no measuring equipment beyond what came on the car, no use of the car's computer for average speed, no calculating devices except paper and pencil; **Novice** = SOP equipment rules and neither contestant has competed in five or more TSD rallies.
- CSCC: Unequipped division (Novice "for beginners only", SOP "intermediate", Masters "experienced") — "pen, paper, timepiece(s) and stock odometer" only, no calculation devices; Equipped division: Limited (calculators/computers/GPS allowed but "does not receive a direct input from any distance-measuring device interfaced with the vehicle" — mileage entered by hand) and Unlimited. (CSCC appendix.tex)
- Scoring summary from Oregon Rally: "Each second is a point, therefore if you pass a control 10 seconds early or 10 seconds late, you are given 10 points. The lowest cumulative score wins." (https://oregonrally.com/roadrally/)

### 1.4 Route-following vocabulary (course rallies)

From CSCC glossary.tex unless noted (its full text was readable; SCCA definitions are similar but not identical — see http://drscca.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/road_rally_103.pdf and the SCCA rulebook):

- **General Instructions (GIs)** vs **Route Instructions (RIs)**: PCA: GIs are the "set of instructions specific to a given rally that defines the format and rally-specific rules to include CASTs, pauses, transits, all control locations, etc." RIs are the numbered turn-by-turn list. JCNA rule: "Don't Assume Anything - Be sure to read the General Instructions and don't hesitate to ask questions." (https://www.jcna.com/library/rally/chapter9.html)
- **Numbered / Note / Supplemental instructions**: Numbered RIs completed in ascending order; Notes are unnumbered and "active from its introduction until cancelled," superseding numbered instructions; Supplementals are handed out at checkpoints and must be done before resuming the numbered sequence. (CSCC route-instructions.tex). Route priority: emergency directions from officials > supplemental > note > numbered > main road. (CSCC rally-route.tex)
- **Main road rule / Main Road Determinants (MRDs)**: PCA: "The main road is the road that is most directly ahead." CSCC applies a strict hierarchy, each used only if it yields exactly one road: (1) ONTO/FOLLOW, (2) TOWARD, (3) PROTECTION ("the single road which leaves the intersection without a back-facing stop or yield sign"), (4) SURFACE (same paved/unpaved type), (5) STRAIGHT AS POSSIBLE (not applicable if equally straight or all exits >45 degrees), (6) LEFTMOST. (CSCC main-road-determinants.tex). SCCA also lists CURVE ARROWS ("the road leaving the intersection as shown by an official curve warning ahead...sign") and CENTERLINE STRIPE as main-road determinants (SCCA rulebook via search summary).
- **ONTO**: SCCA: "When placed onto a road by name or number, the rallyist is to continue on that road, however it may turn, until a subsequent course-directing Route Instruction is executed." (SCCA rulebook summary). CSCC: ONTO/FOLLOW makes that named road the main road "until a non-MBCU deviation occurs."
- **AT**: PCA: "'In the vicinity of', for actions that direct a course to travel; 'even with' for other actions, including CASTs, pauses, mileage, etc."
- **TOWARD**: follow signs in the direction of the landmark at each intersection.
- **T**: "An intersection having the shape of the letter T as approached from the base. It is not possible to execute the instruction S or the instruction STRAIGHT at a T." SCCA: "exactly three roads."
- **Y**: same shape logic, "approached from the base"; SCCA: "always regarded as approached from the bottom of the symbol."
- **SIGNAL**: CSCC: "multi-light traffic signal(s) which may be operating as blinker(s) or may be off." SCCA: "need not be operational...Count only one SIGNAL per intersection."
- **BLINKER**, **STOP** (official stop sign where you must legally stop), **YIELD**, **CROSSROAD** ("A crossing of two legal rally roads...may cross at any angle"), **SIDEROAD** (can TURN in only one direction), **OPP** (opportunity — a possible deviation that is paved and/or named as indicated).
- **L / R / S / TURN**: L = "Leftmost deviation of any angle off the main road"; S = "straightest deviation...within 45 degrees of straight ahead"; TURN = "deviation from the main road in the only direction possible...cannot be executed if an instruction to go straight would take the contestant on the same route."
- **ONEOF**: not in the CSCC glossary; its SCCA/Detroit meaning (one of several named references in a list, execute at whichever appears) is UNVERIFIED here because the SCCA/DRSCCA PDFs could not be fetched. CSCC's analogous construct is **OR**: execute one half of the instruction, "the reference point of the two possibilities which is located first determines which half."
- **ITIS** ("if there is such"): execute only if its action point appears before the next NRI's action point; otherwise skip it and consider it completed.
- **MBCU** ("may be considered unnecessary"): a labeled deviation that may be executed to follow the main road.
- **AFTER / BEFORE**: first intersection beyond / last intersection prior to and in sight of the referenced sign.
- **API** ("after previous NRI"): at the indicated point beyond completion of the previous instruction.
- **OBSERVE**: "To visually note and pass a sign or landmark."
- **SOL**: "Sign must be on left. May not always be given."
- **Action point / Reference point / Landmark**: Action points are where instructions execute (an intersection where you turn, a main-road intersection with official mileage, a stated distance/duration). Reference points are signs, landmarks or official mileage that mark the start of an instruction. Landmarks appear "in upper case (all capital letters) not in quotation marks." (CSCC route-instructions.tex)
- **Alpines**: intersection diagrams; "The dot...represents the road on which the contestant enters" and "an arrowhead indicates the road on which the contestant leaves"; if multiple legal routes, take the shortest. (CSCC)
- **Tulip diagrams**: the European/Indian equivalent of alpines (ball = you enter, arrow = you exit). CVR PCA: a good navigator "can read tulip maps and make calculations without getting flustered." (CVR basics PDF). Team-BHP's TSD explainer also uses tulips. (https://www.team-bhp.com/forum/indian-motorsport/168825-explained-tsd-time-speed-distance-rallying.html)
- **Sign conventions**: CSCC: glossary words have their special meaning "only...when they appear in upper case (all capital letters) and not in quotation marks"; lower-case words mean the dictionary definition. Signs are shown "in upper case within quotation marks" matching "spelling, punctuation, detail, and shape of symbols"; any prominent part of a sign may be quoted but "no intervening words...will be skipped, split, combined, or reordered"; house numbers, mailboxes, graffiti, temporary posters and signs on vehicles are never used; "A speed control sign will be used only when the sign controls the legal speed limit of the road you are currently travelling upon." (CSCC signs.tex)
- **Not visible from the course / "visible from the course"**: standard GI language that only signs readable from the rally route count; exact SCCA wording UNVERIFIED (rulebook not fetchable).
- **Speed-change execution point**: CSCC: speed changes at intersections "execute upon entering intersections"; at references, "as you pass by and are even with the reference"; "Temporary speed changes revert after specified time or distance." (CSCC route-instructions.tex)
- **Course markers**: CSCC uses club-initial markers — green = on course, orange = off course; "DNE" marks a non-rally road; numbered correction markers mean "execute that instruction before continuing." (CSCC rally-route.tex)
- **Rally road**: "A rally road is a public through road"; private, closed, dead-end roads and roads requiring illegal turns are excluded; "No uninstructed U-turn(s) will be necessary to follow the rally route." (CSCC rally-route.tex)
- **Leg**: the timed segment between two controls, scored independently (NW Classic; ABC Region).
- **Critique / "trap"**: course rallies include deliberate traps where a careless reading sends you off course; the SCCA Beginners Guide says course rallies have "possible off-course scenarios and traps." Tour rallies have none.
