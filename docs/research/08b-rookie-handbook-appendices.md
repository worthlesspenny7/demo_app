## 1. Appendix B - Car Calibration (printed 17-21)

### 1.1 Purpose and premise [BOOK]
- "You need to calibrate the car, the navigator and the driver. The car and the driver / navigator team
  need to be calibrated together, so that they can perform in the race the same as they do during the
  calibration."
- "The Great Race and similar rallies have a basic assumption that makes it challenging. This assumption
  is that a car can stop instantaneously and accelerate to speed instantaneously." Real cars lose time on
  many maneuvers in the Course Instructions; the losses add up to large errors; calibration tells you the
  size of each loss so you can correct for it.
- The method is "a relatively simple way for rookies". "The numbers used as examples are purely for
  illustration and do not reflect the actual performance of a particular car." Actual performance charts for
  a 1936 Packard are in the main text on pages 7-9 (08a covers those); "If you do not have time to
  calibrate your car, the 1936 Packard charts are better than nothing."
- The method calibrates the car, gives the driver experience in consistent driving and gives the navigator
  experience with the stop watch.

### 1.2 Test course [BOOK]
- A straight section of road about half a mile long with little or no traffic, with a safe way to turn
  around near each end (little-used country road or a very large mall parking lot).
- Run at speeds 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45 and 50 mph (eight speeds, 5 mph steps, 15 to 50).
- Exact length unimportant (comparisons are between runs at the same speed). "A course that takes about 40
  seconds to traverse at 50 mph is about right." Mark both ends with a visible marker (orange cone or stick
  with a flag). A shorter course may be used for the low-speed runs to save time.
- [DERIVED] 40 s at 50 mph = 2,933 ft = 0.556 mi. The sample constant-speed times (below) are consistent with
  this length (15 mph: 133 s; 20: 100 s; 25: 80 s; 30: 66.7 s; 35: 57.1 s; 40: 50 s; 45: 44.4 s; 50: 40 s).
- At least 4 runs at each speed, in BOTH directions; "You need at least 4 runs to get a good average time. If
  you have a lot of variation, make more runs."

### 1.3 Step-by-step procedure [BOOK]

Step 1 - constant-speed runs ("Run the course at speeds of 15 ... 50 mph. Make at least 4 runs at each speed
and record the times."). Time the whole course at steady speed. This gives "Average Run Time" per speed.

Step 2 - acceleration runs (time lost on acceleration):
1. Drive to the starting marker at one end of the course and STOP.
2. Navigator gives a start count "3, 2, 1, GO" (or "1, 2, 3, GO").
3. Start the stopwatch at GO; the driver accelerates to the speed being measured and, after reaching it,
   continues to the end of the course at that speed. Stop the watch at the end marker.
4. Start must be consistent each time. Side note: "the navigator [should] always end a countdown with the word
   GO. The driver often forgets when he should begin the maneuver, so the term GO is always the indication for
   the driver to execute."
5. Make the accelerations "at a little less than full throttle" so the driver can make a seat-of-the-pants
   adjustment during the race (example given: an uphill road leaving a stop sign). "On the actual race you
   want the acceleration to be exactly the same as the chart."
6. Repeat at each speed until the numbers are consistent enough for a good average.
7. Time lost on acceleration = (average run time from a stop) - (average constant-speed run time).
   Clarification [BOOK]: "this is NOT the time it takes to accelerate to a certain speed. It IS the time lost
   between driving at a constant speed and accelerating from 0 to that speed."

Step 3 - braking runs (time lost on deceleration):
1. Enter the course at the desired speed (flying, i.e. already at speed at the start marker).
2. Brake to a stop at the end of the course "so that the front wheels of the car are at the end marker when
   stopped."
3. "Stop the stopwatch when the car 'rocks' back after the stop."
4. Time lost on braking = (average run time with stop at end) - (average constant-speed run time).

Step 4 - build the Braking-Acceleration chart (matrix) from the two loss columns (see 1.5).

Step 5 - build the Stop & Go pause chart (see 1.5) and the Turns chart (see 1.6).

"The three charts you have made (Braking - Acceleration, Pause Times for Stops, and Turns) are all you will
need for your first few rallies." With experience adjust, e.g. "if you find that you spend more time at 15 mph
during a corner you may want to increase the times lost in that chart."

### 1.5 The Braking-Acceleration chart (printed p.20) [BOOK]

How to fill it [BOOK]: "After you fill in the first vertical column and the top row with the numbers from your
tests, you can then use simple math to fill in the blanks. For instance if the loss on acceleration from 0-30
is 3.2 seconds and the loss from 0-50 is 7.2 seconds, loss from 30 to 50 would be the difference, or 4.0
seconds. Put 4 in the junction between 30 and 50."

Layout: columns are labelled ACCELERATION (target speed, 0, 15 ... 50); rows are labelled BRAKING (starting
speed, 0, 15 ... 50). Grey diagonal cells (same speed) are blanks.
- Row "0" (top row, grey figures) = acceleration losses 0->column speed (Table B-3).
- Column "0" (first vertical column, grey/underlined figures) = braking losses row speed->0 (Table B-5).
- Upper-right triangle (column speed > row speed): acceleration loss from row speed to column speed =
  acc(col) - acc(row). Lower-left triangle (row speed > column speed): braking loss from row speed down to
  column speed = brk(row) - brk(col).

Meaning (sim-relevant): an entry (row r, col c) with c > r is the time lost speeding up from r to c; with

### 1.6 Stop & Go pause chart and Turns chart (printed p.20-21) [BOOK]

**Stop sign rule [BOOK]:** "The Course Instructions usually say to pause at stop signs for 15 seconds. Of course
you must adjust your pause times to allow for braking and acceleration losses. To determine these pause times for
each combination of IN and OUT speeds, add the lost time due to braking to the lost time for acceleration and
subtract from 15 seconds."

    actual_pause(IN, OUT) = 15 - brake_loss(IN -> 0) - accel_loss(0 -> OUT)

Worked example [BOOK]: "for a 40 IN-30 OUT stop sign, add 2.5 (40 to 0 braking loss) to 3.2 (0 to 30
acceleration loss) and subtract from 15 to get 9.3". "The result is the actual time you should pause at the stop
sign so that you are back on time after you reach the OUT speed."

**Turn rule [BOOK]:** "A good way to make a turn is to always slow from the IN speed to 15 mph, make the turn,
and accelerate to the OUT speed. If you brake and accelerate for corners the same way as you do for stop signs,
then you can use the same braking and acceleration numbers from your chart. Take the time lost from braking from
the IN speed to 15 mph and add the acceleration loss from 15 mph to the OUT speed." "The time lost in a corner
must be made up after the OUT speed is reached in order to be back on time."

    turn_loss(IN, OUT) = [brk(IN) - brk(15)]  +  [acc(OUT) - acc(15)]
                       = Braking-Acceleration chart cell (IN -> 15)  +  cell (15 -> OUT)

## 2. Appendix C - Timewise Speedometer Calibration (printed 23-24)

Context [BOOK]: "Proper calibration of the Timewise speedometer is critical to achieving good scores in the
Great Race. This appendix describes initial setting of the speedometer and two methods for making adjustments to
the calibration based on the speedometer calibration runs provided each day during the Great Race." Also
described in the instruction book supplied with the speedometer and in a video on the Great Race website. The
appendix covers only the Timewise electronic unit; mechanical dash speedometers are not discussed in it.

### 2.1 A. Initial setting of the factor (wheel sensors only; driveshaft sensors: see Timewise manual) [BOOK]
1. Inflate the tires to the proper operating pressure.
2. Put a chalk mark on the tire and on the road.
3. Roll the car forward 5 revolutions of the tire.
4. Mark the road again where the tire chalk mark meets the road.
5. Measure the distance between the chalk marks to the nearest 10th of an inch. Eliminate the decimal point; this
   is the factor to enter into the computer. Example: measurement 431.5 inches -> factor 4315.
6. On the back of the speedometer the four holes with the factor switches must be at the bottom (6 o'clock)
   position.
7. Set the factor by adjusting the switches starting on the left (four-digit factor, one digit per switch).
8. TIP: mark the direction of the factor on the back of the speedometer with a paint pen or marker "to avoid any
   uncertainty when setting the speedometer in a hurry during the race."

[DERIVED] 431.5 in / 5 rev = 86.3 in circumference (7.19 ft). Factor units = tenths of an inch of travel per 5
wheel revolutions, so one "click" (1 count of the factor) = 1/4315 = 0.023% of speed/distance.

### 2.2 B. Adjustment Method #1 ("easy to do without a calculator during the race") [BOOK]
1. Divide the initial speedometer factor by 3600. This gives the number of "clicks" the factor must change for
   each second per hour that you are early or late on the speedometer calibration run. It is constant for the
   installation. Example: 4315 / 3600 = 1.198, round to the nearest tenth = 1.2 "clicks" per second per hour.
   Tip: write this number on the back of the unit.
2. Run the speedometer calibration per the Great Race Course Instructions.
3. Decide the direction: "If you are FAST (early arrival) on the course, INCREASE the factor. If you are SLOW
   (late arrival) on the course, REDUCE the factor."
4. Take the difference in seconds between the correct time and your actual time for the calibration run and
   convert it to seconds per hour.
5. Multiply that error (s/hour) by the adjustment factor (1.2) to get the number of clicks; round.
6. Change the factor by that many clicks in the right direction and set it.

Worked example [BOOK] (the book says "Appendix D, instruction #18"; the numbers are the ones printed at
instruction #18 of the Appendix E Trophy Run - see 3.4 - and NOTE the mismatch of appendix letter):
- Correct (perfect) time for the calibration run: 28 min 43.2 s. Actual: 28 min 47.3 s. Difference 4.1 s in 29
  minutes.
- "Multiply this by 2 to change this into seconds per hour. So your error is 8.2 seconds per hour." (hour factor
  approximated as 2 because the run is about 29-30 min; exact would be 3600/1723.2 = 2.089, giving 8.57 s/h.)
- 1.2 x 8.2 = 9.84 clicks, "Round this off to 10 clicks".
- You are SLOW, so REDUCE: new factor = 4315 - 10 = 4305. Set it in the speedometer.

### 2.3 Adjustment Method #2 (calculator; "more difficult to do on the course... Calculators are not allowed to be
used during the race") [BOOK]
1. Run the calibration course per the Course Instructions.
2. Convert the correct time to seconds: 28 min 43.2 s = 1723.2 s.
3. Convert the actual time to seconds: 28 min 47.3 s = 1727.3 s.
4. SLOW case (reduce factor):

       Correct time / Actual time x Factor = New Factor
       1723.2 / 1727.3 x 4315 = 4304.7, so the new factor is 4305.

5. FAST case, the book says: "the formula is reversed to INCREASE the factor: Actual time / Correct time X
   Factor = New Factor."

[NOTE - apparent erratum] As printed, the FAST formula (Actual/Correct x Factor) with Actual < Correct gives a
number SMALLER than the factor, contradicting the rule "FAST -> INCREASE". The formula Correct/Actual x
Factor is the one that gives the right direction in both cases (fast: Correct > Actual, ratio > 1, factor
increases). Method #1 agrees: 10 clicks vs Method #2's 10.3 for the same example. The sim should implement
new = old x (correct / actual) and treat the printed fast-case formula as a typo.

Special note [BOOK]: if the speedometer does not return to zero (usually loss of power to the speedometer while
the car is moving), stop the car, disconnect power, remove the face glass and gently move the needle back to zero.

### 2.4 Facts for the sim (Appendix C only)
- Calibration data come from the "speedometer calibration run" in the daily instructions: a 21-24 mile section at
  50 mph average (D example: ~21 miles / 26 min; E example: 24 miles / 29 min, perfect time 28m43.2s).
- Resolution of the correction: 1 click = 1/4315 of speed = 0.83 s per hour (that is why clicks per s/h = factor/3600).
  Adjustments are in whole clicks.
- Magnitude of a typical first-run error in the worked example: 4.1 s over 1723 s = 0.238% (about 0.12 mph at 50).
- Rally Equipment checklist (Appendix F) lists "Speedometer calibration tools (small screwdriver and
  flashlight)": the factor is changed with a small screwdriver on the four rear switches, in the car.

### 3.2 How the sheets and diagrams are drawn
Page frame (all 13 pages): a four-column grid with a column-letter header row A, B, C, D. Footer: "(c) 2014, Great
Race" (left), "Hemmings Motor News Great Race / Page N of 13" (centre), "Hagerty Trophy Run / Friday, June 20,
2014" (right). Each instruction is one numbered row (number printed small at the bottom-left of column A; 89
rows total, 6-9 rows per page). Handwritten text, circles, strike-throughs and arrows are the annotation layer
added for the handbook ([HW]).

- **Column A - the CAMEO (route diagram).** The route is a heavy black line that starts at a bold filled dot at
  the bottom of the box (your approach position / the point you are driving from) and runs up the page with an
  arrowhead showing travel direction. A turn is drawn as the heavy line bending left or right at a thinner cross
  road (thin line = road you cross or that ends); the heavy line's arrow shows where you go. Straight-ahead
  instructions have a plain vertical arrow, sometimes with the cross road drawn across it (crossing a stop sign
  or light). Bears/forks are curved arrows; Y-intersections are two curved lines meeting at the dot (some
  with a STOP octagon on each leg). T intersections are a vertical line ending against a horizontal (sometimes
  with the diamond T warning sign beside the dot). Road names and sign text are printed in bold next to the
  relevant road; boxed bold text is a roadside sign, drawn on the side of the road it stands on (left of the
  arrow = sign on left, right = sign on right; a sign centred over the arrow = overhead). Standard icons are
  drawn for: traffic light (vertical three-lamp signal), STOP sign (small octagon with "STOP"), flashing
  light (sunburst/starburst lamp; hand-labelled "STOP LIGHT" at #30), Stop Ahead / curve / crossroad / T / reverse
  curve warning diamonds, speed limit rectangles ("Speed Limit 35"), a roundabout (circle with entry arrow and
  the roadside business signs), a toll booth, building/business logo boxes (white-on-black rectangles such as
  "Cumberland Farms", "Tim Hortons", "Shell", "Citgo"). Names in parentheses, such as "(Ricker)",
  "(Kennebunk Pond)", "(Maine Mall Rd)", appear to be roads whose names are not posted [NOTE: inferred, the
  appendix does not define it]. Dashed lines on a cross road (e.g. #45, #50) indicate the far side/lane or the
  stop line.
- **Column B - graphics/pictograms.** Tire with odometer boxes (tire warm-up, "0 0 9 0" = 9.0 miles, the
  right-hand box black = tenths); hourglass (transit/untimed segment, with approximate mileage in odometer boxes);

  camcorder icons (see #76-#80).
- **Column C - time and speed.** Bold speed ("35 MPH") sets the assigned speed from this instruction on. A stop is
  a three-line cell "0 MPH / 0m15s / NN MPH" (stop; official pause 15 s; leave at NN). Printed times use the
  format `NmNN.Ns` (e.g. 4m03.4s) or `NmNNs`. A watch icon with "EDT" and a time of day (12:00:00, 1:40:00) means a
  time-of-day start or restart. Parentheses, e.g. "(1m30s)", mean an approximate/informational drive time from
  this instruction to the next one (the book writes "approx. 1 min. 30 sec. from #5 to #6"); they are not scored.
  Calibration-run rows show two stacked times in a box: the INTERVAL time (top-left, from the previous
  calibration point) and the CUMULATIVE time (bottom-right, from the start of the calibration run).
  Speeds are only printed where they change; when a line has no speed the navigator must carry the current one
  forward ([HW] "WHEN INSTRUCTIONS RECEIVED, WRITE THE SPEED IN ALL SECTIONS WHERE IT IS MISSING"; page headers
  carry a handwritten carried-over speed, "WHEN INSTRUCTIONS ARE RECEIVED, WRITE SPEED FROM PRECEDING PAGE HERE").
- **Column D - written notes.** Short remarks only ("comes quick", "look sharp", "sign on left", "1st exit",
  "2nd [signal icon]" = second signal), plus services/food/fuel lists in the transit sections. This Trophy Run
  prints NO full written-sentence instruction for each row (the sentence instructions of the Appendix D example,
  e.g. "Turn right onto Buchanan Blvd at a crossroad at a Traffic Light", are not in this real 2014 sheet).
- Annotation conventions [HW]: the printed "0m15s" is struck through and replaced by "P" + number (P=Pause)
  from the car's Stop & Go chart; a circled negative number next to a speed, e.g. (-2.9), is the lost time for that
  turn/corner for the IN speed (previous speed) and the OUT speed printed, from the car's Turns chart;
  "PLANNED PAUSE TIMES ARE FROM CHART ON 'STOP & GO'. (P= PAUSE)".

### 5.4 Other differences from 07 / OPEN-QUESTIONS the appendices reveal
1. Column C content (07 1.1-1.4, Q2): speeds and pauses are stacked cells: "0 MPH / 0m15s / 35 MPH" for a
   stop, "20 / 1m39s / 30 MPH" for a timed change, "35 MPH" for a landmark speed. Speed is printed only where it
   changes; blank means carry the previous speed forward. Time format `NmNN.Ns` / `NmNNs`; time-of-day shown with a
   watch icon and "EDT". Parenthesized times are informational (approximate time to the next line).
2. Pause locations (07 1.4-1.6; Q4, Q15): the E sheet prints a 15 s pause at EVERY stop sign (26 lines) and also
   at one traffic signal (#78 straight across a signal) and at flashing lights (#30, #81). Other traffic lights
   appear only in the untimed/transit parts with no pause (#2-#4, #21-#24, #67, #86). No railroad crossing and no
   pause at sharp turns without a stop sign. So Q15's "STOP-only; lights handled by Time Allowance" differs: a printed
   0 MPH/15 s stop at a signal and at flashing lights exists. The appendices do not say whether a green light still
   requires consuming the pause (Q4 untested here).
3. Speed on a turn line (Q3, 07 1.1): E's turn lines print the OUT speed; the circled lost time is for the IN
   speed (previous line) -> OUT speed (annotation at #31: "35 mph IN and a 30 mph OUT turn"); #32 says "At the sign
   drop to the speed of 20 mph and then turn", i.e. the new speed is reached before the turn when it is lower.
   Speed-limit-sign lines (#28, 39, 44, 59): "change speed at the sign" - speed changes at the sign. Consistent with
   Q3 for signs; the turn line's speed is the exit speed.
4. Start/restart (07 5, Q10, Q11, Q17): instructions arrive 30 minutes before the start time (matches Q10 = 30).
   Start time = start-of-day clock time + ASP in minutes (12:00 + 42 = 12:42; D example: 8:00:00 + ASP min);
   one-minute spacing between cars. Lunch/afternoon restart is a printed time-of-day restart (1:40:00 + ASP min =
   2:22:00 for ASP 42), not "arrival + 45 min" (Q17 default). The morning staging to the next day's start uses 30 s
   intervals (8:30:00 + 30 s x position, position 53 -> 8:56:30) for the half-mile to the start; Official Start Time
   10:00:00.
5. Transit and untimed segments (07 1.7): there are three distinct kinds: (a) approximate transit after the
   calibration run, 55m00s for about 5 miles ending at a time-of-day restart; (b) an exact timed transit (#65-#70)
   of exactly 30m00s written by the navigator as arrival + 30:00 = departure; (c) the end-of-day run (#84-#88) of
   about 9 miles / 30 min with a watch icon crossed out (not timed), ending at an Observation Checkpoint at the
   finish line (checkered flag, "stop at Observation Checkpoint"; D: "do not go to your hotel until after you have been
   to the Observation Checkpoint"), which supports Q18 (mandatory stop, nothing timed).
6. Checkpoints (07 4, Q25): E #76-#80 carries "THERE WILL BE NO CHECK POINTS BETWEEN THESE INSTRUCTIONS" with a
   crossed-out camcorder at #76 and a camcorder icon at #80 - the sheet can declare checkpoint-free zones.
   No hidden-checkpoint information is printed anywhere else in the appendices.
7. Instruction format (Q26): in this real 2014 Trophy Run sheet column D holds only short remarks ("comes quick",
   "look sharp", "1st exit", sign side), no full-sentence instruction; the sentence-style text exists only in the
   Appendix D example (written text in column D beside the CAMEO). So Q26 "separate text column" matches
   Appendix D; the real E sheet has no written instruction at all. Warning remarks are used heavily: "comes quick",
   "comes very quick", "look sharp", "look very sharp", "sign overhead", "sign on left", "sign may be twisted".
8. Calibration/practice measures vs Q22 "0-35 and 0-50 times": the handbook never asks for 0-35 / 0-50 times as
   such; it asks for time LOST relative to a constant-speed run at each of 8 speeds (and for braking likewise).
   If the player is meant to measure their own car, the sim should output timed run results for these three run
   types (constant, standing start, braking stop) rather than raw acceleration times.
9. Run count (07 2.1 "repeat the run"): at least 4 runs per speed per type, both directions (about 96 runs for the full chart:
   8 speeds x 3 run types x 4); the handbook estimate for the course is 40 s at 50 mph; low-speed runs may use a shorter
   course.
10. Hourly sensitivity (07 3.4): 8.2 s/h error from a 4.1 s miss on a 29-minute run; correction of 1.2 clicks per
   s/h for factor 4315. Use these as the Timewise calibration unit.
11. Appendix D/E cross-reference inconsistency: Appendix C refers to "Appendix D, instruction #18" while the
    numbers are those of the Appendix E Trophy Run instruction #18 (28m43.2s correct, 28m47.3s actual, [HW]
    "SEE APPENDIX 'C'"). Treat the numbers as belonging to E.
12. Internal book inconsistencies to avoid copying: fast-case Method #2 formula direction (see 2.3); Packard 30->40
    pause 8.2 vs 8.6; Appendix E annotation at #18 says "28m47.3s" matches Appendix C.
