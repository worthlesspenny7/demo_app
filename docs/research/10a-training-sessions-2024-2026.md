## A2. Concrete rules, numbers, procedures and tips (grouped by topic)

### Start procedure, order of start, ASP
- [66:39-67:40] You learn your start time the night before: the end of each day's instructions gives next-day stage start time, the order of start (position, who you follow) and the hotel where instructions are handed out (not always your hotel). Work backwards from it (breakfast, luggage, drive to the pickup hotel).
- [67:40] You may set clock and stopwatch in the hotel room but recheck at the instruction pickup. Instructions are picked up 30 minutes before your start time (matches REG VII.B.2.a).
- [70:15] Know the cars ahead and behind. "That guy is exactly two minutes ahead of you." You hold that position all day unless someone goes off course; the order is redrawn daily.
- [77:58] "Starting on time: single most important thing for a rookie to learn... there is nobody on the course telling you [to go]." Based on your performance chart (0 to 30 etc.); the navigator counts down; "it will always be sooner than the actual time... because you have to compensate for the time it takes to accelerate to that speed."
- [78:30-79:31] Most teams start from a dead stop. Some do a flying/rolling start (whip past, turn around, come back, time it); it suits low-horsepower cars and hills; a chat voice ("Mark", as heard) confirms it is "frowned on" [79:01].
- [91:59-93:30] Sample start: digital clock "1:40" with a Stop Ahead sign, 20 mph out speed. "If you are car 32 starting at 1:40 you add 32 minutes: 2:12." Car 1 starts 1:41, car 2 1:42, car 10 1:50. Verify your place by the cars in the order of start (the Packard two ahead, the '32 Ford, "a Pricer" behind) [93:30].
- [33:19-34:19] Etiquette at start: five or six cars queue at the sign; the car in front who leaves in 10 min blocks everyone behind; do not sit at the sign.
- [76:58-77:28] Start decisions: if school buses/trains block your start, either pull out early and bleed off seconds, or wait and file a time delay (time allowance).
- [111:00] Before any restart "double and triple check your time and look for the cars around you; pull out your order of start."

### Instructions format, pre-read, page check
- [27:39] A day's instructions run "30 to 50 pages". Start order list also on lapboard.
- [28:41] CAMEO: "you are the dot... the arrow shows where you proceed"; drawn signs show the sign face; instructions start pristine and end the day marked up.
- [83:09-84:11] Sections shown in Column B: tire warm-up, speedometer calibration (analog speedometer picture), then sequential timed instructions. Four columns: A road/signage/intersections; B section symbol; C speed and timing; D "don't ignore column D... special instructions, course notes".
- [84:11] "Do not leave the table until you go through that entire thing"; sample says 13 pages; check all pages are present and in order (machines slip).
- [68:10-68:41] Emergency instructions (road closure, flooding, construction) are taped to the car window by the clock and given at registration; read and apply them to your instructions before marking up.
- [97:35] Carry the speed over: at the bottom of each page note the speed; copy it to the top of the next page on every page.
- [98:05] "Don't go on to the next instruction until you completely finish the previous one."
- [100:08] An empty Column C box (e.g. left jog sign) = no speed change; continue previous speed; write the previous speed in.
- [99:37] "Look sharp" in Column D with words to the right of the sign = sign is on the right; words left = sign on left.
- [100:39] Parentheses around a sign in the CAMEO = you will not be able to see it until after the turn.
- [101:09-101:45] "Comes quick" in Column D: highlight it; if it falls at the top of the next page note it on the previous page so you turn the page early.
- [104:48] Check off each instruction (squiggle or tick) so you know which of two identical STOP-sign instructions you are on.
- [111:30-112:02] Mazes: other cars may be heading at you or turning the other way at the same intersection; "run your race, do the maneuver, cross it off."
- [80:02] Instructions "are not designed to fool you"; signs legible; they say which side the sign is on; occasionally "second paved road to the right". Both driver and navigator keep heads up.
- [30:44-31:48] Sign variants: the squiggle sign comes in a left-start and a right-start version; if the book shows one and the road has the other, still do the maneuver. "This is not a trap rally" (a 2023 incident cost two or three good teams).
- [125:22-125:53] Landmark/building drawn in the CAMEO is "just in instructions as a reference" (usually the corner building); if there is a sign you change speed at the sign (Jeff's answer to a navigator question).

### Calibration run and speedometer (Timewise)
- [07:51] Jeff: if you do not have a speedometer "you need to get one and let us know; we might find a used one... they hold their value."
- [13:01] A factory speedometer "is no damn good"; Steve's 1964 Studebaker Stewart-Warner reads about 2 mph slow at 50; you can use it but "you have to do mathematical calculations all the way".
- [11:29-12:30] Timewise: four holes on the back (four factor wheels) set the factor each morning; heavy and tough.
- [38:27-39:27] Timewise uses a magnetic pickup: two magnets on the left front wheel, equidistant; adjustable probe; 12 V power. Power from the car's electrical system or a separate battery (people forget to charge the separate battery).
- [39:58-41:31] Mount it up high in the line of sight; it must be removable so you can turn it over for the daily adjustment; put an "UP" sticker on the back; if upside down "you're in real trouble".
- [41:31-42:02] If you turn the car off while rolling the needle stays where it was; unscrew the face and push the needle back to zero; check it reads zero before you leave. (Bob [46:41]: unplug at 5 mph to learn the trick.)
- [42:34-43:34] Run the calibration: you pass a measured course (generally on an interstate) at exactly 50 mph and compare lapsed time to the rally master's times; adjust as often as needed. "Running early: increase the factor; late: decrease."
- [43:34] New factor = correct time / actual time (seconds) x old factor (usable with a calculator only off the clock).
- [44:06-44:36] Clicks: factor / 3600 = clicks per second per hour of error; Steve's factor gives 1.13 clicks (so 8 s late = 9 clicks). Convert early/late to seconds, multiply, click that many times.
- [45:06] Initial factor: mark a tire spot and the ground, roll "a complete revolution", measure the distance (HB says 5 revolutions; see table). If you replace the tire during the race you must recalibrate. Keep a small flashlight and a jeweller's screwdriver [45:39]; the dial inside has seven white clicks (marks 10 to 0)(?).
- [69:11] The race gives a calibration run every morning; "do not assume your factor from the previous day, even if you won the previous day, is going to be the same."
- [84:42-85:14] Sample calibration: start at a "Speed Limit 65" sign, hold 50 mph, stopwatch started at the sign; caption says "24 miles... 25 minutes" (?) (the printed official time is 26 minutes at [89:56], HB example 21 miles).
- [85:44] Column C shows interval and cumulative: first sign (Kennebunkport) 4m03.4s interval = cumulative; next sign in 3m16s (?); next sign in 1m10s (?).
- [86:16] Use the split time, "don't hit stop and start, you're going to ruin everything"; write each split beside the instruction. If you hit stop by mistake, restart at the next sign and add the intervals [87:50].
- [86:48-87:50] Look for a steady, linear progression of early or late (example: 2 s early at 7:17, 13 s early at 12:55 vs 12:42 (?)).
- [88:22] Do not tell the driver "we're running early/late"; keep him holding the indicated 50 so you know the exact correction.
- [88:22] Traffic on the calibration run: stay in the right lane at 50, people honk; watch for the lane ending.
- [89:23] Overhead signs on the interstate also tell you which lane to be in; if the sign is not directly overhead you are in the wrong lane.
- [89:56-90:27] The hourglass ends the calibration section; compare your total elapsed time with the official (26 minutes (?)) to see seconds early/late.
- [90:27-90:59] Do not adjust at once (you may miss the next instruction); adjust parked at a gas station or at your start.
- [90:59-91:29] Rental car, cannot adjust: convert error to a correction schedule. "If five seconds late in 25 minutes you are one second late every five minutes, so correct one second every five minutes or two every ten."
- [49:12] Calibrate the speedometer as closely as you can before building the performance chart.

### Speed changes and timed changes
- [95:32-96:33] "The race is calculated as though you can start and stop immediately." Split the sign: start slowing before the sign so you cross it mid-change; 35 to 30: "the sign will be right in the middle"; think of a linear deceleration. Technique comes with experience; being close is fine.
- [98:05-98:35] Sign before an intersection with a speed change: change speed at the sign (drawn in the CAMEO), then make the turn at the new speed (30 to 20 at the sign, then turn at 20: "in to 20, out of 20, or down to 15 out of 20").
- [102:15-102:45] Delayed speed change: once you cross the right-curve sign, hold 20 for "1 min 30 or 36 s" (?) then increase to 30; write the unprinted previous speed in (e.g. "20"); you do nothing but let time elapse before moving up.
- [103:15] Double change at a sign: change at the sign, hold 25 for 1m12s, then increase to 30.

### Stops and pauses
- [94:02-95:02] A "0 MPH" line = a stop. "Gives you 15 seconds for each stop; however you lose time when you decelerate and accelerate, so you take the numbers from your performance chart and adjust the 15 seconds." Example: chart shows 10.2 (4.8 s lost), so hold only 10 s; navigator counts "going to 35 in 10, 9, 8, 7, 6" starting at rock-back.
- [97:04] Blinker with printed "0 / 15 s": "treat that blinker as if it were a stop sign."
- [107:55] Driver: where to stop: gravel can make you slide; if you stopped too soon to see traffic, creep forward; "don't feel you have to stay where you stopped if it's a safety issue."
- [53:20] Driver technique: "when I stop the car I wait until the car rocks back"; that is the stopped moment for the stopwatch.

### Turns
- [52:18-52:48] Performance-chart technique: "every time you do a turn you brake down to 15 mph at the turn, then accelerate to your out-speed; then it will be a consistent number."
- [100:39] Example: out-speed 25, turn loss about 1 s (printed in the chart), "you're going to need to make that up".
- [98:35] Combination instruction: sign, then turn, then speed change.

### Transits, restarts, lunch, finish
- [91:29-91:59] Sample no-host lunch inside a transit; numbers in parentheses "(10)" then "(8)" (minutes) tell how far you are from your start/restart.
- [105:20-107:24] Beginning of transit = full hourglass: "you're not off the clock but temporarily on pause." Stopwatch alone is not enough: "write down the exact time of day to the second" at the sign, add the 30 minutes in the box, carry it to the end-of-transit instruction on the next page as the restart time. Under the hourglass is the mileage (10 miles in 30 min) as a guide, "you don't have an odometer".
- [109:59-110:30] Question: the number under the hourglass is mileage; "it looks like an odometer reading", not an odometer you can use.
- [111:30-112:02] Camera with a slash = no checkpoint for a while (traps like a signal where you could lose seconds); camera without slash = checkpoints possible again [112:33].
- [113:04] Clock with slash = off the clock; "you will probably see a checkpoint crew in those last three instructions" so be on time before it.
- [113:34-114:35] After the end of timing there is still a transit to the finish gate; information boxes tell tonight's host dinner, how long you stay in the "park for May" (display), parking at hotels; the next information box gives next-day start time, pickup hotel, refuel info ("first refueling stop in 135 miles"), tolls (mostly electronic; cash if told).
- [72:52-73:53] End of day: finish gate, score given, then park in display for 1.5 hours (or as told); you cannot leave without permission of the race director.
- [71:51-72:21] Lunch is long enough to eat; do not rush; fuel up in advance or follow "get gas here" instructions.

### Checkpoints and scoring
- [70:48-71:51] Score = how accurately you reproduce the rally master's time from start to checkpoint; you do not know it. Green sign at roadside (driver usually sees it first and tells navigator). "You don't stop, you don't talk to the checkpoint workers... drive right through it like it's not there." Hold your speed; if late, return to the assigned speed at the checkpoint because the next leg starts immediately.
- [71:20] Checkpoint crew shouts/signals as you pass: "that's for the navigator so she can mark down the time"; get stopwatch out, read time of day, write it in the margin.
- [76:28-77:00] Rookies get "throw-out legs" (worst legs discarded).
- [121:18-121:49] The checkpoint resets you: a blown leg is a lost leg, the next leg starts clean.

### Equipment, clocks, stopwatches
- [26:39-28:11] Lapboard: clock, performance chart, stopwatch velcroed to the board or on a neck cord; pens and highlighters in clips; the start order; 30-50 pages of instructions; set it up so the driver can see the CAMEO and you can point with a pen.
- [27:09] Clock: sweep second hand with the hand close to the face (no parallax); markings for every second.
- [14:04-14:34] Tires: fresh; pressures at max or slightly above to stop the tire growing and shrinking and changing speeds.
- [14:34-15:04] Road hazard: a construction crew can remove three inches of asphalt after the sweep; "slow down and make up the time".
- [60:28-61:33] GoPro cameras are allowed and encouraged if the phone is not in the car (can sit in the trunk); do not use the phone to start/stop it.
- [58:57] Cell phones are not accessible; carry a cheap digital camera.

### Driver/navigator teamwork and vocabulary
- [19:50-20:54] Driver: hard to hold an exact speed; divide attention between instrument and road; mount the speedometer as high as possible.
- [20:54] Drive the car the same way every time; not wide open throttle; leave a little margin for hills.
- [21:26] Listening to the engine helps hold speed and acceleration rate.
- [21:59] "The driver cannot compensate for what he thinks is too slow or too fast; that's the navigator's job." If you were over, slow by the same amount and hold for the time you were off. "Don't guess '37'... your navigator has got nothing to work with."
- [23:34] "Look down the road": tractor, school bus, grain truck, train; tell the navigator early.
- [29:42-30:44] Common vocabulary for signs (hard left = "90 degree left" = "dead man left"; side road to left; "double dead man" / "hard left chair"; "snake" or "squiggle"): any words, but identical every time.
- [88:22] Do not give the driver early/late information.
- [24:36] Hydration, gum, glasses spare, physical shape; "you have to focus about 10 hours straight"; "the average leg is about an hour and a half" (they mean time seated in a timed section, not a checkpoint-to-checkpoint leg).
- [75:57] The Four S's: Safety first; starting on time; staying on course; staying on time (taken from aviate-navigate-communicate).

### Traps and common rookie mistakes
- [36:54-37:57] Spectators ("lawn chair people") are a good sign you are on course, but they can mislead: "everybody else went that way" at a maze intersection, or distract from the sign on the other side (cost these speakers two places last year).
- [80:02-80:32] If you slow to confirm an intersection that is fine, then speed up to make up; "don't lock up all four wheels and crank it over".
- [108:55-109:25] Finding unseen roads: power lines running off, markers on the roadside, hills hide intersections.
- [119:15-121:49] Lost: speed does not match the road, an unexpected stop sign, or no cars behind. Pull over where safe; the car one minute behind either shows up or you are off course; start the stopwatch when you turn around; compare with the order of start (e.g. the next car is 10 min behind, so you are 10 min late). Ten minutes is not makeable at 45-50 mph: write the leg off, insert yourself 30 seconds behind a car that is on course, lose that leg at the checkpoint, then run the next leg cleanly 30 s behind the car ahead.
- [81:34-82:38] Jeff's "dirty little secret": "90 to 95% of everything we give you is free": if you start on time, stay on course and drive the speeds, you master 90-95% of it; only 5 to 10% is turns, stops and "the occasional tractor".
- [53:51-54:51] Rookies: "your goal is to finish within your minute. Seconds come later." Do not chase a 3 s correction; compare the chart at left (simple) with the one on the right (25 years of refinement): the simple one is enough to do well.

### Performance chart procedure (2024 version)
- [47:11-48:41] Straight level road 0.5 mile (1 mile even better), little traffic, safe turn-arounds, room to reach 50 before the start; a course that takes about 40 s at 50 mph; mark ends with cones/flag; shorter for low speeds; run entering at 15, 20, 25 ... 50.
- [48:41] At least four runs at each speed; record times; average = base time.
- [49:43] Acceleration loss = time from a standing start minus base time at that speed (do 4 or 5 runs).
- [51:17-51:47] Stop and go: enter at speed, stop and immediately accelerate again, two or three times inside the course; (total - base) / number of stop-and-gos = loss per stop and go; minus acceleration loss = deceleration loss.
- [52:18-53:20] Turns: slow to 15 at each turn, then accelerate to the out-speed.
- [50:15] Rental/modern-car teams in Michigan use the same process; no measured distance needed.
- [53:51] One of the chat veterans uses a spreadsheet with percentage relationships among speeds ("not rookie level").

### Time allowances and making up time
- [115:38-117:43] Time allowance: situation beyond your control (train, blocked road, official directions). Driver pulls over out of traffic; navigator runs the stopwatch; when you can go safely and at speed, restart; navigator determines the delay in 10 s increments (may speed up or slow down to hit it) and files; "two time allowance sheets are stapled to the back of your route instructions: pull one off, fill it out" and hand it to the next checkpoint official "at the lunch stop or at the end of the day".
- [117:12] Not allowed: flat tire, out of gas, car breakdown, missed turn (five miles and back).
- [117:43-119:15] 10 % rule: lost 5 s on a 30 mph road: go 33 for 50 s; if the speed changes mid-correction, apply 10 % to the new speed; also works to lose time (leave early with cross traffic, or 10 % over to pass a slow vehicle then 10 % under).

### Start procedure and order of start
- [04:44-06:14] Brad's priorities, in order: have fun and make friends; try not to get lost ("if you follow the instructions, even if you're not exactly on time, you're gonna get there"); third, be "dead nuts on time". About 150 teams, one third rookies (about 50 rookie teams of about 156 [146:55]).

- [53:47-54:18] Official start is not at the hotel or on the calibration run; the field is taken to quiet two-lane roads; the start is a sign at the roadside ("speed limit 35"). - [54:18-54:49] "If it says speed limit 35 you don't start at zero right on the time; you have to leave early. If it takes you 15 seconds to get to 30... leave 15 seconds before your official start time."

- [58:56-59:27] Start point is in the middle of nowhere; verify you are a minute behind the car in front; leave a couple of seconds early for acceleration.
- [70:49-72:22] "There's nobody there to tell you to go; no race official, nobody waving a flag, no checkpoint crew. It's just that sign... you and the clock and your order of start." - [102:16-103:18] At a restart queue, "this is also a good opportunity to watch what second everybody else leaves"; use it only as a check; "don't change your second because there might have been a checkpoint in between"; if a grand champion leaves at a given second that is a good sign.

### Calibration run and speedometer (Timewise)
- [24:56] Calibration is the first performance thing every day; day to day variation exists; "we thought our speedometer was right on and the next day it's fractionally off."
- [25:27-26:30] Timewise is the unit; "everybody should have theirs by now"; mount it where you can remove it daily (do not build it into the dash); high on the dash.
- [26:30-27:32] Power from a separate battery if the car makes electrical pulses (motorcycle/tractor battery, charge nightly) or wire to the ignition (comes on with the key); do not turn off the car while rolling or the needle stays at that speed; unscrew the face to reset to zero, which "takes longer than you want".
- [27:32-28:32] Initial factor: five rotations of the wheel measured with a chalk mark, total inches to the nearest tenth = the factor; set on the back wheels with a small screwdriver; mark top and bottom on the back ("I once had the speedometer upside down").
- [28:32-29:02] Daily: pull it out, turn it round, read the factor, adjust; read the number back a couple of times; flashlight in mouth.
- [29:02-30:34] Calibration 35-40 min of consistent speed (practice sample about 28 min); "factor divided by 3600: our factor is 4057, which gives about 1.13, so for every second I am off I change about one click; at 9 s I make it 10 clicks." The correction with a calculator "you can do when you're first running... when you get to the race event, but you can't do it when you're on the clock; manual on the clock." New factor = correct/actual x factor.
- [31:06-31:38] Magnets on the left front wheel (closest to the center line, matching how the director measured the route); mount the probe away from heat; magnets held with JB Weld (hardware store magnets); put magnets on the spare and some on all wheels.
- [77:29-78:00] Sample calibration: starts at a "Speed Limit 65" sign on the right; driver exactly 50 mph.
- [78:30-79:31] Interval and cumulative are printed (next sign in about 4 min, then 3 min; "elapsed time 7 minutes 19.7 seconds" printed; you might read 19.6 or 19.5); record the split in Column D; "I'm not trying to match that; I'm comparing it to the optimal time."
- [80:03-80:34] Driver helps with "mark": turn head to the sign, say "mark" when the two posts of the sign are parallel; for overhead signs use the bridge abutment; helpful for left-side signs.
- [81:05-81:37] Look for a steady progression of earlier or later; if traffic stops you, stop and restart at the next sign; add the printed intervals yourself to make a new cumulative.
- [82:09-83:11] Driver watches traffic and lane endings; if you must slow to merge, "you're going to blow her time".
- [83:11-84:44] The end of the calibration: total 28m43.2s; whatever you differ by "is your error for a period of 28 minutes; double that to see how many seconds early or late you are in an hour"; example "3 seconds early in the hour, every 20 minutes correct a second."
- [85:15] Off-the-clock after the hourglass; usually a gas station; top up fuel at every opportunity; set the factor; then to the timed start.
- [122:52-123:24] Don't give the driver feedback during the calibration: "No. As a driver, my best deal of the day is holding that speed exactly."
- [84:44] Driver must know when you are off the clock.

### Stops and pauses

- [112:33-113:35] Cross traffic: leave early if five gravel trucks come ("put those seconds in your pocket"); bleed them off at the next stop.

## B4. Teaching points for Dad (driver-facing) and navigator best practices (2026, verbatim-ish)

- [09:19] "When the navigator tells you to do something, don't argue... Don't change anything; let the navigator tell you what speed you should be driving."

# PART C - Cross-video synthesis

## C1. Top findings (relative to the documents)

1. Time allowance in 2026 is a web/app form (car number, 4-digit password, phone number, leg, instruction numbers, 10-s multiples, window 15 minutes, twice a day, red "done" prints scorecard). The 2024 "paper sheets stapled to the back" is obsolete.
2. Callout protocol (answers Q23): navigator states speed, driver repeats and periodically says "holding NN", navigator confirms; full countdown for stops "coming in at 20, out 35, holding for nine... rock back 9...1 go"; driver says "mark" when sign posts line up; stopwatch hit at rock-back.
3. Do not give the driver early/late feedback during the calibration run (both videos): navigator does the math afterwards.
4. Calibration math is confirmed: correct/actual x factor, factor/3600 clicks per second per hour, error x2 = per hour on a ~28-min run, and a manual correction schedule ("1 s per 5 min" or "1 s per 20 min") when you do not adjust the speedometer (important for Josh's stock speedometer).
5. 2026 adds a 55 mph assigned speed (anywhere, including warm-up/calibration/transit), 48 mph is common, chart rows 10 to 55; both contradict the sim's 25-50 multiples-of-5 default.
6. Start procedure: no official release, roadside sign start, first position is +1 minute, leave early by the acceleration loss; do not pull up early; check the cars around you. Cars are one minute apart, so the car ahead/behind is the only early/late sanity check.
7. Pre-read annotations to teach: carry the speed to the top of each page, write the speed in blank boxes, highlight "comes quick", pre-write turn/stop chart losses ("-2.3", "10.2"), write numbered exact checkpoint times in Column D, check "page n of N".
8. Lost-recovery and checkpoint-reset doctrine: stopwatch at the turnaround, double it; rejoin 30 s behind a known car; leg is thrown away; don't tailgate at a checkpoint; checkpoints do not forgive but reset.
9. Speed changes: split the sign (cross at the midpoint), change at the drawn sign, building = reference only (Jeff) vs REG "landmark"; timed hold: "virtual split" 2 s early.
10. 2024 oddities contradict the documents: one wheel revolution for the factor (HB: five); 2026 Bluetooth headsets (REG: wired/tube); 2024 paper TA sheets (REG: phone/web).
