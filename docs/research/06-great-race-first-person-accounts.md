## 1. A typical day from the navigator's seat (as far as the sources establish it)

**Pre-event: rookie seminar, trophy run.**
- Mike Roberts (rookie navigator, 2021, 1968 Camaro SS, driver Ken): "On Thursday, June 17, Ken and Mike drove the Camaro downtown in rush hour traffic and attended a rookie seminar with Bill Croker" (paraphrase of blog). Source: https://www.mikerobertsfurniture.com/blog/tag/Great+Race (via search summary).
- Team Hagerty 2018 describes a "Trophy Run (Day 0)" that is scored like a competition day; they scored 42 s on it. Source: https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/
- Hagerty's 2019 duo (Brad Phillips / Jen Murray) prepared by running a shorter regional rally first: "The two drove a 1966 Volkswagen Beetle in May's Rally Round the Erie Canal as preparation, and Phillips trained Jen as a new navigator, and they finished first in their class in the Trophy Run and fourth in class for the event, even picking up a couple of perfect scores (Aces)." Source: https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/great-race-duo/

**Morning: calibration run, then instructions ~20 minutes before start.**
- "Each daily beginning stage of the Great Race includes a Tire Warm-up, followed by a Speedometer Calibration Run." Source (search summary attributed to the Great Race 101 / Randy Rundle result set): https://www.greatrace.com/docs/Great_Race_101.pdf ; https://www.underhoodservice.com/?p=99496
- "Navigators are given a very detailed set of instructions 20 minutes before each day's start and must follow the instructions exactly, with typical instructions such as 'turn right at the stop sign, go exactly 25 mph for 40 seconds, then increase to 45 mph and turn right at the first paved road.'" Source (search summary; result set included): https://www.mymcmedia.org/the-great-race-the-coolest-car-event-you-never-heard-of-part-1/ ; https://www.greatrace.com/faq/
- "Each day the driver and navigator team receives a set of course instructions that indicate every turn, speed change, stop, and start that the team must make throughout the day (usually 220 to 250 such instructions per day)." Source: https://www.greatrace.com/docs/Great_Race_101.pdf (same wording also surfaced from https://www.greatrace.com/faq/ and https://fuelcurve.com/the-great-race/).
- Implication: a navigator has roughly 20 minutes to read and annotate 220-250 instructions before the first car leaves; that is ~5 seconds per instruction if read once, so pre-marking is necessarily partial and the day is run largely "cold." (Inference from the two figures above; not a quote.)

**Checkpoints.**
- "Along the course route there will be from 4 to 7 checkpoints recording the exact time that the team passes that point. The locations of the checkpoints are unknown to the entrants, but occur at the most inopportune places." Source: https://www.greatrace.com/docs/Great_Race_101.pdf
- Regulations: "Two types of Checkpoints are used: Timing Checkpoints and Observation Checkpoints. Timing Checkpoints may be located anywhere along the Race Route, and may be on either side of the Race Route." Source: Event Regulations (c) 2026, https://bloximages.chicago2.vip.townnews.com/greatrace.com/content/tncms/assets/v3/editorial/c/dd/cdd7fcee-d4f6-43ba-8535-064d9ce6112e/6931b4f9a700a.pdf.pdf
- Team Hagerty 2018, Day 5: "the rally master put out 12 checkpoints (compared to the usual 6-7), and the navigator had to constantly monitor timing as they rounded each corner to avoid being caught unprepared." (paraphrase) Source: https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/
- Mike Roberts 2021 refers to the day in "legs" ("a good first leg with only 2 seconds of error, and Leg 2 being mistake free resulting in 5 seconds early"), i.e., a leg = the segment from one checkpoint (or start) to the next timing checkpoint, and each leg has its own score. Source: https://www.mikerobertsfurniture.com/blog/tag/Great+Race

**Lunch stop.**
- "at lunch the cars roll into a small town destination, where local car clubs and crowds have gathered to see the cars come in, stop for 45 minutes for lunch, then roll out, one a minute." Source (search summary; result set): https://fuelcurve.com/the-great-race/ ; https://www.stpaulqc.org/blog/a-vw-bugs-great-race . "Lunch is also provided for the driver/navigator teams." Source: https://www.greatrace.com/faq/ (via search summary).
- Starting interval: cars leave "one a minute" (above). Curbside Classic documented the 2021 Rolla, Missouri lunch stop with photos: https://www.curbsideclassic.com/blog/motorsports/cc-motorsports-the-2021-hemmings-motor-news-great-race-lunch-stop-in-rolla-missouri/ (content unread).

**Route character.**
- "The course avoids timed segments on interstate highways, opting instead for scenic local, county, and state highways whenever possible." Source: https://www.greatrace.com/faq/ (via search summary).
- Nine competition days; $50,000 first prize; motto "To Finish is to Win". Source: https://www.greatrace.com/faq/ ; https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/great-race-2021-the-green-dragon-rides-again/ (via search summary).

**Scoring at day's end.**
- "The score for each team is the result of the team's ability to follow the designated course instructions precisely, with every second off the perfect time (early or late) at each checkpoint being a penalty point." Source: https://www.greatrace.com/docs/Great_Race_101.pdf
- Daily results are posted and reported by division (Rookie, Sportsman, Expert, Grand Champion, X-Cup); e.g. 2017 Day 3 news item reports per-day scores and division standings. Source: https://www.greatrace.com/newsite/news/page/26

## 2. Direct accounts: hardest parts, mistakes, equipment, practice

### Mike Roberts, rookie navigator 2021 (1968 Camaro SS; driver Ken) and navigator 2024 (Kieley/Roberts team)
Source: https://www.mikerobertsfurniture.com/blog/tag/Great+Race and https://www.mikerobertsfurniture.com/blog/2024/7/13/2024-great-race (search summaries; full posts unread).
- Rookie onboarding: "Mike Roberts knew next to nothing about the Great Race initially but learned over the following weeks and months that he was to be the navigator." 2021 route: San Antonio to Greenville SC "via Oklahoma, Missouri, Kentucky, West Virginia, Virginia, and North Carolina over nine days." First competition stage Saturday June 19, 2021, San Antonio -> San Marcos -> Temple.
- Day-1 wrong direction: "Instead of going North, they were going South, and it took them nearly 10 minutes to notice the error and turn around." They "finished the first day with 21 seconds of error in the timed runs." Also: "a good first leg with only 2 seconds of error, and Leg 2 being mistake free resulting in 5 seconds early." (Note: a 10-minute wrong-way excursion did not translate to a 600-second day; this implies it happened on an untimed transit section or before a checkpoint with a large time cushion. UNVERIFIED which.)
- 2024, Day 1 (June 22): "the team scored six seconds total and finished 6th overall and 1st in their sportsman class." Final: "11th overall and 3rd in their class." "They passed the Grand Champions behind them three times!"
- 2024 train trap: "a major mistake on the first leg where the navigator missed timing when race cars stopped for a train crossing, resulting in a 48-second penalty leg." This is the single most concrete trap anecdote found: a railroad crossing forces an unplanned stop; the navigator must time the stop and add it back (or apply the event's documented procedure for a blocked crossing) and did not.

### Team Hagerty, rookie division, 2018 (Buffalo NY -> Halifax NS)
Source: https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/ (search summary).
- Trophy Run (Day 0): 42 s; "the best rookie team scored 13 seconds, while the Grand Champion Division scored just under 2 seconds."
- After Day 2: 34 s cumulative(?) "putting them 31st overall and second in the rookie division." Day 3 (Troy NY -> Burlington VT): 46 s. Day 4 (Burlington -> Gardiner ME): 20 s, "42nd overall at the halfway point." Day 5: 12 checkpoints instead of the usual 6-7; navigator "had to constantly monitor timing as they rounded each corner to avoid being caught unprepared."
- Takeaway for a rookie: day scores of 20-46 s are normal; 13 s is a very good rookie day; sub-2 s is Grand Champion territory on a trophy run.

### Brad Phillips and Jen Murray, Hagerty 2019 (San Rafael CA start, 2,500 miles)
Source: https://www.hagerty.com/media/motorsports/great-race-duo/ (search summary).
- Practice method: a regional rally (Rally Round the Erie Canal) in a 1966 VW Beetle; driver trained the new navigator; result 1st in class trophy run, 4th in class overall, "a couple of perfect scores (Aces)."
- Quote: they "learned a lot about how to communicate with each other"; "it takes two people working together – driver and navigator – to be successful."
- Equipment/tech vocabulary: an "Ace" = a checkpoint hit at exactly 0 s error.

Source: https://www.greatrace.com/rookie/larry-read-navigator-2015-rookie ; https://www.greatrace.com/rookie-testimonials/larry-read/article_e27f718c-8df9-4b60-81d2-e60f8aa0f790.html (search summaries).

### Speedometer calibration procedure (equipment detail)
- "With tires fully inflated, you measure 5 rotations of the wheel on the ground, and total inches to the nearest tenth is your factor to enter on the back of your Timewise using a small screwdriver to adjust the dials." Source (search summary attributed to the Randy Rundle / Great Race 101 result set): https://www.underhoodservice.com/?p=99496 ; https://www.brakeandfrontend.com/?p=99496 ; https://fifthaveinternetgarage.blogspot.com/2015/10/the-great-racehow-it-works.html
- Regulations: "one operating analog speedometer approved by Great Race as defined here is allowed in each entry vehicle. Calibration divisions on the face must read no closer than one MPH increments." Source: Event Regulations (c) 2026 PDF (URL above).
- "The official speedometer of the Great Race is the Timewise 825 electronic speedometer, which was specifically developed for Great Race type rallies." Source (search summary): https://www.greatrace.com/blog/life-before-the-timewise-speedometer/article_141f8207-df06-4a3a-b344-b375aaeb1df6.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Race_(classic_rally)
- Wayne Bell "was the driving force behind the Timewise speedometer that all Great Racers have used since the early 2000s"; five-time champion as navigator (1989, 1990, 1992, 2000, 2003), 30+ Great Races. Source: https://www.greatrace.com/news/wayne-bell.html
- Note for simulator: the official speedometer reads in 1-mph increments only (by rule), so the driver holds a needle, not a digital value; the calibration factor is a wheel-rollout measurement, which is why tire pressure/warm-up matters and why a morning calibration run is part of every day.

### Allowed / prohibited navigator equipment
- "Navigators cannot use GPSs, maps, radios, smartphones, digital watches, computers or other navigational devices, but competitors may carry an analog stopwatch." Source (search summary): https://www.mymcmedia.org/the-great-race-crosses-maryland-part-2/ ; https://www.mymcmedia.org/the-great-race-the-coolest-car-event-you-never-heard-of-part-1/
- Wikipedia: "vehicles must use original factory parts and modern navigational aids like GPS are prohibited"; "street-legal vintage automobiles at least 45 years old"; "a precision pace race, not a high-speed race, with points awarded on the accuracy of a driver and navigator to match a time and average speed over a predetermined course." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Race_(classic_rally)
- Veteran navigator Ron Rowland has written application notes for an analog "Rally Clock With Rotating Bezel" and an "Analog Stopwatch With Countdown Bezel" (titles only; content unread). Source: https://ronrowland.com/rally-clock-with-rotating-bezel-application-notes/ ; https://ronrowland.com/analog-stopwatch-with-countdown-bezel-application-notes/ . He also wrote "Rally Rule #14: Sanity Check Your Charts" and "Rally Rule #11: 'Right' Is Not 'Correct'" (https://ronrowland.com/rally-rule-14-sanity-check-your-charts/ ; https://ronrowland.com/rally/), whose titles indicate two recurring failure modes: trusting a chart with an arithmetic error, and confusing a "right" turn instruction with "correct" route or similar wording trap. **UNVERIFIED** interpretation of titles.

Source: https://www.greatrace.com/rally_details/awards/
- The Fredettes (father Jeff driving, son Eric navigating, Beecher IL) won the 2018 (35th) Great Race, Buffalo NY -> Halifax NS, "in their flathead-V8-powered 1933 Ford pickup truck." ## 6. Traps and mistakes (specific anecdotes found)

1. **Wrong direction out of the start.** Rookie navigator Mike Roberts, 2021 Day 1: "Instead of going North, they were going South, and it took them nearly 10 minutes to notice the error and turn around." Day total still 21 s. Source: https://www.mikerobertsfurniture.com/blog/tag/Great+Race
2. **Railroad crossing stop not timed.** Roberts, 2024 Day 1 leg 1: "missed timing when race cars stopped for a train crossing, resulting in a 48-second penalty leg." Source: https://www.mikerobertsfurniture.com/blog/2024/7/13/2024-great-race
3. **Checkpoint-density surprise.** Team Hagerty 2018 Day 5: 12 checkpoints vs the usual 6-7; a team that relaxes between expected checkpoints gets caught. Source: https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/
4. **Checkpoints "at the most inopportune places," on either side of the road.** Great Race 101 and the regulations both stress this: https://www.greatrace.com/docs/Great_Race_101.pdf ; Event Regulations (c) 2026 PDF.
5. **Observation checkpoints that require a stop.** "Failure to stop at an Observation Checkpoint may result in a penalty or disqualification." Event Regulations (c) 2026 PDF.
6. **Charts with errors / misread instruction wording.** Ron Rowland's "Rally Rule #14: Sanity Check Your Charts" and "Rally Rule #11: 'Right' Is Not 'Correct'" (titles) https://ronrowland.com/rally-rule-14-sanity-check-your-charts/ ; https://ronrowland.com/rally/ . Content UNVERIFIED.
7. **Instruction phrasing that mixes time and speed.** The canonical example: "turn right at the stop sign, go exactly 25 mph for 40 seconds, then increase to 45 mph and turn right at the first paved road." Source: https://www.mymcmedia.org/the-great-race-the-coolest-car-event-you-never-heard-of-part-1/ (via search summary). The navigator must simultaneously time a 40-s hold, watch for the speed change, and identify "first paved road" (ignoring gravel roads).
8. **Rush-hour traffic in the host city** on the day before the start (Roberts 2021, San Antonio). Source: https://www.mikerobertsfurniture.com/blog/tag/Great+Race
9. **Mechanical failure -> sweep tow.** "the sweep team towed participants back to the hotel" (Riley's Road Show 2025). Source: https://www.onallcylinders.com/2025/06/21/rileys-road-show-tales-from-the-great-race/
10. **Extra passenger penalty** (5 s/stage, occupant 13+). Event Regulations (c) 2026 PDF. Minor, but shows every rule is expressed in seconds.

## Implications for a training simulator (scenarios drawn from the accounts above)

Each item: situation -> what went wrong in the source -> what the learner should practice.

1. **Twenty-minute instruction read.** Hand the learner 220-250 instructions 20 minutes before a start (sources: Great Race 101; mymcmedia Part 1). Failure mode: no time to pre-mark everything. Practice: triage marking (speed changes and turns first, pauses second), and running "cold" from an unmarked page.
2. **Wrong-way start.** Reproduce Roberts 2021: an ambiguous first turn out of a hotel lot into a city grid; the car heads south instead of north. Practice: a 60-second "sanity check" after the first three instructions (landmark confirmation), and recovery arithmetic for turning around (how to re-enter the course at the right time or accept the loss).
3. **Train crossing stop.** Reproduce Roberts 2024: barrier drops, 90-second wait, then traffic. Failure: stop not timed, 48 s leg. Practice: start a second stopwatch at the moment of stopping, log the delay, and re-plan speed/time to the next checkpoint or apply the event's delay procedure.
4. **Checkpoint density surprise.** Reproduce Team Hagerty 2018 Day 5: 12 checkpoints in a day after the learner has been trained on 4-7. Failure: relaxing after the expected number is passed. Practice: treat every corner as a possible checkpoint; never "coast" the end of a leg.
5. **Checkpoint on the far side of the road / hidden after a bend.** From Great Race 101 ("most inopportune places") and regulations ("either side of the Race Route"). Practice: holding speed through blind corners and town entries rather than slowing to look.
6. **Observation checkpoint requiring a stop.** From regulations. Practice: recognizing the checkpoint type and performing the stop without losing the running time.
7. **Time-plus-speed-plus-landmark instruction.** "go exactly 25 mph for 40 seconds, then increase to 45 mph and turn right at the first paved road." Practice: timing a hold while calling a speed change while filtering landmark qualifiers (paved vs gravel).
8. **Performance chart with an arithmetic error.** From Larry Read (charts built with a mentor) and Rowland's "Sanity Check Your Charts." Practice: spotting a chart row that is implausible (e.g., deceleration time-loss larger than acceleration) before it is used at speed.
9. **Morning calibration run.** From Great Race 101 / Rundle: tire warm-up then calibration; factor from five wheel rotations entered on the Timewise. Practice: running the calibration leg, comparing indicated vs. true time, and deciding whether to adjust the factor or compensate mentally (speedo over/under-reads at 35 mph).
10. **Score-interpretation drills.** Show the learner the scoring tables in section 4: what 2 s, 13 s, 21 s, 42 s and 48 s days/legs mean in standings terms (Grand Champion, best rookie, average rookie day, one blown leg). Practice: deciding, mid-day, when to protect the route rather than chase the second ("a wrong turn can cost far more than a small timing error," greatrace.com/driver-navigator-basics).
11. **Lunch restart.** From the 45-minute lunch and one-per-minute roll-out. Practice: resetting all timing at the restart, re-reading the first afternoon instructions during lunch, and leaving on the correct minute.
12. **Nine-day fatigue arc.** From "To Finish is to Win" framing and Jennie Caldwell's "didn't do very well ... had a great time." Practice: late-day and late-week sessions with degraded attention; emphasize routines ("consistent routines" per driver-navigator-basics) that survive fatigue.
13. **Pre-war car module (needs more sourcing).** Pre-war Fords dominate the 2025 podium (greatrace.com/rally_details/awards) and the age factor helps them; the simulator should model slower acceleration/braking and a needle-type 1-mph speedometer (regulations), but first-person flathead-specific behaviour is UNVERIFIED pending reading the Model T forum thread, Hagerty Green Dragon series, and Fredette interviews.
14. **Breakdown decision tree.** From Riley's Road Show (sweep tows to hotel) and regulations (support vehicles wait for sweep). Practice: what to do when the car falters mid-leg: push on to the checkpoint or stop safely; how a DNF day affects standings (penalty figure UNVERIFIED).
15. **Communication protocol.** From Phillips/Murray and driver-navigator-basics. Practice: a fixed call-and-response format for speed changes and turns, with countdowns, so the driver never has to look at the instructions.
