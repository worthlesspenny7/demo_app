## 1. Official rules: equipment allowed / prohibited

Source for this section unless noted: the Great Race Event Regulations PDF (© 2026) via search summaries — https://bloximages.chicago2.vip.townnews.com/greatrace.com/content/tncms/assets/v3/editorial/c/dd/cdd7fcee-d4f6-43ba-8535-064d9ce6112e/6931b4f9a700a.pdf.pdf

### 1.1 Odometer — must be covered or removed (NOT visible)
- VERBATIM-LIKELY: "All competing vehicle odometers must be covered or completely removed prior to the start of the event, and the cover must be applied in such a way that the odometer will be rendered unreadable." (Regulations PDF, above)
- FAQ wording: "GPS or computers are not permitted and odometers are taped over." https://www.greatrace.com/faq/ ; repeated at https://cokertire.com/great-race-101
- Rally Equipment page: "The factory odometer has to be covered as per rules; the speedometer is optional." https://www.greatrace.com/bill-croker/rally-equipment.html
- Answer to the question "Is the car's odometer allowed to be visible?": **No.** It must be covered/removed so it is unreadable. The Great Race is run entirely on speed x time, not distance (see section 2).

### 1.2 Speedometer
- "One operating analog speedometer approved by Great Race is allowed in each entry vehicle. Most Great Racers use a Timewise Speedometer." (search summary attributing to the Regulations PDF and https://www.greatrace.com/bill-croker/rally-equipment.html)
- "You can do the rally with the vehicle's stock speedometer but you will be much happier with a special speedometer that can be calibrated" https://www.greatrace.com/bill-croker/rally-equipment.html
- "The official speedometer of the Great Race is the Time wise 825 electronic speedometer" (about $1,000; installed by gluing two small magnets to one wheel, a bracket for the electronic pickup, mounted for viewing by the driver) https://fifthaveinternetgarage.blogspot.com/2015/10/the-great-racehow-it-works.html ; also https://cokertire.com/great-race-101
- Timewise 825: "meets the strict size and appearance guidelines for use in The Great Race"; "To the casual observer, the Timewise 825 appears to be an analog speedometer... Internally, however, the 825 is fully digital"; "microprocessor driven stepper-motor"; "fine point analog dial needle and maximum allowable faceplate diameter provide interpretable resolution of 0.05 mph"; "The four-digit calibration factor is the distance, in inches, traveled over 10 pulses from the transducer... resolution to 1/10 of an inch"; "used since 1999 in The Great Race by more than 90% of all contestants." http://www.timewise.us/products/825.html
- Interpretation: the speedometer must LOOK analog (needle), may be electronically driven, and is calibrated to the rally master's measuring instruments on a daily calibration run (section 4). The speedometer has NO visible odometer function in use.

### 1.3 Time-of-day clock (primary timepiece)
- VERBATIM-LIKELY: "One mechanical or quartz-crystal analog time-of-day watch or clock is the primary timepiece. The watch/clock may have analog hour, minute, and second hand, and must not have stopwatch, compensating, calculating, or split-action functions, or digital readout." (Regulations PDF)
- VERBATIM-LIKELY: "Any clock or watch that is self-correcting or self-synchronizing by using radio, cellular, or satellite/GPS technology is prohibited." (Regulations PDF)
- Practice: analog clock mounted in the center of the dash, continuous-sweep second hand, with every second numbered around the dial ("Sawtooth Rally Clock" mentioned) https://www.greatrace.com/bill-croker/rally-equipment.html ; https://fifthaveinternetgarage.blogspot.com/2015/10/the-great-racehow-it-works.html ; Ron Rowland on rotating-bezel rally clocks: https://ronrowland.com/rally-clock-with-rotating-bezel-application-notes/
- Great Race's own clocks are "named for the late Rex Gardner" https://www.greatrace.com/news/great-race-clocks-named-for-the-late-rex-gardner.html (content not read).

### 1.4 Wristwatches
- VERBATIM-LIKELY: "The driver and navigator may each wear one approved analog wristwatch without stopwatch function, and a digital wristwatch is prohibited." (Regulations PDF)

### 1.5 Stopwatch — ONE per team, digital OR analog allowed
- VERBATIM-LIKELY: "Each Team may have one approved digital or analog stopwatch; the stopwatch may also have split-action, time-of-day, date, and alarm functions." (Regulations PDF)
- Answer to "Are digital stopwatches allowed?": **Yes — one per team, digital or analog, split (lap) function permitted.** But only one stopwatch; the time-of-day clock may not have stopwatch functions, and wristwatches may not have stopwatch functions.
- Practice: navigators hang the stopwatch around the neck and keep it in hand "so that they can instantly start/stop it"; they also "use the accuracy of the stopwatch for 'time of day' starts during the rally, which eliminates the possible error of reading the minute hand wrong on the analog clock." https://www.greatrace.com/bill-croker/rally-equipment.html
- Ron Rowland uses an analog stopwatch with a countdown bezel: https://ronrowland.com/analog-stopwatch-with-countdown-bezel-application-notes/

### 1.6 Calculators, computers, phones, GPS, radios
- VERBATIM-LIKELY: "The use of a cellular telephone's clock, calculator, or mapping/positioning functions is prohibited at all times." (Regulations PDF)
- VERBATIM-LIKELY: "Any calculating device, timing device, measuring device, recording device, or radio (including one that receives WWV or CHU) is prohibited, except as specifically allowed above." (Regulations PDF)
- VERBATIM-LIKELY: "Digital cameras and video cameras which have a digital clock are permissible, but use of the digital clock is unsportsmanlike conduct." (Regulations PDF)
- FAQ: "GPS or computers are not permitted" https://www.greatrace.com/faq/
- Press summary: "no GPSs, maps, radios, smart phones, digital watches, computers or other navigational devices are allowed" https://www.mymcmedia.org/the-great-race-the-coolest-car-event-you-never-heard-of-part-1/
- Answer to "Are calculators allowed?": **No.** The regulations prohibit "any calculating device ... except as specifically allowed above", and the search of the regulations found no clause specifically allowing a calculator; the phone's calculator function is prohibited by name. Navigators work from pre-computed paper tables/charts (speed-time tables, performance/time-loss tables) instead. [Residual uncertainty: the full PDF was not read; if a clause permits a non-programmable calculator it was not surfaced by any search. Mark as HIGH-CONFIDENCE but not verbatim-verified.]
- Pre-computed paper aids are clearly legal in practice: "calibration charts" under a clear plastic lapboard surface https://www.greatrace.com/bill-croker/rally-equipment.html ; "performance tables" and time-loss tables "affix[ed] to their lapboard" https://ronrowland.com/analog-stopwatch-with-countdown-bezel-application-notes/ ; navigator-made "speed charts" https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/

## 2. How course instructions are written

### 2.1 Volume, timing of hand-out, physical form
- "Each day the driver and navigator team receives a set of course instructions that indicate every turn, speed change, stop, and start that the team must make throughout the day (usually 220 to 250 such instructions per day)." https://www.greatrace.com/faq/
- Regulations: "One set of Course Instructions for each Stage will be available to each competing Team each day near the Stage start, exactly 30 minutes before." (Regulations PDF). Older press says 20 minutes: "Navigators are given a very detailed set of instructions 20 minutes before each day's start" https://www.mymcmedia.org/the-great-race-the-coolest-car-event-you-never-heard-of-part-1/ ; 2003 diary: "pick up their instructions 20 minutes before their start time" https://www.greatrace.com/blog/great-race-diary-from-2003/article_5bcad477-d987-5a11-b73e-3a2e039fd27e.html. Current rule = 30 min.
- "about a half hour before a leg of the race starts, the team is given 18 to 20 pages of instructions detailing every turn, every speed change and every stop." (search summary; source among https://www.greatrace.com/ pages / https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-commercial-appeal/20130627/281801396539747 — exact page UNVERIFIED)
- "The instructions are best described as cryptic, and navigators work quickly to decipher the pages and prepare their drivers for the day's challenges... Road names are rare, stopwatches required, and speed changes abundant." https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/
- Navigators 3-hole-punch the sheets into a lapboard ring binder so pages flip https://www.greatrace.com/bill-croker/rally-equipment.html

### 2.3 Great Race style vs typical SCCA/TSD style
- Great Race: no odometer, no distances. Every instruction is a landmark-triggered action plus an assigned speed, or a timed element (pause N seconds; hold speed for N seconds/minutes). The navigator's only instruments are the speedometer, the clock and one stopwatch. Perfect time between landmarks = rally master's measured distance / assigned speed, but the competitor never sees the distance; the competitor sees only speed (and sometimes interval time). Sources: https://www.greatrace.com/faq/ ("every turn, speed change, stop, and start"), Regulations PDF (odometer unreadable; Column C interval times), https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/ ("road names are rare, stopwatches required, and speed changes abundant").
- Rally-master's model: "The Great Race and similar rallies have a basic assumption that a car can stop instantaneously and accelerate to speed instantaneously. Of course, your car cannot do this." https://www.hagerty.com/media/archived/great-race-2019-1100-miles-in-1917-peerless-green-dragon/ — i.e., the perfect time is computed as if the car is at the new speed the instant it passes the landmark; stops contribute only their printed pause.
- Typical SCCA-style TSD (for contrast): instructions reference mileages and are driven with an odometer/rally computer; speed changes are "CAST" ("Change Average Speed To"): "CAST 35 at 'Yield'"; turns "Right at T", "Left at STOP"; pauses are given in hundredths of a minute: "A 'Pause 50' says to figure in a delay of half a minute (timing is given in cents). A Pause 100 indicates a full one minute delay." Road Rally Handbook https://www.roadrallyhandbook.com/pdf/RRH.pdf (also https://www.scribd.com/document/721999193/RRH); JCNA rally chapters https://www.jcna.com/library/rally/chapter1.html , https://www.jcna.com/library/rally/chapter2.html ; SCCA novice tutorial http://www.indyscca.org/RallyFiles/Novice_3.pdf
- Generic TSD guidance on stops (not Great Race-specific; relevant to modeling): "the given speed continues through stop signs, through traffic, and through corners. If you are traveling at 40 mph and encounter a stop sign, you can lose up to 30 seconds slowing down, coming to a stop, and reaccelerating" (search summary, source among the TSD tutorials above — UNVERIFIED which). "for most cars... these speeds require about one second per 10 mph to reach or burn off. That is, it will take about 4 seconds to stop from 40, and about 2 seconds to accelerate to 20." (search summary attributed to JCNA chapter 1 / zone8 Rallying for Beginners https://www.zone8.org/assets/docs/Rallying_for_beginners.pdf — UNVERIFIED which).
- Great Race pause arithmetic as practiced: "if you use a performance table indicating a loss of 6.5 seconds for the stop/start speeds, then rotate the bezel to 8.5 seconds (15 minus 6.5)." https://ronrowland.com/analog-stopwatch-with-countdown-bezel-application-notes/ — i.e., the navigator shortens the printed 15-s pause by the car's measured decel+accel loss so the net effect equals the rally master's instantaneous model.
- Great Race seconds, not cents: all Great Race timing found is in seconds (15-second pause, "40 seconds", scores in seconds). SCCA uses hundredths of minutes. (FAQ; ronrowland.com; RRH)

### 2.4 Time-loss ("speed chart" / performance table) practice
- "The navigator creates speed charts that calculate the time lost for accelerations and turns, and marks the directions, making alterations to fit the car they are navigating." https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/
- How to build: "Make at least 4 runs at each speed and record the times. You need at least 4 runs to get a good average time. If you have a lot of variation, make more runs. You need to be as consistent on every run as possible" https://www.hagerty.com/media/archived/great-race-2019-1100-miles-in-1917-peerless-green-dragon/
- "Knowing the amount of time lost when starting and stopping is vital to keeping your rally team on time. Great Racers spend hours, sometimes days, collecting performance data. Then they meticulously compile all the collected information into a neat little table and affix it to their lapboard." https://ronrowland.com/analog-stopwatch-with-countdown-bezel-application-notes/
- "Methods for determining the amount of time lost include using performance tables and half-speed interval (splits) measurements. Whichever method you prefer, providing a countdown to begin accelerating is critical." (same)
- "The navigator has to not only interpret the sometimes-cryptic instructions, but also properly calculate the time loss incurred by the stated maneuvers." (greatrace.com — Preparing Your Car / Driver-Navigator Basics, https://www.greatrace.com/preparing-your-car)
- "The more consistently the driver accelerates, brakes, turns, and holds speed, the more useful the team's performance charts become." https://www.greatrace.com/driver-navigator-basics
- Sanity-check charts: https://ronrowland.com/rally-rule-14-sanity-check-your-charts/ (content not read).
- Speeds: "Rarely will Great Race competitors reach speeds more than 50 mph." https://fuelcurve.com/the-great-race/

## 3. Scoring

### 3.1 Checkpoints and penalties
- "Along the course route there will be from 4 to 7 checkpoints recording the exact time that the team passes that point. The objective is to arrive at each checkpoint at the correct time, not the fastest. Every second off the perfect time (early or late) at each checkpoint is a penalty point." https://www.greatrace.com/faq/
- Checkpoints are secret/"surprise": "arrive at the surprise checkpoints exactly when the rally master has estimated you will" https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/ ; "five or more secret checkpoints each day" (search summary of greatrace.com news / https://www.mymcmedia.org/the-great-race-crosses-maryland-part-2/)
- Regulations (VERBATIM-LIKELY): "Times recorded at Timing Checkpoints are to the nearest second." "Each team will receive a penalty score based on each second their leg time differs from the perfect time calculated for that leg."
- Two checkpoint types: "Timing Checkpoints and Observation Checkpoints." "Timing Checkpoints may be located anywhere along the Race Route, and each has a timing line identified by a green Day-Glo Timing Checkpoint sign. Your arrival time at the Checkpoint is recorded when your vehicle's front wheels cross the timing line from the correct direction. Do not stop at a Timing Checkpoint." "Observation Checkpoints are included as the final checkpoint of each Stage." "You must stop at an Observation Checkpoint and have your Course Instructions endorsed; failure to stop may result in a penalty or disqualification." (Regulations PDF)
- Leg reset at each checkpoint: "as they cross the timing line, the previous leg ends and the next leg begins, with the start time for the next leg being the same as the arrival time at the checkpoint." (Regulations PDF) — so an error does NOT carry into the next leg; each leg is scored independently from the actual time you crossed the previous line.
- "An entry vehicle must not stop or travel at 5 MPH or less within sight of a Timing Checkpoint" — penalty per V.E.3.a. (Regulations PDF)
- Missed checkpoint: "A vehicle which misses (fails to pass) a Checkpoint or passes a Checkpoint not under its own power will receive the penalty stipulated in V.E.2." "A vehicle which arrives at any checkpoint more than 30 minutes after the computed cumulative perfect time from the most recent Stage Start or Restart, including the final Observation Checkpoint of the Stage at the Finish Line, will be scored as having missed that checkpoint." (Regulations PDF). The numeric value of the missed-checkpoint penalty (V.E.2) was not surfaced — UNVERIFIED.
- Restart: "There is a penalty for any vehicle that leaves a lunch stop or rest stop more than 5 minutes before its specified time." (Regulations PDF)
- "Ace" = a perfect zero at a checkpoint: "A perfectly timed checkpoint earns a team an Ace, with each second early or late equating to one point, and the lowest score wins." https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/ ; teams count Aces as a stat ("4 Aces total", "tied for 3rd place in Aces won") https://www.greatrace.com/john-corey/great-race-post-10-final-this-year.html

### 3.2 Age factor (handicap)
- "For purposes of scoring, the older the vehicle, the better the age factor adjustment the team will receive." "The rules reward older cars by giving a percentage reduction of the teams score based on the age of the vehicle, with the older the model year resulting in a bigger percentage deduction." "the age factor scoring multiplier applies to vehicles 1954 and earlier." https://www.greatrace.com/faq/ ; https://cokertire.com/great-race-101
- Age Factor table values surfaced from the Regulations PDF: **1954 and later = 1.000; 1953 = 0.915; 1940 = 0.850; 1939 = 0.845; 1926 = 0.760; 1925 = 0.750; 1912 = 0.620; 1911 = 0.610.** (Regulations PDF). Intermediate years not surfaced — UNVERIFIED, but the surfaced points imply ~0.005/yr from 1953 back to ~1939 and larger steps (~0.01/yr) in the 1910s–20s. The step from 1954 (1.000) to 1953 (0.915) is a cliff.
- **For a 1939 Ford Deluxe: factor 0.845**, i.e., the team's raw seconds are multiplied by 0.845 (a 15.5% reduction). Example: a raw day of 40 s scores 33.8 s. This is why published final scores carry hundredths (e.g., 0m49.72s) even though checkpoint times are to the nearest second.
- Eligibility link: "A vehicle that because of its power train, suspension, equipment, or other modifications would be considered a post-1974 vehicle for purposes of applying an Age Factor may be accepted for entry on an individual review basis." (Regulations PDF) — i.e., the age factor is judged on the car's effective era, not just title year.

Calibration instructions give signs/landmarks and average speeds between them; "Column C ... contain[s] the interval times required to travel between the signs and landmarks at the assigned speeds." - Start order and spacing: "Cars start one minute apart, so the rally is not a parade, and this makes it more difficult for novices to 'puppy dog' behind expert teams." "On Trophy Run (Stage 0) and Stage 1, the order of start is the 2025 GREAT RACE Grand Champion, followed by Corporate entries sponsored by major Event sponsors, Group I, Group II, Group III." ## 7. What the navigator actually does, minute to minute (published accounts)

- Pre-start (30 min): receive 18–20 pages / 220–250 instructions; read through; mark turns; annotate every stop/pause with the car-specific net pause (printed pause minus time-loss); compute cumulative times where useful ("speed charts"); punch pages into lapboard. https://www.hagerty.com/media/events/team-hagerty-great-race-2018/ ; https://www.greatrace.com/bill-croker/rally-equipment.html ; https://www.greatrace.com/faq/
- Start: leave exactly on official start time (stage start + car number minutes); many use the stopwatch, started on the minute, as the authoritative time-of-day reference instead of reading the analog minute hand. https://www.greatrace.com/bill-croker/rally-equipment.html ; Regulations PDF
- Calibration run (≥15 mi): drive the assigned speeds between landmarks; compare elapsed interval against Column C perfect interval; adjust the Timewise calibration factor (inches per 10 pulses) so speedometer matches the rally master's instruments. (Regulations PDF; http://www.timewise.us/products/825.html)
- Each instruction: navigator reads ahead and calls the next landmark so the driver already knows "what sign, road, or intersection comes next" before the navigator looks down https://www.greatrace.com/driver-navigator-basics ; driver holds the assigned speed to within a fraction of a mph (Timewise resolution 0.05 mph) http://www.timewise.us/products/825.html
- Each stop sign / light: "The most common use of a stopwatch in the Great Race is measuring the pause time at stop signs and traffic lights." Navigator starts the stopwatch at the stop, counts down the shortened pause (e.g., 8.5 s instead of 15 s) and calls "go" so acceleration begins on time: "providing a countdown to begin accelerating is critical." https://ronrowland.com/analog-stopwatch-with-countdown-bezel-application-notes/
- Timed segments: hold speed for printed durations (e.g., "go exactly 25 mph for 40 seconds, then increase to 45 mph") https://www.mymcmedia.org/the-great-race-the-coolest-car-event-you-never-heard-of-part-1/
- Checkpoints: do not slow/stop within sight of a green Day-Glo timing sign; crossing resets the leg clock to actual arrival. Teams can pass checkpoints without noticing them. (Regulations PDF; https://www.greatrace.com/blog/great-race-diary-from-2003/article_5bcad477-d987-5a11-b73e-3a2e039fd27e.html)
- Errors: when off course, "they calculate how much time they've lost and attempt to make it up"; getting one instruction out of sync causes panic and requires a "reverse hack" to re-sync https://www.greatrace.com/blog/great-race-diary-from-2003/article_5bcad477-d987-5a11-b73e-3a2e039fd27e.html . Example of a restart error: "19 seconds late getting to a restart after a refueling stop" https://ronrowland.com/2025-great-race-team-2-graf-rowland/
- Multi-year navigator diaries: https://ronrowland.com/2023-great-race-team-windup-key/ , https://ronrowland.com/2024-great-race-team-supernova/ , https://ronrowland.com/2025-great-race-team-2-graf-rowland/ ; greatrace.com "Navigator Notes" https://www.greatrace.com/archive/navigator-notes/article_c7d5d429-3c3a-5fb1-aaf2-85518a2c7084.html ; John Corey posts https://www.greatrace.com/john-corey/great-race-post-10-final-this-year.html ; Mike Roberts blog https://www.mikerobertsfurniture.com/blog/tag/Great+Race ; Traynor's Travels https://www.traynorstravels.com/blog/the-great-race (contents not read).

## 8. Implications for a training simulator

Concrete things the simulator must model (each traces to a cited fact above):

- No distance information to the player: hide odometer/trip entirely; present only speedometer (analog needle, 0.05 mph-class precision optional), one analog time-of-day clock with sweep second hand, and exactly one stopwatch (digital or analog, with split/lap). (§1.1, §1.3, §1.5)
- No calculator: force the player to work from pre-built paper-style tables (speed→seconds-per-mile is not even useful since distance is hidden; what matters is time-loss tables per speed pair and pause arithmetic). Provide an in-sim "lapboard" where the player can pre-compute and annotate before the start. (§1.6, §2.4)
- Course instructions as a numbered list of landmark-triggered actions + assigned speed, with timed elements in whole seconds (not cents): turn-at-landmark, "speed NN", "pause NN seconds", "hold speed for M:SS", stop signs that imply "stop, pause 15, resume". 220–250 instructions per full day; ~18–20 pages. (§2.1–2.3)
- Rally-master's perfect-time model = instantaneous speed changes at the landmark; stops contribute exactly the printed pause. The car model must have realistic, repeatable acceleration/deceleration curves (for a 1939 Ford: slow accel, long braking) so the player must learn to shorten pauses by the measured loss (e.g., 15 → 8.5 s). (§2.3, §2.4)
- Allow a "performance data" mode where the player can run repeated 0→N and N→0 trials (≥4 runs per speed) and build their own loss table; reward consistency. (§2.4)
- Speedometer calibration: start each day with a ≥15-mile calibration run with landmark intervals and a Column-C perfect interval time; include a small speedometer error the player must detect and dial out via a calibration factor. (§4)
- Starts: official start = stage start time + car number in minutes; one-minute spacing; early departure from lunch/restart (>5 min early) penalized; "Start on time" must be a graded skill (clock reading vs. stopwatch-as-time-of-day technique). (§4, §5)
- Checkpoints: 4–7 secret timing checkpoints per day, visible only as a green sign at the line; arrival recorded to the nearest second when front wheels cross; no stopping/≤5 mph within sight (penalty); crossing resets the leg so errors do not compound; final checkpoint of a stage is an observation checkpoint where you stop. Score = |seconds early or late| summed; display "Ace" for zero. (§3.1)
- Missed-checkpoint handling: >30 min behind cumulative perfect time since last start/restart = missed; off-course/wrong-turn recovery with time-made-up logic ("calculate how much time they've lost and attempt to make it up"). (§3.1, §7)
- Age factor: multiply daily raw seconds by the car's factor (1939 → 0.845; 1954+ → 1.000; sample table in §3.2); show both raw and factored scores with hundredths. (§3.2)
- Divisions and benchmarks: Rookie/Sportsman/Expert/Grand Champion/X-Cup; present targets such as ~2 s/day (champion), ~13 s/day (best rookie on a trophy run), 30–45 s/day (ordinary), and multi-day totals (winner ≈ 50–60 s over 9 stages; rookie winners 1m50s–4m24s). (§3.3, §3.4)
- Event structure: optional Trophy Run (Stage 0, short, tiebreaker) + 9 stages; tire warm-up → calibration → morning legs → lunch restart → afternoon legs → finish observation checkpoint. (§4)
- Navigator workflow scripting: 30-minute pre-read phase; "read ahead and call the next landmark before looking down"; stop-sign countdown ("3-2-1-go") to start acceleration; standardized vocabulary for signs/turns/features. (§5, §7)
- Traffic realism: red lights, slow traffic, and sign-sighting failures as random events the player must absorb with time-loss estimates (the given speed is assumed to continue through traffic). (§2.3)
- Keep speeds realistic: rarely above 50 mph; many 25–45 mph segments on back roads; road names rare, so landmarks are signs, intersections, railroad crossings, pavement changes. (§2.1, §2.4)
