## 1. Software and apps

### 1.1 Event-timing / scoring systems (organizer-side, with a competitor app)

**Richta GPS Checkpoints (Competitor app + Rallymaster app)** — iOS/Android, free.
- Purpose: "automates the timing of competitors in time-speed-distance road rally events with no requirement for traditional checkpoint crews to be physically present at checkpoint locations." The app "monitors your car's location during the rally and records your arrival time at each checkpoint. Once you have arrived at a control, the app will calculate your score for that leg and display it to you." Timing/scoring data is "automatically sent to the organizers." — https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1448298019 and https://apps.apple.com/il/app/competitor/id1448298019
- Operational constraints reported in event instructions: heavy battery drain ("plan to have your device connected to a power supply"), you "cannot run any other apps on the device running the Richta app during the rally" — https://www.alcan5000.com/pdf/AlcanRichtaSetup.pdf ; the phone "will not need to have cell phone service during the rally… it only needs a GPS signal", and "At each timing control, the Richta Competitor App will ding and report your time and score at the control." — https://www.motorsportreg.com/events/badger-sett-2025-tour-roadrally-sauk-city-wisconsin-park-ride-scca-milwaukee-858306 and https://dl.motorsportreg.com/0582beb7-9921-4db0-a182-ff2ed6e4a581/
- Adoption: used by SCCA regions (Milwaukee "Badger Sett", "Kettle Moraine Colors", Central Division "Mini Monte") and the Alcan 5000 — https://motorsportreg.com/events/kettle-moraine-colors-2024-tour-roadrally-kohls-department-store-scca-milwaukee-934663 , https://cendiv.motorsportreg.com/index.cfm/event/event.requirements/uidEvent/3805FC21-96ED-07F7-0743FB3B0836F917 . SCCA ran a RoadRally Zoom get-together on 20 June 2024 titled "Richta GPS Checkpoints: Upcoming Enhancements / 10 Tips For Success Using Richta Rally" — https://scca.com/pages/roadrally-quarterly-zoom-get-togethers
- Reviews: US App Store rating 3.0/5 from 7 ratings — https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1448298019 . Other storefronts show "not enough ratings" — https://apps.apple.com/il/app/competitor/id1448298019
- Practice / simulation mode: none found in any search result. **UNVERIFIED** whether a demo rally exists. Takeaway for us: Richta is the de-facto *scoring* layer for US club TSDs; it gives immediate per-leg feedback (the "ding" and score) which competitors like, but it does not teach anything.

Observations across the app market:
- Every TSD app is an in-car *instrument* (a software rally computer). None found advertises a desktop/at-home simulation or tutorial mode. The only "practice" features are on-road (Rallymeter GET REFS, Rally Timer, Pacenote Recce Mode).
- Common UI patterns worth borrowing: a big **early/late pace indicator** (bar, LEDs, or seconds ahead/behind) for the driver, spoken/beeped countdowns to speed changes, "two of T/S/D → third" split calculators, dual clocks A/B, and post-stage accuracy reports (Regularity Mate, Rabbit "Reporting after race").
- Hardware-sensor integration (wheel sensor/OBD-II) is the differentiator for serious regularity apps because GPS is not legal or not precise enough in many formats (Rabbit Box, Regularity Mate Pro, Rallymeter blackBox).

## 7. Implications for a training simulator

### Borrow
1. **Immediate per-leg score feedback** (Richta "ding" + score; Great Race "ace" sticker for 0 s). The ace/sticker is a proven micro-reward; make "aces" a visible collectible and show seconds-off instantly at each checkpoint. — https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1448298019 , https://www.greatrace.com/about/faq/
2. **Early/late pace indicator** as the core HUD element — 7-LED seconds ahead/behind (Terratrip), blue/orange/green ±0.5 s LEDs (RallyTrip), semicircular gauge (HistoricRallyMeter), pacing bar (Regularity Mate). Offer it as a *training aid that can be switched off* so learners graduate to doing it mentally with only a stopwatch, as Great Race rules require.
3. **Demo/time-lapse mode** (RallyTrip: set simulated speed, 5× time-lapse, tiny closed loop) — proves a simulated car + clock is enough to practise the regularity loop without a road.
4. **3-2-1 cue before speed changes** (RegularityPace flash; Rally Co-Pilot/RallyTrip spoken countdowns) — teach anticipation; later remove the cue so the navigator must read ahead.
5. **Faithful instrument replica + worked solutions** (The Flight Computer Teacher) — render the analog stopwatch/clock accurately (1/5 s sweep, central 60-minute hand, 12-hour jump disc à la Heuer Monte Carlo) and *show the arithmetic* after each leg.
6. **Drill mechanics from pilot-math apps**: random question generators by skill module (time-for-distance, distance-for-time, speed-change splits, stop-time adjustments), timed rounds, high scores, streaks, badges for speed/accuracy/daily consistency.
7. **Working-memory load** (PacenoteExercises N-back): deliver instructions as an audio/visual stream and require the trainee to hold "current CAST + next instruction + elapsed time" — the real cognitive bottleneck ("no time to second guess").
8. **Curriculum order from real schools** (Detroit SCCA; Great Race rookie handbook): terms → route-instruction format → following the route → control/checkpoint procedure → timekeeping (first with calculator, then by hand/table) → practice legs → review. Add Great Race-specific: speedometer calibration/odometer leg, acceleration/deceleration tables, timed stops.
9. **Post-stage accuracy report / GPX-style replay** (Regularity Mate, Rabbit "Reporting after race") — show where time was lost (late speed change, slow stop recovery, misread instruction).

### Avoid
- Building yet another in-car rally computer: the market has 10+ apps and five hardware brands doing this well; and Great Race bans all of it in competition anyway.
- GPS dependence or any real-road requirement for practice; the gap is *at-home*, *before-the-event* training.
- Shipping copyrighted club route books; author original instruction sets in the documented formats instead.
- Over-automating the arithmetic (Blunik EVO OCR, Rally Co-Pilot split calculator) in *training* modes — the Great Race navigator must do it with pencil, tables and an analog clock.

### Gaps in the market the simulator can fill
- **There is no TSD/Great Race navigation simulator or trainer anywhere** — not on the app stores, itch.io, Steam or GitHub (section 2). The nearest things are a German iPhone tripmaster's demo mode and a pilot-math genre in aviation.
- **Great Race rookies and X-Cup students** receive one classroom session, a mentor, and a Friday practice run before a 9-day event; they describe school as "a foreign language." A browser trainer that simulates 220–250 instructions/day with timed stops and speed changes directly addresses this.
- **Analog-instrument literacy**: no tool teaches reading a 1/5 s mechanical stopwatch and an analog time-of-day clock under time pressure, which is exactly the Great Race equipment constraint.
- **Driver/navigator communication drills** (callouts, confirmation protocol) are taught only verbally at schools; a two-player or voice-driven mode would be unique.
- **Progressive de-scaffolding** (pace bar → numeric early/late → stopwatch only) has no precedent in rally tools.
