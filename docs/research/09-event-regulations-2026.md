## 1. Nature of the event (I.A, p.4)

- I.A.1: "The Great Race National Event (HEMMINGS GREAT RACE presented by Coker Tire) and Great
  Race Regional Rallies are automotive competitions based on precision driving and navigational
  skills in classic, antique, and vintage automobiles. The Events are timed, controlled-speed,
  endurance rallies---not top-speed races. Each vehicle must follow a prescribed common route
  while attempting to maintain assigned average speeds."
- I.A.3: "[GREAT RACE only] GREAT RACE is June 20 to June 28, 2026, from Springfield, Illinois to
  Pasadena, California, an official Route 66 Centennial activity. ... The competition is the
  optional Trophy Run (Warm-up Rally/Stage 0) on June 19, followed by 9 Stages. The exact format
  of those Stages will be given in an Event Supplement. Those Stages will include the following:
  a. Stages 1 through 7 are qualifying Stages. b. Stages 8 and 9 are a two-Stage Championship Run."
- I.A.4: "[Regional Rallies only] The competition is three Stages."
- I.A.5: "the term "Team" refers to one vehicle entry, along with its Competition Crew (driver(s)
  and navigator(s)) and Support Crew."
- I.D.1 Preliminary schedule [GREAT RACE only]: Thu June 18 "Registration and Technical
  Inspection"; Fri June 19 "Rally School; Trophy Run (Warm-up Rally/Stage 0)"; Sat June 20
  "Official Start, Springfield, Illinois; Stage 1"; Sun June 21 through Fri June 26 "Stages 2-7
  (Qualifying Stages)"; Sat June 27 "Stage 8 (Championship Run)"; Sun June 28 "Stage 9
  (Championship Run); Grand Finish, Pasadena, California". Full schedule to come in an Event
  Supplement.
- I.B.2: "GREAT RACE and each Great Race Regional Rally are governed by these regulations, and any
  Supplements issued thereto." I.C.5-6: Supplemental Regulations, Emergency Instructions, official
  notices may be issued; oral information "is always on an informal basis, and is not official ...
  The Event Regulations, Event Supplements, and other written materials govern the Event and take
  precedence over any oral information or interpretation."
- Official notices: I.C.2.a "posted at the morning start location"; I.C.2.b "Great Race officials
  will make all attempts to post official notices during the Event no later than 30 minutes prior
  to the official Stage start each day."

## 2. Divisions (I.E, p.5)

"Each Team will compete in Grand Championship Division or Expert Division or Sportsman Division or
Rookie Division [GREAT RACE only] or X-Cup Division."

- I.E.1 Grand Championship: "Any member of a Team which has finished in first place in the final
  championship standings (all divisions/classes combined) on any previous GREAT RACE."
- I.E.2 Expert (except those required in Grand Championship): "a. Any member of a Team which has
  won Sportsman Division on any previous GREAT RACE; b. Any member of a Team which has finished in
  the top ten positions in the final championship standings (all divisions/classes combined) on
  any previous GREAT RACE; c. Any person who has three times finished in the top five positions on
  any GREAT RACE Stage (all divisions/classes combined); d. Any Competition Crew member who, in the
  opinion of the Event Director or Director of Competition, is ineligible for Sportsman Division
  due to his or her qualifications as a rallyist".
- I.E.3 Sportsman: "All other Teams, except Rookie Teams and X-cup Teams".
- I.E.4 Rookie: "a. [GREAT RACE only] In order to qualify for Rookie Division and be eligible for
  Rookie Awards, no vehicle occupant on any Stage may have been a Competition Crew member in any
  previous GREAT RACE." (Regional: any previous GREAT RACE or Great Race Regional Rally.) "c. Also,
  any Competition Crew member may be determined to be ineligible for Rookie Division due to his or
  her qualifications as a rallyist by the opinion of the Event Director or Director of Competition."
- I.E.5 X-Cup: "qualified high-school/college/technical-school/youth-group Teams. X-Cup Teams are
  eligible for X-Cup awards only." Drivers: enrolled (or graduated within 12 months), "at least 19
  years old as of Stage 1". Navigators: "under 22 years old as of Stage 1. Each X-Cup Team must
  have 2 or more different navigators, each navigating a maximum of five Stages." "On each Stage,
  only one driver (adult team member) may be in the competing vehicle. More than one navigator is
  allowed, subject to the restrictions of II.D.2 and II.D.4." "Each member of the Team must be
  eligible for Rookie Division or Sportsman Division".

## 3. Championship scoring, discarded legs, awards (I.F, pp.5-6)

### 3.1 Eligibility for Championship awards
- I.F.1 [GREAT RACE only]: complete the 9-Stage competition: "a. The competing vehicle must pass
  all required Technical Inspections. b. ... must start Stage 1. c. ... must start and finish both
  Stages of the Championship Run (Stages 8 and 9). d. The Team must not be disqualified."
- I.F.2 [Regional]: same, with "start and finish Championship Day Stage 3".
- V.F.5 [GREAT RACE only]: "DNF or FNS on either Stage of the Championship Run. They are still
  eligible for Stage Awards and Ace Awards." V.F.6 [Regional]: DNF/FNS on Stage 3 likewise.

### 3.2 Cumulative Score with discarded legs [GREAT RACE only] (I.F.3)
"A Cumulative Score for the Event (Grand Championship Score) is determined for each Team in the
following manner":
- a. Grand Championship Division: "the total scores for all but the 3 worst of each Team's Legs on
  any of the Qualifying Stages (Stages 1-7), plus the total scores for the Championship Run
  (Stages 8 and 9). (Grand Championship Division Teams' cumulative standings during the Event will
  show one Leg discarded after Stage 3, another after Stage 4, and a third after Stage 5.)"
- b. Expert Division: "all but the 4 worst ... (... one Leg discarded after Stage 2, another after
  Stage 3, a third after Stage 4, and a fourth after Stage 5.)"
- c. Sportsman Division: "all but the 5 worst ... (... one Leg discarded after Stage 1, another
  after Stage 2, a third after Stage 3, a fourth after Stage 4, and a fifth after Stage 5.)"
- d. Rookie Division: "all but the 6 worst ... (... one Leg discarded after Stage 1, another after
  Stage 2, a third after Stage 3, a fourth after Stage 4, a fifth after Stage 5, and a sixth after
  Stage 6.)"
- e. X-Cup Teams: "all but the 5 worst ... (... one Leg discarded after Stage 1, ... and a fifth
  after Stage 5.)"
- f. "An additional penalty, such as a DNF (Did Not Finish), FNS (Finished No Score) or others
  specified in V.E.3, can be one of the "legs" discarded on Qualifying Stages (Stages 1-7)."
- Note: the discards are pooled across Stages 1-7 (worst N legs of the whole qualifying block),
  not N per stage. Stages 8 and 9 are never discarded.

### 3.3 Regional Rallies (I.F.4, highlighted in part)
Grand Championship: "total scores for all Stages". Expert: "all but the 1 worst of each Team's
Legs on either of the Qualifying Stages (Stages 1-2), plus ... Championship Day (Stage 3)".
Sportsman: "all but the 2 worst". Rookie: "all but the 3 worst ... (Rookie Division Teams'
cumulative standings during the Event will show two Legs discarded after Stage 1 and another
after Stage 2.)" (highlighted).

### 3.4 Basis for awards (I.F.5)
- "a. Overall awards are awarded based on the Championship Standings as described in I.F.3 or I.F.4."
- "b. Grand Championship Division Awards, Expert Division Awards, Sportsman Division Awards, Rookie
  Division Awards, and X-Cup Division Awards are based on Cumulative Score".
- "c. [GREAT RACE only] Stage Awards (daily winners) are based on the day's score with no legs
  discarded."
- "d. Ace Awards are given for each Ace (perfect leg score of zero)."
- I.F.6: "All competing vehicles and Competition Crew members are subject to search by Great Race
  officials at any time during the competition. Refusal to allow a search is cause for
  disqualification. A Team found with illegal equipment is subject to the penalties specified in VI.B.3."
- I.F.9: Great Race may alter or terminate the competition if unexpected circumstances prevent
  completion. I.G: prizes after certification of results; amounts "will be announced" (not in regulations).

## 4. Team composition, crew, occupants (II.D, p.8; II.E)

- II.D.1: "A Competition Crew of two persons (generally described as a driver and a navigator) is
  required in each competing vehicle."
- II.D.2: "A competing vehicle must have a seat and (except for a motorcycle) a seat belt for each
  occupant." II.D.3: all occupants "MUST DISPLAY OFFICIAL GREAT RACE IDENTIFICATION TAGS during all
  competition and Event functions."
- II.D.4 Additional occupants: "a. A Team which will have more than two occupants in their vehicle
  on any portion of a Stage must notify Race Headquarters, in writing ... prior to the start of that
  Stage. b. A penalty of 5 seconds will be added to the Stage score of any competing vehicle having
  additional occupant(s) of age 13 years or older present in the vehicle during any portion of that
  day's competition, other than an Official Great Race Staff Person or a person (such as a VIP or
  media representative) authorized in writing by Great Race. c. [GREAT RACE only] There is no
  penalty for additional occupant(s) on the Trophy Run (Warm-up Rally/Stage 0)."
- II.D.5 Substitutions: "a. Competition Crews beginning any single Stage (day) must run the entire
  Stage with no substitutions allowed. Competition Crew substitution is permitted between Stages.
  ... must be noted in writing to Race Headquarters before the start of each Stage. b. Driver and
  navigator may trade positions with each other at any time. c. After the start of the Event, a
  person must not compete in a vehicle other than his or her official entry vehicle without
  specific permission from the Event Director."
- II.E.3: "During a Stage, a competing vehicle may receive service and/or assistance from official
  Great Race Course Vehicles and personnel, from another Competition Crew member, and from a
  business or individual not otherwise directly associated with any Team. During a Stage, a
  competing vehicle must not receive service, assistance, or communications from anyone else
  associated with any Team including, but not limited to, a Support Vehicle, a Support Crew
  member, a family member, or a friend."
- II.E.5-8: Support Vehicles must not travel near their competing vehicle; where instructed not to
  be in the field, "it is recommended that Support Vehicles depart at least 30 minutes prior to the
  official start of the Stage." II.E.8 penalties: "1 minute for the first occurrence, 5 minutes for
  the second occurrence, and disqualification for the third occurrence."

## 5. Equipment and instruments (II.H, pp.9-10) - verbatim

II.H.1 "The following equipment is allowed in competing vehicles:"
- a. "Pencils, pens, and scratch pads"
- b. "Vehicle performance notes and speedometer performance notes"
- c. "Race tables, instructions, and maps furnished by Great Race."
- d. Timepieces:
  - (1) "One mechanical or quartz-crystal analog time-of-day watch or clock: This is the primary
    timepiece and is the responsibility of the Competition Crew. The watch/clock may have (one
    each) analog hour, minute, and second hand. The clock must not have stopwatch, compensating,
    calculating, or split-action functions, or digital readout. The clock must not be electrically
    connected to the vehicle (i.e., it must have its own internal battery, rather than using the
    vehicle's). A clock with forbidden functions (such as stopwatch, split-action, etc.) that has
    been modified to disable those functions is prohibited. Temporary marks are allowed on the glass
    face. The maximum clock dimension (height, width, diameter, etc.) is 10 inches measured on the
    face of the clock."
  - (2) "Wristwatches: In addition, the driver and navigator may each wear one approved analog
    wristwatch without stopwatch function. A digital wristwatch is prohibited." (highlighted)
  - (3) "Stopwatch: Each Team may have one approved digital or analog stopwatch; the stopwatch may
    also have split-action, time-of-day, date, and alarm functions. The stopwatch must not be
    electrically connected to the vehicle (i.e., it must have its own internal battery, rather than
    using the vehicle's)." (highlighted)
  - (4) "Any clock or watch that is self-correcting or self-synchronizing by using radio, cellular,
    or satellite/GPS technology is prohibited."
  - (5) "No other timekeeping devices are allowed in the vehicle."
- e. "One radio receiving the standard broadcast AM-FM frequencies only"
- f. "One analog tire pressure gauge, one analog compass, one analog thermometer, and one analog
  altimeter"
- g. "One non-radio intercom system allowing communication only between driver and navigator which
  requires a wired or tube connection to function; i.e., helmets with microphones and earphones"
- h. "One operating analog speedometer approved by Great Race as defined here is allowed in each
  entry vehicle. Any type of measuring device other than as defined here is prohibited."
  - (1) "The one operational speedometer may be an aftermarket unit; if so, THE VEHICLE'S ORIGINAL
    SPEEDOMETER MUST BE COVERED."
  - (2) "Digital speedometers are prohibited."
  - (3) "An in-line speedometer correction gear box is allowed."
  - (4) "Maximum face diameter is 4 inches; maximum overall housing diameter is 4-3/4 inches."
  - (5) "Calibration divisions on the face must read no closer than one MPH increments."
  - (6) "ALL COMPETING VEHICLE ODOMETERS MUST BE COVERED OR COMPLETELY REMOVED PRIOR TO THE START OF
    THE EVENT. The cover must be applied in such a way that the odometer will be rendered
    unreadable. (The speedometer can remain readable.) Teams may remove the odometer wheels from
    their speedometer or cover the odometer face prior to Technical Inspection in lieu of having the
    cover applied to the face of the speedometer at Technical Inspection."
  - (7) "Great Race officials may require the modification or removal of any odometer or other
    device to assure that it cannot be used to measure distance."
  - (8) "Speedometer drives for electronic speedometers: Only one magnetic pickup unit is allowed on
    the vehicle; it should be located in an unobtrusive location."
- i. "Cellular telephones are to be used only in case of emergency, and for the purpose of
  submitting Time Allowance Requests (see V.H.3). Otherwise, the cellular telephones must not be
  used beginning when each team leaves the Start line each day until they arrive at the final
  Observation Checkpoint at the Finish Line that day. The use of a cellular telephone other than in
  case of emergency will result in a warning for the first offense, and penalties of 10 seconds for
  the second offense and 1 minute for further offenses. The use of a cellular telephone's clock,
  calculator, or mapping/positioning functions is prohibited at all times." (highlighted)
- j. "Digital cameras and video cameras which have a digital clock are permissible, but use of the
  digital clock is unsportsmanlike conduct."

II.H.2 "The following equipment is prohibited in competing vehicles:"
a. "Tachometers are prohibited. If the vehicle has an original tachometer, it must be either removed
or covered in advance or at Technical Inspection so that it is not readable." b. "Any maps, charts,
tables, pace notes, or course materials from prior Great Race Events, except as specifically allowed

which has a digital clock, digital display, or position-determining capability"

II.H.3: "Prohibited equipment (a calculator, computer, iPad, short-wave/WWV radio, GPS device, etc.)
must be carried in a support vehicle if you have one; if not it must be stored in an inaccessible
location in the competing vehicle." II.H.4: "A Team found with illegal equipment is subject to the
penalties specified in VI.B.3." (VI.B.3: "up to 2 minutes" added to the Stage score, DNF for the

IX.C.20-22 (p.22): "Odometers are prohibited. See II.H.1.h(6), (7)." "Tachometers are prohibited."

## 6. Vehicles (Part IX, pp.19-22) - timing-relevant parts

- II.A.2 / IX.A.1 [GREAT RACE only]: "Vehicle entries must have been manufactured in 1974 or
  earlier". Post-1974-by-equipment vehicles "may be accepted for entry on an individual review
  basis". [Regional]: "Vehicles of any Model Year".
- IX.B.1: "For cars of Model Year 1953 and earlier, the use of a performance component of a newer
  year than the manufacture of the vehicle will cause the Team to be scored using a "Scoring Year"
  based upon the year of manufacture of that component. For cars of Model Year 1954 and newer, the
  "Scoring Year" is the same as the Model Year." IX.B.2: "In no case will a vehicle's Scoring Year
  be earlier than its Model Year."
- IX.C.4.b examples: "A 21-stud Ford Flathead engine in a vehicle manufactured before 1932 will be
  scored as a 1932"; "A 24-stud Ford Flathead engine in a vehicle manufactured before 1938 will be
  scored as a 1938"; IX.C.4.c: "a 21-stud Ford Flathead engine in a 1939 Ford will be scored as a
  1939 and not as a 1932".
- IX.C.12.d: unapproved later model rear axle (Model Year 1953 and earlier) "will carry a 2-year
  adjustment in "Scoring Year""; IX.C.25: altered front/rear suspension (1953 and earlier) "will
  carry a 2-year adjustment in "Scoring Year" for each axle."; IX.C.7 carburetors and IX.C.12.b
  transmissions: adjustment "as described in IX.B.1".
- IX.C.13.a-c brakes: disc brakes allowed; "Conversion from two-wheel brake system to four-wheel
  brake system is permissible." (see also V.H.2.b "two-wheel brakes").
- IX.B.9-11 required: lap seat belts for driver and navigator; "One operational fire extinguisher,
  dry chemical, at least 2 1/2 pounds A/B/C"; first aid kit; "One tow rope or chain, at least sixteen
  feet in length"; flares/reflectors; "One gallon container of drinking water"; flashlight.
  IX.C.9.d: "A FUEL RANGE OF 200 MILES IS STRONGLY RECOMMENDED."
- Not in regulations: vehicle performance data (acceleration, braking, speedometer error) of any
  specific car, including a 1939 Ford.

## 7. Start procedure, order of start, restarts, lunch, transits

### 7.1 Course Instructions availability and official start time (VII.B.2, p.16)
- VII.B.2.a: "With the possible exception of the Trophy Run (Warm-up Rally/Stage 0) and Stage 1, one
  set of Course Instructions for each Stage will be available to each competing Team each day near
  the Stage start, exactly 30 minutes before the official time for the Team to start the Stage. The
  time and location for each Stage start (after Stage 1) will be found at the end of the Course
  Instructions for the previous Stage. Each Team's official starting time is determined by adding
  their starting position (see III.A,B,C) in minutes to the official Stage start time."
- VII.B.2.b: "For Trophy Run (Warm-up Rally/Stage 0) and Stage 1, special provisions may be made for
  issuing Course Instructions; if so, this will be noted in an Event Supplement."
- VII.B.2.c: "The Competition Crew member must show his/her identification tag with the vehicle
  number in order to receive the Course Instructions. At this time the Competition Crew member may
  be required to draw for the next Stage starting position."

### 7.2 Order of start (III, p.11)
- III.A [GREAT RACE only]: Group I "hometown and home state entries"; Group II "Corporate Entries";
  Group III "Private Entries and X-Cup entries". "Great Race at its discretion may exercise the
  option to assign starting positions to certain entrants."
- III.A.3: "On Trophy Run (Stage 0) and Stage 1, the order of start is the 2025 GREAT RACE Grand
  Champion, followed by Corporate entries sponsored by major Event sponsors, Group I, Group II, Group
  III. The starting order within Group II and within Group III is determined by the date of entry".
- III.A.4: "On Stages 2 through 8, the order of start will be Group I, Group II, Group III. Teams
  will draw for start positions within Group II and within Group III." III.A.5: "On Stage 9 the order
  of start will be determined by Great Race officials."
- III.B [Regional]: by date of entry for Stage 1, then draw each subsequent Stage.
- III.C: disqualified-but-continuing teams "may receive starting positions behind all Teams still
  eligible". III.D: teams that leave must notify Race Headquarters.
- The "starting position" is a number of minutes added to the official time (VII.B.2.a; Example
  Rally #1: "Leave here at 8:00:00 plus your assigned start position in minutes").
- Not in regulations: how many cars per minute; whether a team may be released early; any penalty
  for starting late (see section 13 note); the exact location of the master clock beyond V.C.1.b
  (below).

### 7.3 Time zone and master clock (V.C.1, p.12)
- V.C.1.b: "Official time is Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). UTC is available from the U.S.
  National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) on radio station WWV, radio station CHU of
  the National Research Council of Canada, and other sources. ... We intend to have a digital clock
  set to WWV at the start location each day to allow contestants to synchronize their clocks."
- V.C.1.c: "Time Zones: The time of the time zone at the start of each Stage is used for scoring
  throughout that Stage, regardless of time zone changes (don't forget, however, to reset your
  timepiece before the next Stage start if the time zone changes). Time zone information will be
  given in an Event Supplement and in the Course Instructions."

### 7.4 Tire warm-up, calibration, transits, restarts, lunch (V.B.2, VII.C.5, glossary, Example Rally)
- V.B.2.a: "Each Stage includes a Tire Warm-up at the beginning, followed by a Speedometer
  Calibration Run of at least 15 miles. The Tire Warm-up and Speedometer Calibration Run are free
  zones (see VII.C.5). Teams may also receive a Measured Mile at Registration."
- V.B.2.c: "Transit times are assigned for the Tire Warm-up and Speedometer Calibration Run. The
  transit time given for the calibration run is usually slightly longer than the official time given
  for calibration, to allow adequate time to recover from variations you may encounter. The transit
  times are included as part of the scoring time."
- Glossary: "TRANSIT: A portion of the race in which there are no Timing Checkpoints and for which no
  specific speed is assigned. A time for passage or Restart time from the end of the transit will be
  given." "FREE ZONE: A specified portion of the race in which there are no Timing Checkpoints."
  "LEG: A part of the Race Route extending from one Timing Checkpoint to the next, or from an
  assigned Start or Restart point to the next Timing Checkpoint". "STAGE: One day's competition".
- VII.C.5: "a. A free zone is a specified portion of the timed course where no Timing Checkpoints
  will be encountered. This allows the Team to proceed on that portion of the Race Route without
  concern about encountering a Timing Checkpoint. b. A free zone may be instructed in the Course
  Instructions. c. There will be a 2-minute free zone after the end of each transit. Parking is
  sometimes limited at the end of a transit; this will allow vehicles to park along the Race Route
  after the end of transit."
- VII.E.1.b: "A required time for traveling between two points may sometimes be given in lieu of a
  required average speed. This is called a transit, and may be used for lunch stops, refueling
  stops, passing through congested areas, etc."
- Restart, Example Rally #12 (p.2): "End Transit at the referenced sign. Time-of-day restart. Leave
  this point at 8:55:00 plus your assigned start position in minutes. Begin average speed of 30
  miles per hour." #24 (p.4): "Leave this point at 2:55:00 plus your assigned start position in
  minutes."
- Example Rally arithmetic (morning): start 8:00:00 + Tire Warm-up 20m00s + Calibration 26m00s +
  Transit 9m00s = 8:55:00 restart.
- Lunch/refuel/pit/rest are INSIDE a transit (Example Rally #19, #20, #22, #23): "Hosted meal stop
  (usually lunch). After lunch, leave here 45 minutes prior to your end-of-transit time. (Meal stops
  always occur within Transits, and the time given is included in the specified transit time.)"
  Refuel: "Leave here 3 hours 10 minutes prior to your end-of-transit time."; Hosted Pit stop:
  "leave here 2 hours 10 minutes prior to your end-of-transit time"; Rest Stop: "Leave here 3 minutes
  prior to your end-of-transit time."
- V.E.3.h penalty for leaving a promoted Lunch Stop, Pit Stop or Rest Stop early (see section 13).
- Example Rally #17: "End timed portion. The timed portion of the stage resumes at the next
  restart, if there is one; otherwise, this is the end of the timed portion of the stage. In this
  case the timed portion resumes at instruction #24." #35: "End timed portion; this is the end of the
  timed portion of the stage. Begin Transit of approximately 20 miles to the finish line; take 30
  minutes to complete the Transit." #37: "Finish Line. End Stage. Stop at Observation Checkpoint."
- V.C.2.b(2): ">30 minutes after the computed cumulative perfect time from the most recent Stage
  Start or Restart" (see 11.2).
- Not in regulations: whether a human official releases each car at its restart minute, or the car
  self-starts; any restart-line procedure beyond "Leave this point at <time> plus your assigned start
  position in minutes".

## 8. Course Instructions and the GRIID format (VII.B-F, pp.16-19)

### 8.1 Using the instructions (VII.B.1, B.3)
- VII.B.1.a: "The Course Instructions are a series of instructions which are used to follow the Race
  Route. They are numbered in ascending numerical order, and are to be executed in that order."
  B.1.b: "Some Course Instructions might be letter/numbered (3a, 3b, etc.) or a number might be
  marked "omitted". This may be done to facilitate last-minute changes; the order of execution will
  always be obvious."
- VII.B.3.a: "In general, each instruction will direct an action to be taken, such as a turn or a
  speed change, or a reference point to be observed such as a sign or landmark. You are to execute
  each instruction at the first opportunity to do so. You must complete each instruction before you
  can consider the next instruction for execution. An instruction is completed when the last speed
  change specified has been completed. If there is no speed change indicated, then the instruction
  is completed when the sign, landmark, or intersection has been passed."
- VII.B.3.b: "It is recommended that you always be aware of at least the next two instructions so
  that you won't pass an execution point while trying to read the next instruction. If you don't
  know your next instruction, stop and read it before proceeding."
- VII.B.3.c: "The GRIID (Great Race International Instruction Design) format will be used. Each
  instruction will consist of five columns."
  - "(1) The first column contains the instruction numbers."
  - "(2) The second column (labeled "A") contains CAMEO images or diagrams of signs, landmarks, and
    road and intersection configurations. The CAMEO diagram of an intersection will indicate the road
    on which you are approaching the intersection with a dot, and the road on which you are to leave
    the intersection with an arrow. The route that you are to follow through the intersection is
    represented by a bold line from the dot to the arrow. Thin line(s) in the CAMEO diagram of the
    intersection represent road(s) not taken."
  - "(3) The third column (labeled "B") contains symbols specifying the tire warm-up, the speedometer
    calibration run, transits, free zones, lunch stops, refueling stops, pit stops, etc."
  - "(4) The fourth column (labeled "C") contains assigned average speeds and timing information.
    Time of day for Starts and Restarts is represented with colons separating hours, minutes, and
    seconds (e.g., 7:30:00 indicates a time of day). Interval times for the tire warm-up, the
    speedometer calibration run, transits, pauses, timed speed changes, etc., are represented as
    follows: 3h15m00s indicates an interval time of 3 hours 15 minutes; 0m45s indicates an interval
    time of 45 seconds. Interval times in parentheses are advisory, but not official, e.g.,
    (35m00s). Other numbers in this column represent assigned average speeds in miles per hour,
    e.g., 45 MPH."
  - "(5) The fifth column (labeled "D") may contain additional information as part of the Course
    Instruction to assist you in following the course. It may have information such as "Comes
    quick", "Look sharp", "1st paved road", "Follow this Curve Warning Sign", or other helpful
    information."
- VII.B.3.d: examples are in the attached Example Rally. VII.B.3.e: "Information contained in
  parentheses or brackets is helpful or informative but not essential."
- In the Example Rally, Column D carries the full written instruction text for every row (e.g. #2
  "Turn right onto Washington Way at a crossroad at a Traffic Light."), i.e. the written instruction
  is in a column separate from the CAMEO in Column A.
- In the Example Rally, Column B also shows an odometer-style box (e.g. 0 0 8 0 for the 8-mile
  warm-up, 0 2 1 0 for the 21-mile calibration run, 0 0 4 5 for the 4.5-mile transit, 0 7 5 0 for the
  75-mile transit, 0 1 2 0, 0 2 0 0) matching the "approximately N miles" in Column D. Observed in
  the example only; the regulation text does not describe it.

### 8.2 Following the route (VII.C, p.17)
- VII.C.1: "In general, the Race Route will follow principal roads and interstate highways. At each
  intersection, you will execute the active instruction if it applies. If no instruction applies at
  an intersection, you will continue on the obvious principal road. Do not take an exit from an
  interstate highway or limited-access highway without an instruction."
- VII.C.2: instructions may be redundant with the obvious road. VII.C.4: the route "will never
  enter a private road, driveway, parking lot, unpaved road, or dead-end road without an
  instruction"; "will not make illegal turns or illegal entries".

### 8.3 Signs and landmarks (VII.D, p.18)
- Text in rectangles/diamonds/circles in the CAMEO is text from a sign; "While spelling is supposed
  to be exact, there are no "traps" based on spelling."
- "Signs will be found on the right or left of your route or overhead." "Signs painted on road
  surfaces will not be used unless Column D ... specifies "sign painted on road surface."" "Signs on
  vehicles or other temporary surfaces will not be used. You will not have to look back (over your
  shoulder) to read a sign." Referenced text is continuous and may be partial but principal part.
  "Referenced Speed Limit signs are those which apply to the Race Route."
- Landmarks "will either be obvious from their usual meaning, or will be identified by a sign, or
  will be defined in the Glossary."
- Emergency Signs (VII.D.3): Day-Glo "pink, red, yellow, or orange with the letters 'GR'" and: "a. An
  instruction number; the instruction is to be executed at that point. b. An arrow ... c. The letter
  "I": Ignore the sign or landmark on which the emergency sign has been placed. d. A speed change,
  pause, start of transit, etc. e. "End Leg": The leg has been canceled and will not be scored.
  Continue on course in a safe manner at approximately assigned speeds. The next leg, if any, will
  begin at the next time-of-day Restart you come to."

### 8.4 Average speeds and speed changes (VII.E, p.18-19)
- VII.E.1.a: "Required average speeds will be stated in the Course Instructions. A clear reference
  point will be given for each speed change. The speed(s) assigned in an instruction will appear in
  Column C in miles per hour (MPH). On highways, the required average speed will often be 50 MPH or
  55 MPH." (highlighted)
- VII.E.1.c: "If you should encounter a portion of the Race Route where posted speed limits are below
  the assigned average speed, there should be adequate opportunity to recover the lost time before
  the next Timing Checkpoint."
- VII.E.1.d: Emergency Signs may direct speed or route changes; "Any such changes will be adjusted in
  scoring."
- VII.E.2 Speed changes:
  - a. "There are two types of speed changes. The first type is executed at a specific referenced
    point on the route; this location will be identified as a sign, intersection, or landmark. The
    second type of speed change is executed after a specified time interval. Both types of speed
    changes are specified in instruction Column C."
  - b. "When a speed change references only a sign or landmark, it is executed when the front tires of
    the vehicle passes (comes even with) the sign or landmark; see instruction # 13 in the Example
    Rally."
  - c. "Speed changes at intersections will occur at a sign (such as a stop sign) if there is one
    referenced in Column A of the CAMEO diagram; see instruction # 27 in the Example Rally.
    Otherwise, speed changes at intersections will occur at the center of the intersection or the
    apex of the turn; see instruction # 28 in the Example Rally."
  - d. "Timed speed changes occur after the time interval specified in Column C has elapsed. The time
    interval begins in one of two ways: If the time interval is preceded by a speed change, the
    interval starts at the same place as that speed change; see instruction # 14 in the Example
    Rally. If the time interval is the first item specified in Column C (this is called a delayed
    speed change) then the interval begins at the point specified in VII.E.2.b or VII.E.2.c; see
    instruction # 25 in the Example Rally."
- VII.E.3 Emergency Reduced Speeds: "In case of inclement weather, fog, poor road conditions,
  extraordinary traffic, or other unexpected conditions, a Team may elect to use reduced speeds. See
  V.H.2."

### 8.5 Official Times in the instructions (VII.F, p.19)
- VII.F.1: "During Speedometer Calibration Runs, there will be two official times in a box in Column
  C; these times may be given to the 1/10 of a second. The first time will be the interval time to
  the instruction from the previous instruction for which a time was given; the second time will be
  the cumulative official speedometer calibration time to that point". Example: box "8m40.7s /
  16m02.0s": "8 minutes 40.7 seconds is the time required to travel the official distance from
  instruction # 7 to instruction # 9 at the current assigned speed. 16 minutes 02.0 seconds is the
  official time from the start of the Speedometer Calibration Run to instruction # 9."
- VII.F.2: "An asterisk ( * ) in the interval time column indicates that the next interval time is
  from this instruction".
- VII.F.3: Column D may give "Approximately 12m00s after previous instruction": "The time given is not
  the official time, but is the approximate time which will be required to reach the execution point
  from the previous instruction averaging the assigned speed. The official time will probably be more
  or less than 12 minutes."

## 9. Checkpoints (V.A, pp.11-12)

V.A.1: "Two types of Checkpoints are used: Timing Checkpoints and Observation Checkpoints. You will
usually not be told in advance the location of Checkpoints."
- Timing Checkpoints (V.A.1.a): "may be located anywhere along the Race Route, and may be on either
  side of the Race Route."
  - (1) "Each Timing Checkpoint has a timing line which is identified by a green Day-Glo Timing
    Checkpoint sign (an example of the sign will be displayed at Registration and/or Rally School).
    Your arrival time at the Checkpoint is recorded when your vehicle's front wheels cross the timing
    line from the correct direction."
  - (2) "Do not stop at a Timing Checkpoint. As you cross the timing line, the previous leg ends and
    the next leg begins. Your time to start the next leg at the timing line is the same as the time
    you arrived at the timing line."
  - (3) "An entry vehicle must not stop or travel at 5 MPH or less within sight of a Timing
    Checkpoint. An entry vehicle which stops or slows to 5 MPH or less within sight of a Timing
    Checkpoint will receive the penalty specified in V.E.3.a." (30 Seconds)
- Observation Checkpoints (V.A.1.b): "may be located anywhere along the Race Route, and may be on
  either side".
  - (1) "identified by a red Day-Glo Observation Checkpoint sign ... You must stop at an Observation
    Checkpoint and have your Course Instructions endorsed; failure to stop may result in a penalty or
    disqualification."
  - (2) "may be used to verify that Teams are following the correct route, operating their vehicles
    safely and legally, and complying with the rules of the Event; or for other purposes deemed
    appropriate by the organizers. It may issue emergency instructions when necessary."
  - (3) "Observation Checkpoints are located so that Teams have adequate time to make up the stopping
    time before the next Timing Checkpoint; or where timing is not critical, such as within a
    transit; or additional time may be allotted at the Observation Checkpoint."
  - (4) "There may be an official Great Race Lunch Gate at the lunch stops. The Gate is on the route
    directed by the Course Instructions. There may be an Observation Checkpoint located near the Lunch
    Gate, in which case each Team must pass through the Gate and stop at the Checkpoint. Do not stop
    in the Gate unless asked to do so by a Great Race official."
  - (5) "There is an official Great Race Finish Gate or Finish Line at the destination each day ...
    There may be an Observation Checkpoint located near the Finish Gate or Finish Line. If so, the
    official may give Teams their Checkpoint times or scores for the Stage. This Observation
    Checkpoint is the final Checkpoint for that Stage."
- V.A.2: "A Team who interferes with Checkpoint operations, whether deliberately or otherwise, may be
  penalized in accordance with V.E.3.b." V.A.3: "Checkpoints remain open consistent with V.C.2.b for
  all vehicles except those known to have dropped out or who have been disqualified."
- V.G.1-2 (p.14): "A lead car will precede the race to ensure that the Race Route is clear and
  correct, Timing Checkpoints are in place and accurately located, and to post emergency signs if
  needed." "Sweep vehicles will follow the race to verify route integrity, close Checkpoints, check
  watches, and provide assistance to Competition Crews when possible."
- V.G.3: "The Executive Committee at their sole discretion may discard a leg from scoring if it feels
  that conditions or problems on that leg rendered it unsuitable for competition. Such a decision
  will be posted in the evening Event Venue and at the morning start location. The Executive
  Committee's decision to discard a leg is not subject to claim."
- Not in regulations: number of Timing Checkpoints per Stage, their spacing, how often they follow a
  stop/turn, whether a Lunch Gate Observation Checkpoint is timed.

## 10. Official time, mileage, calibration (V.B, p.12)

- V.B.1.a: "Official mileages (from which official times are computed) are measured by vehicles using
  radial tires and having equipment which measures to a resolution of .01 mile; the Speedometer
  Calibration Runs may be measured to a resolution of .001 mile. Since competing vehicles have no
  odometer, official mileages are not used in the Course Instructions."
- V.B.1.b: "Official mileage will approximate statute miles. However, day-to-day variations may occur
  because of changes in weather, equipment, etc."
- V.B.2.b: "The calibration instructions include references to signs and landmarks, and average
  speeds to be maintained between those signs and landmarks. In Column C of the Course Instructions
  are the interval times which are required to travel between the signs and landmarks at the assigned
  speeds, and the cumulative times to those points. Several signs and landmarks are referenced during
  the calibration run; different speeds may be given between different sets of signs and landmarks.
  You will be able to "calibrate" your vehicle's speedometer against the official mileage by driving
  at the stipulated speed and comparing the time you require to travel between the signs and
  landmarks to the official time. You may attempt to adjust your indicated speeds to match the
  official times."

## 11. Timing and scoring rules (V.C, pp.12-13)

### 11.1 Timing (V.C.1)
- a. "Times are in hours, minutes, and seconds."
- d. "Times recorded at Timing Checkpoints are to the nearest second."

### 11.2 Scoring (V.C.2)
- a. "Each Team will receive a penalty score based on each second their leg time differs from the
  perfect time calculated for that leg."
- b. Missed Checkpoints: "(1) A vehicle which misses (fails to pass) a Checkpoint or passes a
  Checkpoint not under its own power will receive the penalty stipulated in V.E.2. (2) A vehicle which
  arrives at any Checkpoint more than 30 minutes after the computed cumulative perfect time from the
  most recent Stage Start or Restart, including the final Observation Checkpoint of the Stage at the
  Finish Line, will be scored as having missed the Checkpoint, and will receive the penalty
  stipulated in V.E.2."
- c. "A Team which does not receive a starting time at one or more Timing Checkpoints (if, for
  example, a Timing Checkpoint is missed) will be scored at the next Timing Checkpoint where they are
  timed, based on that in-time and their previous assigned starting time compared to the combined
  perfect times for those legs in between."
- d. "If a leg is discarded from scoring for unforeseen conditions, any penalties incurred on that leg
  will be dropped from scoring, except those listed in V.E.3.e, V.F, and VIII.D." (V.E.3.e = failure
  to stop at a Stop Sign.)
- e. "After completion of each Stage, each Team's score for that Stage received under V.E is
  multiplied by the applicable "Age Factor" and the result rounded to the nearest 0.01 second. The
  Team with the lowest total score is declared the winner of the Stage. Subsequent positions are
  determined in the same manner. A Cumulative (Championship) Score for the Event is determined for
  each Team in the same manner, using the total scores adjusted as specified in I.F.3 and I.F.4."
- f. Ties:
  - (1) [GREAT RACE only] Trophy Run (Stage 0): "(a) The older vehicle by Scoring Year wins the tie
    and the newer Scoring Year vehicle takes the next position. (b) If a tie still exists, the scores
    for each leg of the Trophy Run, except legs discarded per paragraph V.G.3, are compared. The Team
    having the lower score on a leg wins that leg; the Team winning the greater number of legs wins
    the tie. (c) If a tie still exists, the scores of the tied Teams are compared for each leg of the
    Trophy Run, in inverse order. The Team having the lower score at the first leg where their scores
    differ wins the tie."
  - (2) [GREAT RACE only] each Stage after the Trophy Run and Cumulative Event Score: "(a) The older
    vehicle by Scoring Year wins the tie and the newer Scoring Year vehicle takes the next position.
    (b) If a tie still exists, the Team having the higher finishing position on the Trophy Run
    (Warm-up Rally/Stage 0) wins the tie."
  - (3) [Regional]: older vehicle; then leg-by-leg count (except legs discarded per V.G.3); then
    inverse-order leg comparison.

### 11.3 Posting of scores (V.C.3)
- a. Scores posted on www.greatrace.com; "individual scores will be given to Teams as they arrive at
  the finish, or otherwise before they have been posted."
- b. "Scores for each Stage are final 30 minutes after complete scores have been posted, or 30
  minutes after the computed cumulative perfect time from the most recent Stage Start or Restart for
  the last car, whichever is earlier, unless scoring review or Problem Resolution Requests are
  pending".
- c. Final Event scores posted "following Final Technical Inspection and the resolution of any Formal
  Problem Resolution Requests. These scores are subject to change only for computational errors, and
  are final 30 minutes after posting."

## 13. Penalties (V.E, V.F, VI.B.3, others)

### 13.1 Timing penalties (V.E.1, p.14)
- "a. Each second early or late at a Timing Checkpoint compared to the computed perfect time ...
  1 second"
- "b. Maximum late penalty under V.E.1.a (unless V.C.2.b applies) ... 2 Minutes"
- "c. Maximum early penalty under V.E.1.a ... 5 Minutes"
(As printed. Note the late cap (2 Minutes) is lower than the early cap (5 Minutes) and lower than
the missed-checkpoint penalty (3 Minutes); verify against the PDF page 14 before hard-coding.)

### 13.2 Missed checkpoints, "or being more than 30 minutes late (see V.C.2.b)" (V.E.2)
- "a. Missing a Timing Checkpoint, except the final Timing Checkpoint of a Stage ... 3 Minutes"
- "b. Missing the final Timing Checkpoint of a Stage ... DNF or FNS"
- "c. Missing an Observation Checkpoint, except the final Observation Checkpoint of a Stage ... 3
  Minutes"
- "d. Missing the final Observation Checkpoint of a Stage ... DNF or FNS"

### 13.3 Other penalties (V.E.3)
- "a. Stopping or traveling 5 MPH or slower within sight of a Timing Checkpoint ... 30 Seconds"
- "b. Blocking a Checkpoint in-line (intentionally or not) or interfering with Checkpoint operations
  ... 5 Minutes or DNF"
- "c. More than two persons in a vehicle ... see II.D.4" (5 seconds per II.D.4.b)
- "d. Support Vehicle violation ... see II.E.8" (1 minute / 5 minutes / disqualification)
- "e. Failure to stop at a Stop Sign ... DNF"
- "f. Being carried on any trailer for any part of a Stage ... DNF"
- "g. A vehicle which is towed or pushed by another vehicle on any part of a Stage, except to receive
  assistance in starting, getting back on the road, or leaving a dangerous location, will receive a 2
  minute penalty."
- "h. A vehicle that leaves a promoted Lunch Stop, Pit Stop, or Rest Stop earlier than 5 minutes
  before its scheduled departure time, or a Display Area (see VIII.C.1, 2) before the specified time,
  will receive penalties of 1 minute for the first offense, 5 minute for the second offense, and
  referral to section VI.B.3 of the Event Regulations (unsportsmanlike conduct) for the third and
  subsequent offenses."
- V.E.4 [GREAT RACE only]: "Teams receiving a DNF (Did Not Finish) under V.E.2.b or V.E.2.d but
  finishing the Stage under their own power will be scored as FNS (Finished No Score). FNS and DNF are
  equivalent for scoring purposes."
- II.H.1.i cell phone use: "warning for the first offense, and penalties of 10 seconds for the second
  offense and 1 minute for further offenses."
- VI.B.3 (unsportsmanlike/detrimental/dangerous conduct, illegal equipment): "a. The addition of up
  to 2 minutes to the Team's rally score for the Stage b. DNF for the Stage c. Disqualification".
- VI.B.2: bad-faith or baseless Problem Resolution Request may be penalized as VI.B.3.
- V.H.7: "Abuse of these procedures [Time Allowance] will be subject to penalty under VI.B.3."

### 13.4 Disqualification and ineligibility (V.F, p.14; VIII)
- V.F.1: automatic disqualification if judged guilty by the Executive Committee, or cited/warned by law
  enforcement, of: "a. Driving while intoxicated or under the influence b. Consuming alcoholic
  beverages or illegal controlled substances while participating c. Dangerous or reckless driving,
  which may include speeding".

## 14. Time Allowance Requests (V.H, pp.14-15) - verbatim

- V.H.1: "Delays on the route: If you are delayed on the instructed route by circumstances beyond
  your control, such as blockage of the route by a train or need to assist at the scene of an
  accident, you may request time allowance using the procedure described in V.H.3. Mechanical failure
  (flat tire or other vehicle failures), lack of vehicle capability (inability to maintain assigned
  speed), and personal failure are not grounds for Time Allowance Requests."
- V.H.2 Emergency Reduced Speeds: "a. If you should encounter conditions described in VII.E.3 which
  make travel at the assigned speeds hazardous, you may elect to use reduced speeds which will allow
  you to travel safely. You may resume the assigned speeds at your discretion. b. Teams competing in
  vehicles with two-wheel brakes may elect to use emergency reduced speeds when they believe that
  conditions, such as mountain grades, make travel at assigned speeds hazardous. Such requests must
  include the phrase "two-wheel brakes". c. Such reduced speeds will result in your leg time being
  greater than the official leg time; you must determine (or estimate) the amount of additional time
  you used because of the reduced speeds and request an allowance using the procedure described in
  V.H.3. If the delay occurs during more than one leg, a separate Time Allowance Request must be
  submitted for each leg. d. This provision is for the purpose of improved safety in case of unusual
  or changed conditions which were not anticipated in the Course Instructions. Each request will be
  considered on its merits. The Time Allowance Committee may reduce or refuse a request if the claimed
  conditions are not verified by another Competition Crew member or Great Race official; if the time
  requested exceeds that which appears reasonable based upon the emergency conditions; or if the
  Committee feels the allowance requested is not justified by the conditions encountered or has not
  been made in good faith (see VI.B.3)." (d. highlighted)
- V.H.3: "Your request must be submitted by cellular telephone to the Great Race Scoring Crew, using
  the procedure and at the locations included in each Stage's Course Instructions; see instructions
  # 18 and # 36 in the Example Rally. It must include the Stage number, your car number, the leg
  number, the Course Instruction number(s) on/between which the delay occurred, and a brief
  description of the circumstances causing the delay. The time allowance requested may be any amount
  of time in multiples of 10 seconds (e.g., 0m10s, 0m20s, 4m50s), not to exceed 29 minutes 30
  seconds." (highlighted) [The Example Rally instructions #18/#36 describe a web page,
  www.grscores.com/timeallowance, with the deadline "Within 15m00s"; V.H.3's wording says cellular
  telephone. The two are not reconciled in the PDF.]
- V.H.4: "The Time Allowance Committee will review your request and, if allowed, the time allowance
  will be subtracted from your time for the leg in which the delay occurred. The Time Allowance
  Committee consists of the Director of Competition and others he may designate. The Committee may
  consult with other staff members, other Competition Crew members, and the party submitting the
  request, as they deem appropriate or necessary."
- V.H.5: "If you are delayed by conditions beyond your control, we expect you to attempt to make up the
  time. In considering whether to allow a Time Allowance Request, the Time Allowance Committee will
  consider the distance from the delay to the Timing Checkpoint and whether some or all of the lost
  time could have been made up. Give information such as: "Delayed 0m45s by a farm tractor. Made up
  0m25s. Request 0m20s." As always, witnesses should be listed when possible, especially for delays
  over 1m00s. If you are attempting to make up time and encounter a Timing Checkpoint, you may lose
  time at 5 MPH (or faster) to bring your delay request to a multiple of 0m10s. Remember, your Time
  Allowance Request may or may not be allowed; it is in your interest to attempt to make up lost time
  safely. The Time Allowance Committee will deny time allowances where in their judgment the team
  could have made up the lost time as specified in V.H.2 d." (last sentence highlighted)
- V.H.6: "A Time Allowance Request submitted with the wrong car number will not be allowed. A Time
  Allowance Request submitted with the wrong leg number will usually not be adjusted to reflect the
  correct leg number. A Time Allowance Request submitted for an amount of time that is not a multiple
  of 0m10s (e.g., 1m17s) will be adjusted up or down to a multiple of 0m10s (1m10s or 1m20s) to the
  possible detriment of the contestant."
- V.H.7: "Abuse of these procedures will be subject to penalty under VI.B.3."
- II.H.1.i: the cellular telephone may be used "for the purpose of submitting Time Allowance Requests".
- Not in regulations: whether traffic lights qualify as a delay "beyond your control" (only a train
  and an accident are named); a ceiling per day; how many requests per leg other than "a separate
  Time Allowance Request must be submitted for each leg".

## 21. Answers to OPEN-QUESTIONS (Q1-Q26)

Legend: ANSWERED / PARTIAL / NOT IN THIS DOCUMENT. Sim default: RIGHT / WRONG / UNCONFIRMED.

### Q1 - Perfect cumulative times printed for every landmark all day, or only calibration run?
ANSWERED. VII.F.1: "During Speedometer Calibration Runs, there will be two official times in a box
in Column C ... interval ... cumulative", to 1/10 s. Elsewhere only assigned speeds and interval
times for transits, pauses, timed speed changes (VII.B.3.c(4)); Column D times are "not the official
time" (VII.F.3); official mileages are not in the instructions (V.B.1.a). Sim default (calibration
run only; "open book" option): RIGHT.

### Q2 - Exact Column C wording/format
ANSWERED. VII.B.3.c(4): time-of-day "7:30:00"; intervals "3h15m00s", "0m45s"; advisory intervals in
parentheses "(35m00s)"; speeds "45 MPH"; calibration boxes "5m32.0s / 7m21.3s"; asterisk "*" = origin
of next interval. Example Rally formats: pause = stacked "0 MPH / 0m15s / 45 MPH" (#15, #34); timed
change = stacked "30 MPH / 0m36s / 45 MPH / 1m12s / 50 MPH" (#14); delayed change "1m12s / 40 MPH"
(#25); transit "20m00s" / "(3h25m00s)". Sim default (own GRIID-like syntax, "35", "P15", "30 for
0:36 then 40"): WRONG for "P15" and "30 for 0:36 then 40" (not official syntax); "35" -> "35 MPH" is
the official form. Switch to the official Column C vocabulary.

### Q3 - Where a landmark speed change takes effect
ANSWERED. VII.E.2.b: "executed when the front tires of the vehicle passes (comes even with) the sign
or landmark". VII.E.2.c: "Speed changes at intersections will occur at a sign (such as a stop sign)
if there is one referenced in Column A of the CAMEO diagram ... Otherwise, speed changes at
intersections will occur at the center of the intersection or the apex of the turn." (Example #27,
#28, #26.) Sim default (near edge of sign; leading edge of intersection; speed on a turn line applies
after the turn): WRONG. Correct: front tires abeam the sign; at a referenced sign if one is in
Column A; else the center of the intersection / apex of the turn.

### Q4 - Green light with a printed Pause: must time still be consumed?
PARTIAL. Glossary: "PAUSE: To delay a specified time. The pause time is added to the time for the
leg." So the leg's perfect time includes the pause regardless of signal state; the regulations do not
discuss a green light. (In the Example Rally Pauses are printed at a Blinker #15 and a Stop Sign #34,
not at the Traffic Light #2.) Sim default (pause always adds time): RIGHT by implication
(unconfirmed in explicit wording).

### Q5 - Time Allowance mechanics for lights/trains
ANSWERED (mechanics) / lights NOT IN THIS DOCUMENT. V.H.1-V.H.7: submitted to the Scoring Crew by
cell phone/web at the locations in the Course Instructions (Example #18/#36 "Within 15m00s"); must
include "Stage number, your car number, the leg number, the Course Instruction number(s)" and a brief
description; "multiples of 10 seconds ... not to exceed 29 minutes 30 seconds"; Committee may reduce
or refuse; denied if time could have been made up; witnesses; non-multiples rounded "up or down to a
multiple of 0m10s ... to the possible detriment of the contestant"; wrong car number not allowed;
trains and accidents are the named examples; mechanical failure/lack of capability/personal failure
are not grounds. Sim default (declare seconds at the next CP; credited = min(declared, actual
delay); over-declaring flagged): WRONG in particulars: 10-second granularity, 29m30s cap, filed at
designated instruction points within the stated window (not "at the next CP"), per-leg, subject to
committee review/verification and a make-up expectation, rounded against the contestant.

### Q6 - Missed checkpoint / max penalty, sight-zone penalty, late start penalty
ANSWERED (first two), late start NOT IN THIS DOCUMENT.
- Per-second: V.E.1.a "1 second"; max late V.E.1.b "2 Minutes"; max early V.E.1.c "5 Minutes".
- Missed Timing CP (not final): V.E.2.a "3 Minutes"; final Timing CP: V.E.2.b "DNF or FNS". Missed
  Observation CP (not final) V.E.2.c "3 Minutes"; final Observation CP V.E.2.d "DNF or FNS".
  More than 30 minutes after computed cumulative perfect time from the latest Start/Restart = missed
  (V.C.2.b(2)).
- Sight zone: V.E.3.a "30 Seconds" (stopping or traveling 5 MPH or slower).
- Late start: not in regulations.
Sim default (300 s cap per CP; sight zone 30 s; late start = seconds late): 300 s cap is WRONG
(missed = 180 s; late cap = 120 s; early cap = 300 s, as printed); sight zone 30 s is RIGHT; late
start UNCONFIRMED.

### Q7 - Full age factor table
ANSWERED. V.D (section 12 above), complete 1900-1954+. Sim default (linear interpolation between
known points): WRONG in method (use the table directly). The table is NOT linear between 1939 and
1926 as the listed anchors would imply: slope is 0.005/yr from 1953 to 1930, then 0.010/yr from 1930
to 1900 (e.g. 1930 = 0.800, whereas linear interpolation 1939->1926 gives about 0.786). The known
anchors themselves (1954+ 1.000, 1953 .915, 1940 .850, 1939 .845, 1926 .760, 1925 .750, 1912 .620,
1911 .610) are all RIGHT. Also key on Scoring Year (IX.B).

### Q8 - Rookie "drop worst leg" rule
ANSWERED. I.F.3.d [GREAT RACE]: Rookies drop "the 6 worst of each Team's Legs on any of the
Qualifying Stages (Stages 1-7)"; Regional I.F.4.d drops 3 worst over Stages 1-2. Discards for all
divisions (Grand 3, Expert 4, Sportsman 5, Rookie 6, X-Cup 5; I.F.3.a-e). Stage awards: no legs
discarded (I.F.5.c). Sim default (off by default; toggle): WRONG as a rule description (it is
mandatory for Championship scoring, pooled across Stages 1-7, by division); fine as a
single-stage-sim default because Stage scores use no discards.

### Q9 - Does the Trophy Run count toward overall?
ANSWERED. No: Championship standings use Stages 1-7 and 8-9 (I.F.3); Trophy Run (Stage 0) is
"optional" (I.A.3) and is used as a tie-break: V.C.2.f(2)(b) "the Team having the higher finishing
position on the Trophy Run (Warm-up Rally/Stage 0) wins the tie" (after "older vehicle by Scoring
Year", (a)). Sim default (tiebreaker only): RIGHT; add that the first tie-break is the older Scoring
Year. Trophy Run has no additional-occupant penalty (II.D.4.c).

### Q14 - Calibration run on the clock (part of leg 1) or followed by a restart line?
ANSWERED. V.B.2.a: Tire Warm-up and Speedometer Calibration Run "are free zones"; V.B.2.c: transit
